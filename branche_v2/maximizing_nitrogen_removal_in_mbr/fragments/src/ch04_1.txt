Fig. 3 presents the ranges of key operational features and their 
Pearson correlation matrix, offering insight into the interactions among 
process variables and their influence on treatment performance. The 
selected parameters vary greatly in scale, including DO-Ax (0.1–0.5 mg/ 
L), ORP-Ax (–400–400 mV), Tem-An (10–24 ◦C), DO-R (0.05–0.48 mg/ 
L), Sludge-R (19–905 m³/d), and MLVSS-Ae (2000–8000 mg/L). This 
variability necessitates data normalization or standardization to prevent 
parameters with larger magnitudes from disproportionately influencing 
the model. The Pearson correlation coefficient (r) ranges from –1 to + 1 
values which indicate the direction and strength of the linear relation­
ship between variables. Positive correlations signify direct associations, 
while negative values reflect inverse trends. Despite their numerically 
weak values, several correlations hold high process-level significance. 
This reflects the complex interdependencies inherent in the multistage 
MBR configuration, which includes a swing basin and a DO-R tank.
Nitrification and denitrification performance are closely governed by 
redox regulation across the system, as indicated by the positive corre­
lation between TN and oxidation–reduction potential in the anoxic tank 
(ORP-Ax, r = 0.31). This moderate correlation suggests that maintain­
ing stable redox conditions is critical for effective nitrogen trans­
formation, rather than implying direct linear control. The swing basin 
supports this regulation by flexibly switching between aerobic and 
anoxic modes, buffering redox fluctuations. Extending the aerobic 
duration at low temperatures enhances nitrification efficiency by 
providing sufficient time for ammonia-oxidizing activity [53]. In 
contrast, dissolved oxygen reduction (DO-R, r = −0.04) and sludge re­
turn flow (Sludge-R, r = −0.04) exhibit weak but negative correlations 
with TN, reflecting their indirect roles in limiting oxygen intrusion and 
regulating biomass distribution within the anoxic zone.
A moderate positive correlation between temperature in the anoxic 
zone (Tem-Ax, r = 0.30) and COD removal suggests that temperature- 
dependent microbial activity enhances organic matter degradation, 
with the swing basin indirectly supporting this effect by stabilizing up­
stream nitrification and redox conditions through flexible aero­
bic–anoxic operation. In contrast, the weak positive correlation between 
sludge return from the DO-R basin (Sludge-R, r = 0.07) and COD 
removal indicates limited linear dependence. At the process level, the 
DO-R basin conditions the return sludge under low-DO conditions to 
enhance its heterotrophic activity. This preconditioned sludge subse­
quently improves COD removal efficiency in downstream treatment by 
utilizing organic matter more effectively while minimizing oxygen 
interference in anoxic zones [54].
The weak negative correlation between dissolved oxygen in the DO- 
R basin (DO-R, r = −0.16) and TP removal shows a limited linear 
dependence. A moderate negative correlation between MLSS-Ax 
(r = −0.37) and TP removal indicates that elevated biomass concen­
trations in the anoxic zone may hinder phosphorus removal efficiency. 
Residual oxygen in this zone can disrupt the anaerobic–anoxic condi­
tions required for effective biological phosphorus removal. The presence 
of oxygen suppresses the metabolism of polyphosphate-accumulating 
organisms, impairing the phosphorus release–uptake cycle essential 
for efficient removal [55]. The swing basin partially offsets this limita­
tion by flexibly regulating aerobic–anoxic conditions, stabilizing 
polyphosphate-accumulating organism activity and preventing exces­
sive biomass accumulation in the anoxic zone.
