Phosphorus removal (Fig. 9d–f) exhibits secondary sensitivity to 
operational parameters. Limited yellow regions appear under high 
Sludge-R and specific ORP-Ax or pH-Ae conditions, indicating that 
phosphorus removal benefits modestly from increased sludge recycling 
and controlled oxygen levels. Elevated aerobic pH promotes chemical 
precipitation with divalent cations, while higher sludge recirculation 
improves biomass-mediated uptake and transport of phosphorus to 
aerobic or settling zones. The swing basin indirectly supports TP

For COD (Fig. 9g–i), the plots reveal that low anaerobic temperature 
(Tem-An) and moderate mixed liquor suspended solids (MLSS-An) favor 
higher COD removal, reflecting enhanced microbial activity under sta­

microorganisms are present to effectively degrade organic matter, 
improving the biological oxidation of COD [62,63]. Excessively high 
MLSS may reduce treatment efficiency due to increased inorganic sus­

activity and decrease the proportion of active biomass in the system.

promotes hydrolysis and fermentation of organic matter, supporting 
carbon availability for downstream denitrification. The Sludge-R versus

reducing organic matter degradation. The swing basin’s intermittent 
aerobic–anoxic operation further stabilizes oxygen distribution, pre­
