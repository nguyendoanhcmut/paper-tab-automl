Municipal wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs) integrate a 
sequence of physical, chemical, and biological processes to progressively 
remove pollutants from influent streams [1–4]. Among these, biological 
treatment is essential for the elimination of organic matter, nitrogen, 
and phosphorus via microbial transformations [5–7]. Over the past two 
decades, membrane bioreactor (MBR) technology has become an 
attractive alternative to conventional activated sludge systems due to its 
high treatment efficiency, small footprint, and ability to maintain high 
biomass concentrations [8–11]. Typically, MBRs operate through a 
combination of anaerobic, anoxic, and aerobic environments that sup­
port distinct microbial processes for nutrient and organic pollutant 
removal [12,13].
However, conventional MBR configurations frequently experience 
insufficient total nitrogen (TN) removal due to two interrelated

Numerous studies have attempted to overcome these shortcomings 
by modifying MBR configurations or operating conditions. For example, 
controlling free ammonia concentration was used to enrich nitrifica­
tion–denitrification (ND) microbial communities, achieving TN removal 
up to 97.12% with 33% lower carbon consumption [19]. An intermit­
tently aerated moving bed MBR facilitated partial nitrification and 
denitrification under low chemical oxygen demand (COD)/N conditions, 
achieving 86.14% TN removal [20]. Other studies integrated elemental 
sulfur-based autotrophic denitrification with coagulant dosing,

While previous studies have advanced nitrogen removal in MBR 
systems, most have focused on optimizing isolated parameters, such as 
aeration mode, coagulant dosing, or carrier type, rather than on the 
synergistic integration of complementary strategies. This fragmented 
approach leaves critical challenges unaddressed. Specifically, low tem­
peratures inhibit nitrification, causing ammonia breakthrough and un­
stable TN removal [19,24]. Simultaneously, oxygen imbalance between 
aerobic and anoxic zones suppresses denitrification by exposing de­
nitrifiers to excessive DO [25]. These dual limitations are further com­
pounded by the frequent neglect of nitrogen-phosphorus interactions, 
particularly under carbon-limited conditions typical of industrial 
wastewater, where TN removal becomes even more challenging.
To address these challenges, the present study proposes a novel MBR 
configuration specifically designed to enhance nitrogen removal while 
maintaining high COD and total phosphorus (TP) removal. Given the 
influent characteristics of the studied industrial wastewater, nitrogen 
removal was prioritized in the design. The modified system incorporates 
two key engineering innovations, including swing basin and DO 
reduction (DO-R) tank: (i) The swing basin operates between anoxic to 
aerobic modes, allowing flexible adjustment of aerobic and anoxic vol­
umes to maintain effective nitrification under varying influent and 
temperature conditions. (ii) The DO-R tank temporarily halts aeration 
downstream of the aerobic zone, allowing microorganisms to lower DO 
and maintain the redox balance for efficient denitrification. This 
research presents a systematic approach for the evaluation and optimi­
zation of MBR performance, based on the integration of machine 
learning (ML) models with bio-inspired optimization algorithms. ML 
was utilized to model the complex and nonlinear relationships between 
operational conditions and treatment performance in the MBR system, 
which are difficult to describe using conventional mechanistic models. 
Random forest (RF) and deep neural network (DNN) models were 
developed to predict the simultaneous removal of TN, COD, and TP. 
Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis was applied to enhance 
model interpretability and to quantify the contribution of each proposed 
innovation. Three bio-inspired algorithms were compared with the dif­
ferential evolution (DE) method to identify optimal operating conditions 
for maximizing nitrogen removal. While DE is a reliable and widely used 
optimization method, it typically requires more iterations and longer 
computation times. In contrast, bio-inspired algorithms offer faster 
calculation speeds, but their accuracy must be validated against a 
benchmark method. Therefore, this study compared the convergence 
results of each bio-inspired algorithm with those obtained from DE to 
identify which algorithm most closely approximates DE's optimal solu­
tion. The algorithm demonstrating the closest agreement with DE, while 
maintaining high computational efficiency, was selected as the most 
suitable optimization approach for practical MBR operation.
This work provides a comprehensive process and data-driven 
framework that combines engineering modifications, including swing 
basin and DO-R tank, with ML-based optimization. Unlike previous 
studies focusing on isolated operational adjustments, this study in­
troduces a synergistic process configuration validated at full scale and 
interprets its performance using explainable ML, enabling both opera­
tional optimization and mechanistic understanding. The proposed 
configuration enhances biological stability, ensures efficient pollutant 
removal under varying influent conditions, and contributes novel in­
sights into integrated process design for advanced MBR systems.
