During optimization, the process variables were adjusted to reduce 
the effluent concentrations of COD, TN, and TP, thereby maximizing the 
system's overall treatment efficiency. Based on the most accurate ML 
algorithms, the DE method, as a reliable benchmark, was compared with 
three recently developed bio-inspired optimization algorithms, 
including the Slime Mould Algorithm (SMA), the Mayfly Optimization 
Algorithm (MOA), and the Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO). These 
algorithms are inspired by distinct biological processes and incorporate 
different search mechanisms, allowing a comprehensive evaluation of 
their effectiveness in minimizing multiple targets in complex, nonlinear 
systems. Fig. 2 illustrates the concepts of the optimization algorithms 
used in this study, represented through their underlying natural 
behaviors.
DE is an evolutionary optimization technique that employs mutation, 
crossover, and selection operators to evolve a population of candidate 
solutions iteratively. It generates diversity by perturbing individuals 
with scaled differences of randomly chosen solutions, ensuring robust 
exploration of the search space [49]. DE is well recognized for its 
simplicity, low computational cost, and stable convergence behavior, 
making it one of the most widely adopted classical optimizers. Although 
its convergence can be slower compared with newer metaheuristics, 
DE’s key strength lies in its robustness and reliability across a broad 
range of continuous optimization problems, establishing it as a 
dependable baseline for comparison.
SMA is inspired by the oscillatory foraging behavior of slime mould, 
where adaptive movements are governed by positive and negative 
feedback mechanisms [50]. It utilizes bio-oscillation equations to update 
the positions of search agents, thereby dynamically balancing explora­
tion and exploitation. By adjusting the search strategy according to the 
fitness of candidate solutions, SMA demonstrates strong capability in 
avoiding local minima. It is characterized by global search efficiency, 
rapid convergence, and competitive optimization accuracy. Owing to its 
novelty and flexibility, SMA is particularly suitable for tackling 
high-dimensional and complex engineering or environmental optimi­
zation problems.
MOA mimics the swarming and mating behavior of mayflies, inte­
grating attraction, mating, and flight dynamics into its search strategy 
[51]. The algorithm divides the population into male and female may­
flies, with males performing attractive flights toward superior solutions 
and females attracted to males for mating. Offspring are generated 
through recombination processes, maintaining population diversity and 
preventing premature convergence. MOA achieves a balanced trade-off 
between exploration and exploitation, resulting in stable convergence 
performance. Its principal advantages include flexibility, high accuracy, 
and applicability to multi-objective and nonlinear optimization 
problems.
HHO is inspired by the cooperative hunting strategies of Harris 
Hawks, particularly their surprise pounce and dynamic prey-escaping 
mechanisms [52]. The algorithm models different hunting phases, 
including exploration, transition, and exploitation, based on the 
escaping energy of the prey, thus adaptively shifting between global and 
local search modes. This dynamic adaptation enables HHO to effectively 
avoid local optima and accelerate convergence toward the global opti­
mum. Key features include adaptive search capability, efficient 
convergence, and robustness in navigating complex multimodal land­
scapes. HHO has shown particular strength in solving challenging 
optimization problems where maintaining exploration–exploitation 
balance is critical.
