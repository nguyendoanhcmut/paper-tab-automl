This study demonstrates that integrating a swing basin with a DO-R 
tank provides an effective strategy for improving TN removal in full- 
scale MBR systems while maintaining stable COD and TP removal. The 
swing basin improved nitrification by extending aerobic conditions, 
while the DO-R tank enhanced denitrification by reducing residual DO 
in recycled sludge. The ML analysis of long-term operational data 
showed that the RF model outperformed the DNN algorithm in pre­
dicting COD, TN, and TP concentrations. The HHO optimization algo­
rithm produced results closest to those of DE among the evaluated 
methods. This performance was achieved at a Sludge-R value of 
146.23 m³ /d and a DO-R value of 0.20 mg/L. Beyond the specific case 
study, this work reveals how integrating process engineering in­
novations with explainable ML and bio-inspired optimization can pro­
vide a general framework for understanding, predicting, and improving 
the performance of complex biological wastewater treatment systems. 
One limitation of this study is that the results are based on operational 
data from a single full-scale wastewater treatment plant, which may 
limit the generalizability of the ML models and optimization outcomes 
to systems with different influent characteristics or operational condi­
tions. Future research should evaluate the proposed framework across 
multiple full-scale plants to confirm its applicability under different 
operational and influent conditions. Nevertheless, the proposed frame­
work provides practical guidance for improving nutrient removal and 
offers a scalable strategy for optimizing MBR operation under variable 
environmental conditions.
