The swing basin configuration enhances nitrification by dynamically 
extending the aerobic zone in winter, thereby compensating for 
temperature-induced inhibition of nitrifying bacteria. At low tempera­
tures, the metabolic activity and growth rate of ammonia-oxidizing and 
nitrite-oxidizing bacteria decline, leading to reduced nitrification effi­
ciency. The swing basin mitigates this limitation by extending the aer­
obic zone, which increases oxygen availability and retention time, 
allowing nitrifiers to sustain ammonia oxidation under cold conditions. 
This prolonged aerobic exposure stabilizes nitrifier populations and 
maintains the sequential conversion of ammonium to nitrate, preserving 
overall nitrogen transformation efficiency. Consequently, the system 
sustains efficient sequential nitrification–denitrification processes, 
leading to improved total nitrogen removal across seasonal variations.
In this study, the DO-R tank plays a critical role in enhancing deni­
trification by creating favorable anoxic conditions (Fig. 10b). In the 
absence of an external carbon source, microorganisms initially consume 
the residual dissolved oxygen through endogenous respiration, leading 
to a progressive decrease in DO concentration. This oxygen depletion 
suppresses aerobic metabolic pathways and promotes the establishment 
of an anoxic environment. Since denitrification is an anoxic process that 
requires the absence of free oxygen, the reduced DO level allows 
denitrifying microorganisms to utilize nitrate (NO₃⁻) as an alternative 
electron acceptor. Consequently, higher nitrate availability combined 
with lower oxygen concentration accelerates denitrification kinetics, 
resulting in improved total nitrogen removal efficiency.
