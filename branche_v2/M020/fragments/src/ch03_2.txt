The LOCF imputation method is a strategy for handling missing data in 
longitudinal or time-series studies. In this method, when a data point for 
a certain period is missing, the most recent observation is used to fill in 
the gap (Woolley et al., 2009). The distribution of the datasets is pre­
sented in Table 2. The dataset was randomly split into a 9:1 training set 
and a test set (Zhu et al., 2023). While the original data had temporal 
characteristics, the objective of this study was to compare the perfor­
mance of MLP and LSTM in a non-sequential context. By shuffling the 
data, the split transitioned from a time series dataset to a general 
dataset. This process not only improved model generalization but also 
addressed potential data leakage concerns, as temporal order was no 
longer a factor following the shuffle. 
Normalization or standardization of input features is beneficial for 
ML models, specifically neural networks. Without standardized inputs, 
optimization algorithms such as gradient descent may have difficulty 
achieving convergence due to the oscillatory behavior of gradients, 
particularly when the scales of the features vary. While it is true that in 
certain algorithms, a significant difference in numerical range can cause 
numbers in higher ranges to influence the model disproportionately 
(Ozsahin et al., 2022), the primary objective of standardization in our 
neural network models is to promote efficient and stable convergence 
during training. 
Additionally, the significance of consistent feature scaling increases 
when regularization techniques are employed. This ensures that the 
weight of no feature is unfairly penalized based on its original scale. As 
Eq. (1) demonstrates, a standardization function was utilized to address 
this issue and facilitate the comparison and analysis of features. After 
standardization, the characteristics have a mean close to 0 and a stan­
dard deviation close to 1. This transformation optimizes the perfor­
mance of machine learning models while preserving the original 
distribution shape of the features.
