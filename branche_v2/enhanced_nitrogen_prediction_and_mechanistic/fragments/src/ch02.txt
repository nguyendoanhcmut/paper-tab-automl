With the rapid advancement of urbanization and industrialization, 
water scarcity and pollution issues have intensified (Bao and Fang, 
2012). Chemical manufacturing, food processing, pharmaceuticals, and 
seawater desalination commonly produce high-salinity wastewater (Guo 
et al., 2023). Elevated salt concentrations pose severe environmental 
threats, including soil permeability degradation and aquatic life toxicity 
(Linari´c et al., 2013). Membrane bioreactors (MBRs) combine mem­
brane separation with biological treatment, making them an ideal 
technology for treating high-salinity wastewater (Neoh et al., 2016).
In MBR operations, wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs) face sig­
nificant salinity-driven challenges. High NaCl (>1%) suppresses 
ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (AOB) activity, necessitating costly chem­
ical supplementation to maintain nitrogen compliance (Zhang and Liu, 
2023). Salinity-stressed microbes prioritize osmoprotectant synthesis 
over denitrification, but existing models fail to quantify these carbon 
flux trade-offs (Jeon et al., 2022). Additionally, traditional sensors 
cannot detect salinity-induced microbial shifts in real time, leading to 
delayed aeration adjustments and excessive energy consumption (Waki 
et al., 2018). These issues inflate operational costs by 18–25 % in 
WWTPs, highlighting the need for interpretable models linking pre­
dictions to actionable process controls (Borzooei et al., 2019).
Traditional MBR approaches like mechanistic models, empirical re­
gressions, and hybrid frameworks have succeeded in low-salinity 
wastewater scenarios. For example, Park et al. (2015) developed a 
transmembrane pressure-driven flux model predicting fouling rates, 
while Waki et al. (2018) optimized aeration cycles using activated 
sludge models (ASM), reducing energy consumption by 18 %. However, 
these models struggle with high-salinity wastewater. Mechanistic 
models suffer from kinetic parameter drift when applied to saline con­
ditions (Huang et al., 2020), and hybrid models like ASM coupled with 
computational fluid dynamics (ASM-CFD) coupling overpredict nitrifi­
cation rates at elevated salinity (Xu et al., 2024). Empirical models fail to 
generalize across varying salinity gradients, as osmotic stress disrupts 
carbon-to-nitrogen (C/N) metabolic pathways (Zhang et al., 2015).
Recent advances in machine learning (ML) have enhanced predictive 
capabilities in wastewater treatment. Neural networks, such as artificial 
neural networks (ANN), have been used to predict total nitrogen (TN) 
removal (Jeon et al., 2022), while long short-term memory (LSTM) 
models handle dynamic water quality data (Yaqub et al., 2020). Tree- 
based models like XGBoost optimize chemical oxygen demand (COD) 
removal (Yaqub and Lee, 2022), and random forests (RF) identify heavy 
metal toxicity thresholds (Gaur et al., 2024). Hybrid models, such as 
ASM-ANN coupling, demonstrate the potential of machine learning in 
wastewater treatment (Xu et al., 2024).
Research on high-salinity and heavy metal wastewater has pre­
dominantly focused on membrane fouling, with limited attention on 
microbial metabolic processes (Borzooei et al., 2019). Studies on heavy 
metal wastewater have emphasized feature selection (e.g., pH, ion 
strength) yet often overlook process control integration (Ar´evalo et al., 
2013). Notably, no study has employed SHAP’s force plots or partial 
dependence plots (PDPs) to explore the salinity-induced trade-offs be­
tween nitrification enzyme activities and carbon allocation in MBRs. 
This gap limits the practical application of ML models in hypersaline 
environments, where threshold-driven control strategies are crucial for 
optimal performance.
This study bridges these gaps by developing an interpretable ML 
framework that quantifies and prioritizes salinity-impacted control 
variables in MBRs. The objectives are: 1) to establish an interpretable 
machine learning framework that decodes salinity-driven nitrogen 
removal bottlenecks; 2) to quantify multiscale interactions between 
operational parameters and microbial nitrification–denitrification 
functions under high-salt stress; and 3) to develop decision-centric vi­
sual tools that translate model predictions into actionable control stra­
tegies for WWTPs. This approach advances beyond conventional ML 
applications by embedding biological mechanism transparency, offering 
a paradigm shift toward intelligent, cost-effective MBR optimization.
