This study used interpretable machine learning to predict nitrogen 
removal in MBR systems treating high-salinity wastewater. CatBoost 
model showed significant improvements in R2 (9 %) and RMSE (14 %) 
for NH4
+-Nout, and similar gains for TNout. SHAP analysis highlighted 
salinity as a key factor, disrupting nitrification and denitrification, while 
CODeff, DO and C/N ratio influenced microbial activity. Prior studies 
have consistently demonstrated the inhibitory effect of high salinity on 
ammonia oxidation by ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (AOB) (Shi et al., 
2021; Xu et al., 2024). The SHAP analysis corroborates this but adds 
depth by revealing how salinity interacts with other key parameters, 
such as COD removal efficiency. Specifically, we found that high COD 
removal exacerbates carbon competition and starving nitrifiers of 
essential substrates, a nuanced finding not emphasized in prior work. 
Research on denitrification under saline conditions has shown that 
salinity impairs Nar activity (Li et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2015). Salinity 
inhibits Nar and alters the microbial community structure, further 
impairing carbon transfer to anoxic zones. Previous studies have 
explored this interplay less, often focusing on single stressors.
The temperature-related findings also align with previous research 
but provide more operationally relevant insights. Luo et al. (2019) 
examined temperature effects on nitrification in non-saline systems but 
did not account for the complex interactions with salinity. The SHAP 
waterfall plots indicate that below 20 ◦C, Nar activity decays signifi­
cantly due to Arrhenius-type behaviour, but this effect diminishes above 
25 ◦C due to thermal inertia. This insight provides more specific guid­
ance for operational strategies in saline wastewater treatment. 
Furthermore, while studies on aeration optimization have focused on 
improving DO for nitrogen removal (Zhang et al., 2019), this research 
highlights specific DO thresholds (0.5–1.2 mg/L) for optimal perfor­
mance under salinity stress. This result contrasts with prior work that 
provided general aeration guidelines, offering a more precise opera­
tional range.
This study’s model has some limitations for direct application in real- 
world environments. Extensive calibration is required when applying 
the model to wastewater with different compositions and for potential 
variations in plant-specific operational conditions (e.g., fluctuations in 
salinity levels). Continuous monitoring of environmental parameters is 
also necessary to ensure reliable model predictions. Additionally, the 
study focuses solely on high-salinity wastewater, and further adapta­
tions may be needed for other types of influent. Future research could 
explore the combined effects of salinity with other stressors like heavy 
metals (Ar´evalo et al., 2013) or organic pollutants (Holloway et al., 
2014), as these could compound the challenges of nitrogen removal. 
Factors such as adsorbents and membrane fouling could be incorporated 
into future models to refine operational strategies (Xu et al., 2024). 
Further validation under real-world conditions and incorporation of 
additional variables will strengthen model applicability and robustness.
