The SHAP summary plot elucidates the relative contributions of 
input variables to the predictions of NH4
+-Nout and TNout, revealing 
distinct mechanistic drivers for each output parameter (Fig. 3). The se­
lection of the top six predictors for NH4
+-Nout and TNout was based on the 
synergy between SHAP rankings and mechanistic interpretability, 
prioritizing parameters with both high model influence and actionable 
process relevance.
For NH4
+-Nout predictions, salinity is identified as the most influential 
factor, with a SHAP value nearly twice that of the second-ranked vari­
able, NO2
−-Nout, indicating its substantial impact. The other key pre­
dictors include NO2
−-Nout, CODeff, pH, NH4
+-Nin, and DO. While DO, 
CODin, and C/N ratio have similar SHAP values, DO was prioritized due 
to its direct role in regulating nitrification and denitrification, making it 
more practical for process control (Fig. 3a and Fig. 3b). Salinity exerts 
primary control over ammonium accumulation by directly suppressing 
nitrifying bacteria activity. Elevated salt concentrations disrupt trans­
membrane proton gradients in AOB, inhibiting ammonia mono­
oxygenase (AMO) and hydroxylamine oxidoreductase (HAO) enzymes 
critical for NH4
+ oxidation (Yan et al., 2018). NO2
−-Nout accumulation 
exacerbates nitrification stagnation through feedback inhibition, as ni­
trite binds to AMO active sites competitively. CODeff further modulates 
NH4
+-Nout by reflecting carbon source competition: high COD removal 
efficiency implies heterotrophic dominance, depriving nitrifiers of 
essential carbon substrates for energy metabolism (Bai et al., 2023). pH 
fluctuations (optimal range: 7.5–8.5) destabilize proton pump equilib­

For TNout, TNeff has the highest SHAP contribution, as expected due 
to its strong correlation with TNout, followed by salinity, which has a 
SHAP value about two-thirds of TNeff. The remaining four dominant 
predictors include temperature, CODeff, DO, and the C/N ratio. Although 
CODin and NO2
−-Nout have SHAP values close to the C/N ratio, the se­
lection of these six predictors was not solely based on SHAP rankings but 
also on their practical implications in MBR operation. Specifically, DO 
and the C/N ratio were prioritized over CODin and NO2
−-Nout due to their 
critical roles in regulating microbial activity and nitrogen trans­
formation pathways (Fig. 3c and Fig. 3d). TNeff emerges as the strongest 
predictor, directly quantifying systemic nitrogen removal performance. 
Salinity exerts dual inhibition: it disrupts both nitrification (limiting 
NH4
+→NO3
−
conversion) and denitrification by suppressing nitrate 
reductase (Nar) and nitrite reductase (Nir) activities (Qiang et al., 2023). 
Salt-stressed microbes prioritize compatible solute synthesis (e.g., 
glycine betaine) over metabolic functions, diverting carbon resources 
from denitrification electron donors. Temperature sensitivity in TNout 
predictions aligns with the thermophilic nature of denitrifiers (optimal 
range: 20–40 ◦C). Low temperatures (<15 ◦C) inactivate Nar enzymes, 
stalling NO3
−
reduction. CODeff and C/N ratio jointly regulate carbon 
availability—high COD removal efficiency or low C/N ratios (<5) starve 
denitrifiers of organic electron donors, leaving residual NO3

−un­

elevated DO (>0.5 mg/L) in anoxic zones inhibits Nar activity, with 
salinity-induced aeration heterogeneity exacerbating oxygen intrusion.

+-Nin show limited relevance. CODout 
primarily reflects terminal COD degradation, offering no incremental 
insight beyond CODeff. Denitrifiers tolerate broader pH ranges (6.5–8.5), 
and salinity’s ionic effects may buffer pH fluctuations. NH4
