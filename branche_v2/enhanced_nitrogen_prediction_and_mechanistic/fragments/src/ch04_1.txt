3.1.1. Hyperparameter optimization

Bayesian optimization with Gaussian processes was applied to tune 
hyperparameters across six models, utilizing a search space defined by 
prior experimental knowledge and pilot trials. For each algorithm, key 
parameters were bounded within physiochemical and computational 
constraints: learning rates (0.01–0.30) to balance convergence speed 
and stability, tree depths (3–20) to prevent overfitting, and regulariza­
tion terms (L2: 1–10) to control model complexity. The optimization 
process iteratively evaluated different parameter combinations per 
model via a lower confidence bound (LCB) acquisition function, maxi­
mizing the expected improvement in 10-fold cross-validation RMSE. 
CatBoost’s depth (4–10) and L2 regularization (1–10) were optimized to 
mitigate overfitting under high-salinity noise, converging at depth = 8 
and L2 = 3. LightGBM’s leaf count (20–50) and RF’s tree depth (5–20) 
addressed feature interaction capture in nonlinear salinity-response 
systems. XGBoost’s subsampling (0.50–1.00) enhanced robustness 
against ionic strength-induced data sparsity (see supplementary mate­
rials). The algorithm-specific ranges ensured physiochemical relevance 
with known microbial metabolic hierarchies. Optimal configurations 
reduced validation RMSE by 12–18 % compared to default settings, 
confirming Bayesian optimization’s efficacy in hypersaline wastewater 
prediction tasks.
