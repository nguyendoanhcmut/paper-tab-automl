This study aims to simulate real-world conditions in MBRs treating 
high-salinity wastewater, focusing on salinity-induced metabolic stress 
and its impact on nitrogen removal efficiency. The experimental con­
ditions were selected to reflect typical operational challenges in high- 
salt environments. The synthetic wastewater was prepared using 
glucose (C6H12O6), potassium dihydrogen phosphate (KH2PO4), 
ammonium chloride (NH4Cl), sodium chloride (NaCl), and trace ele­
ments based on previous studies (Khan et al., 2011; Zhong et al., 2022). 
Five batches of reactors, each with an adequate volume of 10 L, were 
cultivated at salinity levels ranging from 0 to 5 %. Regulate the tem­
perature between 5 and 35 ◦C using a water bath heating system, 
maintaining suspended solids (SS) concentrations between 3 and 8 g/L 
and pH values ranging from 7.0 to 8.5. The peristaltic pump achieves 
continuous water inlet and outlet, the hydraulic retention time (HRT) is 
between 19 and 30 h, and the dissolved oxygen (DO) level is between 0.1 
and 3.5 mg/L. The influent total nitrogen (TNin) consisted of ammonia 
nitrogen (NH4
+-Nin), ranging from 20 to 90 mg/L. While the initial car­
bon source concentration of chemical oxygen demand (COD) equivalent 
varied between 180 and 300 mg/L. Hollow fibre membrane components 
were employed, and membrane fouling was monitored using trans­
membrane pressure gauges. The treatment procedure in each reactor 
module, whereby wastewater was introduced into the MBR using a 
peristaltic pump, agitated by a stirring and aeration system. Organic 
matter was biodegraded by denitrification and activated sludge process.
