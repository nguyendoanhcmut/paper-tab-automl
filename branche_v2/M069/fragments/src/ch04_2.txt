generated to illustrate the influence of key input parameters: SRT, PARmax, and OD680, on both NH4-Neff and the probability of meeting 
water reuse standards (preuse). An increase in SRT from 1.88 to 3.8 days generally showed a decrease in NH4-Neff (Fig. 3b and d), 
resulting in higher probability to produce reclaimed water that accomplishes the Italian regulation (Fig. 3a and c). This is aligned with 
the previous results reported, as longer SRT implies longer time for microalgae to spend in the MPBR system, allowing higher growth 
(and subsequently, higher OD680) under the SRT ranges evaluates. However, the relationship between SRT and HRT with ammonium 
removal is only linear in certain ranges. Out of those optimal ranges, microalgae performance decreases. Previous experiments showed 
that lengthening SRT from 6 to 9 days in an outdoor MPBR system promoted the proliferation of competing organisms and grazers, 
decreasing microalgae activity [46], whereas too short SRTs (lower than 1.6 days) tended to show higher NH4 −Neff concentration, 
suggesting those SRT values being insufficient for optimal microalgae growth [35,45], thus decreasing the probability of reusing water 
(Fig. 3d). As a consequence, the empirical relationship OD680 = f (SRT) was valid only for the SRT range of 1.6-6 d (Fig. S3).
With respect to HRT, too long values could limit the amount of nutrients to be supplied to the culture, unbalancing the equilibrium 
of the microbiota, whereas HRT values shorter than 1 d have shown to promote competing organisms such as heterotrophic and ni­
trifying bacteria [47]. In open microalgae cultivation system, Aparicio et al. [48] reported the highest performance of an outdoor 
membrane high-rate algal pond (MHRAP) at 2-d HRT in comparison to shorter (1-d HRT) and longer values (4- or 6-d HRT). In fact, the 
2-d HRT was the only period that the MHRAP system was able to remove nitrogen and phosphorus efficiently from municipal 
wastewater. In the other periods, the culture microbiome did not reach an equilibrium between microalgae and bacteria, thus reducing 
the performance of the system. Hence, knowing the appropriate SRT/HRT ranges of operation is essential for implementing this kind of 
systems.
From the predicted results, it was also observed that the lower the PARmax, the greater the influence of SRT on the probability of 
producing reclaimed water (Fig. 3a). This can be related to the fact that at low solar irradiances, the microalgae activity was probably 
photo-limited. This means that the amount of photos available was not enough to carry out the photosynthetic process efficiently, so 
that NH4 removal was below the optimal. Under these simulated conditions, the system would be expected to lengthen the SRT with 
the goal of providing more time (and subsequently more photons) to microalgae. As a consequence, PARmax below 1600 μmol m−2 s−1 
simulated NH4-Neff concentrations above the Preuse limit (Fig. 3b). On the contrary, at higher PARmax levels, photosynthetic activity 
may have approached a saturation threshold, where further increases in light intensity would not enhance ammonium uptake. 
Although excessive irradiance can lead to photoinhibition, such effects were not observed in this system, probably due to light 
attenuation from biomass shading. A similar trend was observed with OD680, but even with a greater influence than PARmax on the 
probability of reusing water (Fig. 3a and c).
As OD680 is an indirect measurement of the microalgal biomass [40], higher OD680 values are related to higher biomass pro­
duction, and subsequently, with higher NH4 consumption. From an operational perspective, the proposed approach offers several 
practical advantages for potential large-scale systems: i) it enables the estimation of NH4-Neff ranges without relying on costly NH4 
sensors, which require frequent maintenance and calibration [19]; ii) it supports the implementation of strategies for process opti­
misation (further details in Section 3.4); iii) the models could be integrated with weather forecasting tools to predict the treatment 
capacity of the system according to ambient conditions; iv) the curves determined by alternative logistic regression models do not 
require complex implementation unlike other MCM- or ML-based methodologies (Table S1). However, the accuracy of the results is 
limited as they are based on the probability of obtaining NH4-Neff within a certain range of values. The numerical NH4-Neff values used 
in this study are just approximations to the highest values of these ranges (as described in Section 2.2). As NH4-Neff concentration is the 
main limiting factor for reusing water, this approach could be enough for estimating the probability of not accomplishing reuse re­
quirements), which is highly relevant from a regulatory perspective.
