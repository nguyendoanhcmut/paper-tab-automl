ranged from 83.45% to 88.89% across thresholds, indicating that most experimentally compliant or low-ammonium episodes were

Model robustness was additionally assessed by propagating measurement uncertainty in DO, PAR, and temperature, both sepa­
rately and simultaneously. Input uncertainty was represented by normal distributions with standard deviations equal to 5–25% of the 
nominal mean values, while the remaining predictors were fixed at their averages. The mean predicted probability of compliance with 
NH4–N < 1.6 mg N L−1 remained stable at 0.47–0.51, but prediction dispersion increased with measurement uncertainty. DO had the 
greatest influence, with the coefficient of variation increasing from 0.39 to 0.85, compared with 0.12–0.46 for temperature and 
0.08–0.35 for PAR. The combined T–DO–PAR scenario produced coefficients of variation of 0.41–0.83, confirming DO as the dominant 
contributor to prediction uncertainty (Table S14). These results highlight the importance of DO sensor calibration and data-quality 
control. Predictions based on inputs outside the model-development ranges should be treated as extrapolations and flagged for 
advisory use only until additional validation or recalibration is available.
