2.1. Measurement data

2.1.1. Experimental pilot plant
Experimental data were obtained from the 1.5-year continuous operation of a 470-L outdoor MPBR system comprising two flat- 
panel PBRs (1.25 × 2 × 0.1 m). These connected to a two-step harvesting system featuring 14-L tanks with 3.44 m2 hollow-fibre 
membranes (PURON® KMS PUR-PSH31, 0.03 μm). The PBRs were monitored using regularly calibrated on-line sensors for pH, 
temperature, dissolved oxygen, and vertically measured photosynthetically active radiation (PAR). A SCADA-controlled PLC managed 
data acquisition and regulated operational variables, including influent/effluent flows, HRT, SRT, and aeration [38]. This SCADA 
system also controlled the influent and effluent flows of the MPBR, allowing calculating the HRT and SRT of the system, as well as other 
operational variables such as Fair, simulating typical operations at large scale.
The MPBR system treated the effluent from a pilot-scale AnMBR treating municipal wastewater from Carraixet WWTP 
(39◦30′04.0″N 0◦20′00.1″W, Valencia, Spain) and operated continuously outdoors. While the AnMBR targeted organic matter and 
pathogens, the MPBR facilitated nutrient removal for water reclamation. The biomass obtained in both steps was treated in an 
anaerobic co-digestion (AcoD) process to maximize biogas production (Fig. S1). This combined system was evaluated in Seco et al. 
[39]. However, due to the relevance of the microalgal process in water reuse potential, detailed analysis of this second step was carried 
out in this study. The influent flow to the MPBR system was analysed by grab samples with the same frequency than the rest of MPBR 
parameters. This allowed evaluating the second stage of the process independently from the first step, so that biological performance of 
the AnMBR was not considered in this study, as the experimental dataset of this latter system was not sufficient to include it in the 
LR-framework.

2.1.2. Data pre-processing and input variables
The system yielded 230 samples across 10 experimental periods (2016–2018, 2–3 samples/week), excluding start-up and main­
tenance phases [40]. From 40 monitored variables, LR model inputs were selected based on previous multivariate (PCA, PLS) [40] and 
multiple linear regression analyses [31]. Selected inputs (Fig. 2) comprised: controllable operational parameters (HRT, SRT, Fair); 
inflow parameters (NLR, PLR); ambient factors (T, Tmax, PAR, PARmax); microalgal activity (DO, DOmax); and light distribution 
(OD680) [31].
Although NLR and PLR typically depend on HRT, they can be decoupled via stream blending to optimize biological activity and 
enable advanced control strategies [12,35]. Daily averages and maximums for PAR and T were incorporated, as their fluctuations 
critically dictate system performance [41,42]. Likewise, OD680 and DO serve as key indicators of light utilization and overall effi­
ciency [42]. Previous analyses of this dataset using multivariate projection methods (PCA and PLS) [40] and machine-learning 
techniques [31] identified the variables most strongly associated with process performance. Specifically, input variables for the LR 
model were selected based on correlation results and multiple linear regression (MLR) analysis, as detailed extensively in Szelag et al. 
[31]. Accordingly, the following inputs were considered (Fig. 2): (i) operational parameters (SRT, HRT, and Fair); (ii) influent con­
ditions (nitrogen (NLR) and phosphorus loading rates (PLR); (iii) photosynthetic activity indicators (DO and DOmax); (iv) ambient 
conditions (PAR and PARmax, and temperature T and Tmax); (v) light distribution, assessed by optical density at 680 nm (OD680); and 
(vi) effluent ammonium concentration (NH4–Neff). A Z-score pre-screening was performed to identify outliers and potential mea­
surement errors, with Zi = (xi −μi)/σi. All values were within the acceptance threshold (Zi = 0.01–2.95 < 3.0; Table S2), indicating no 
evidence of measurement errors in the analysed dataset.
