3.4.4. Advantages of the selected approach and future research
The approach employed in this study aligns with standard literature practices, wherein machine learning (ML) models are 
developed from experimental data, and the optimal model structure is identified during training and validation [68]. A primary 
advantage of the present approach is its ability to directly evaluate the dependencies between input variables and predicted outcomes, 
ensuring that the derived relationships are fundamentally sound from both a technological and biological perspective [72]. Conse­
quently, these ML models provide a robust foundation for the real-time optimisation and control of complex processes, tasks that 
traditionally require mechanistic control models (MCMs) [73]. However, calibrating MCMs is a highly complex endeavour; the 
resulting models are not always practical due to the intricate interactions and uncertainties among the calibrated kinetic and stoi­
chiometric parameters. In contrast, the logistic regression (LR) model developed in this study is highly interpretable and yields 
technologically correct operational dependencies.
Furthermore, this combination of logit models successfully mapped complex non-linear relationships, which frequently require 
complex “black-box” models such as MLP, SVM, or XGBoost [4,10]. Another advantage is the model's capacity to handle limited 
number measurement data, which typically poses a significant challenge when training multiclass ML models. The 1.5-year study 
period captured a wide range of wastewater quality fluctuations, encompassing episodes of both highly efficient system performance 
and significant process deterioration [37,40,47]. From a modelling perspective, this high variance was incredibly advantageous, 
enabling the creation of a robust tool capable of predicting NH4-Neff across widely varying conditions. In contrast, modelling 
microalgae-based systems at the pilot scale is often constrained by continuous high efficiency, which results in low output variance and 
limits the model's predictive scope. Despite a limited sampling frequency, all crucial factors governing the MPBR operation were 
captured [31].

Flow, DO, and temperature are commonly available as SCADA signals. In contrast, online TN, TP, PAR, and OD680 measurements 
may require additional instrumentation. Online nutrient analyzers can involve substantial capital and operating costs associated with 
reagents, calibration, cleaning, and maintenance. Where such instruments are unavailable, laboratory measurements or validated

Because LR has low computational requirements, its equations can be implemented within SCADA software, programmable logic 
controllers, process historians, or external supervisory platforms. The model can calculate the probability of compliance with the 
ammonium threshold and provide operator warnings or recommendations for adjusting HRT and SRT.

To further advance the predictive control and management of microalgae-based wastewater treatment and reuse systems, future 
research should focus on the following directions: 
i) Advanced Data Augmentation. To address the challenge of data scarcity common in long-term bioprocess monitoring, data 
augmentation techniques should be explored. Methods such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) or bootstrapping could 
be used to create larger, realistic synthetic datasets. This would not only improve the robustness and goodness-of-fit of the 
current models but also enable the training of more complex machine learning architectures that are currently unfeasible.
ii) Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs). Implementing PINNs based on fundamental differential equations (e.g., mass 
balances for substrate NH4-N and biomass) would seamlessly integrate physical and biological laws into the machine learning 
framework, ensuring predictions strictly adhere to physical constraints and enhancing the model's mechanistic validity and 
allowing for complex non-linear relationships and interactions between inputs.
iii) Sequential Hybrid Modelling (MCM þ ML). Developing a hybrid framework that integrates MCMs with ML models. MCMs 
could be used to simulate high-resolution internal biological state variables (e.g., specific biomass concentrations, inorganic 
nitrogen forms, dissolved oxygen, heterotrophic bacteria, ammonia-oxidising bacteria, nitrite-oxidising bacteria, and slowly 
biodegradable particulate organic matter). These simulated variables would then serve as inputs for the ML-based water reuse 
model. Additionally, ML algorithms could be deployed in series to dynamically correct the residual NH4-N prediction errors of 
the MCMs.
iv) Temporal Dynamics and Advanced Architectures. Incorporating the dynamic nature of input data on NH4-Neff estimates by 
moving beyond static models. High-resolution sensor data could be fed into advanced sequence models, such as Transformers or 
Liquid Neural Networks, which inherently account for temporal dependencies and operational delays.
v) Universal Model Development. Correlating the coefficients estimated via logistic regression with specific biological pa­
rameters (e.g., microalgae/bacteria ratios, activity of competing microorganisms) as well as physical and geographical con­
ditions, progressing toward the development of a universal predictive model that can be applied across different climates and 
reactor designs.
