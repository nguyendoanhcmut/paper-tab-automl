4. Conclusions
This study developed a logistic regression (LR) framework to probabilistically predict effluent ammonium nitrogen (NH4-Neff) and 
quantify water reuse potential in an outdoor membrane photobioreactor (MPBR) treating municipal wastewater. Based on 1.5 years of 
pilot-scale operational data, the following primary conclusions are drawn:

• Local sensitivity analysis indicated that photosynthetic activity (DO, DOmax) and ambient temperature exerted the strongest in­
fluence on predicted NH4-Neff, while the relevance of the aeration flow rate (Fair) increased markedly during high-ammonium 
episodes.
• Relying solely on hydraulic and solids retention times (HRT and SRT) is insufficient for maintaining optimal long-term performance 
under fluctuating outdoor conditions.
• In-silico simulations of an advanced control strategy—simultaneously optimising SRT, HRT, NLR, and Fair, projected a 12–50% 
reduction in NH4-Neff and a 32–38% reduction in aeration demand compared to fixed-parameter operations, thereby maximizing 
the estimated production of compliant reclaimed water.
While the LR framework presents a promising, highly interpretable alternative to complex mechanistic control models and “black- 
box” machine learning algorithms, it is important to note that this methodology was validated using pilot-scale data and computational 
simulations. Consequently, the broad applicability of this approach remains uncertain until subjected to further validation across 
diverse full-scale reactor designs, varying wastewater influent characteristics, and different climatic conditions.
