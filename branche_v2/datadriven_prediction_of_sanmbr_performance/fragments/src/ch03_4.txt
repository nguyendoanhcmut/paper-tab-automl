2.4  Data Acquisition and Pre-processing
The predictive modelling analysis was based on an experi­
mental dataset collected throughout the operational period 
of the Submerged Anaerobic Membrane Bioreactor 
(SAnMBR). In total, 189 valid data points were obtained, 
with no missing entries. The selection of input features (X) 
was guided by their known relevance to biological waste­
water treatment and their operational importance in the 
SAnMBR process. Six independent variables were used in 
the modelling: influent COD (mg/L), organic loading rate 
(OLR, gCOD/m³·day), influent 2,4-dichlorophenol (mg/L), 
influent turbidity (NTU), influent TSS (g/L), and influent 
pH. The dependent variable (Y) was the COD removal per­
centage (%). To ensure reliable model training and evalua­
tion, the dataset was randomly divided into two parts: 70% 
(around 132 samples) for model training and the remain­
ing 30% (around 56 samples) for testing. This division 
allowed the trained models to be evaluated on data they had 
not previously encountered, providing a fair assessment of 
their generalization capability. Prior to normalization, the 
dataset was first split into training and testing subsets. The 
Min–Max scaling parameters were then fitted using only the 
training data and subsequently applied to both training and 
testing sets. All input features were normalized using the 
Min–Max scaling method to rescale their values to the [0,1] 
range. This normalization step avoided bias toward vari­
ables with larger numerical ranges and ensured that each 
input contributed equally during training.
