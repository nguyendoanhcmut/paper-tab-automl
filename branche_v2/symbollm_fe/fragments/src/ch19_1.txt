Case 1: The Abrupt Age Threshold Effect
On the Titanic dataset, symbolic regression dis-
covered the formula tan(−0.607 −tan(X5)),
where X5 denotes passenger age. From a purely
mathematical perspective, the nested composition
tan(tan(X5)) is deeply opaque. Readers would
reasonably ask: why should the tangent of the tan-
gent of age carry predictive signal? This appears
to be the kind of overfitted artifact that genetic pro-
gramming is often criticized for producing.

nent tan(X5) and endowed it with domain seman-

trends. The rationale further explains that the tan-
gent function can model periodic and steep changes
in the relationship between age and the target vari-
able. In the context of a spaceship disaster, the
influence of age on survival is far from linear: mi-
nors may receive rescue priority, the elderly may
face mobility disadvantages, and a sharp transition
zone exists in middle age where survival probabil-
ity changes dramatically. The tan function mathe-
matically captures precisely this kind of threshold-
crossing behavior, it is the numerical projection of
the “women and children first” rescue doctrine into
the feature space. The leap in interpretability is
from “a nested trigonometric black box” to “the
rescue-priority steep-change effect of age.”
