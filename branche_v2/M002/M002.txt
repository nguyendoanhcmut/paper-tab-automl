1. Introduction
Membrane technology has been much popularly used in water and
wastewater treatment. However, it is confronted with a serious problem
of membrane fouling (Lin et al., 2012; Shen et al., 2019; Zhang et al.,
2017, 2019). Currently, knowledge of membrane fouling in water
processing applications, especially in membrane bioreactor (MBR)
systems, is still quite insuﬃcient (Lin et al., 2014b; Meng et al., 2017;
Teng et al., 2018b; Zhang et al., 2018), which encourages the related
research communities worldwide to further explore it.
It is widely accepted that, membrane fouling in MBRs is mainly
caused by adhesion of various foulants on membrane surface (or foulant

layer formation) (Chen et al., 2016, 2019; Wang et al., 2014; Zhang
et al., 2013). MBR provides a very complicated scenario for foulant
adhesion. Though hydrodynamic force from aeration, stirring and/or
ﬁltration would transport various foulants near membrane surface, ﬁnal
adhesion of the foulants is determined by the short-range interfacial
interactions of foulants with membrane surface (Chen et al., 2015,
2012; Hoek and Agarwal, 2006; Wang et al., 2013). Quantiﬁcation of
these interactions is, therefore, crucial for membrane fouling under-
standing. To realize this quantiﬁcation, extended Derjaguin–Landau–-
Verwey–Overbeek (XDLVO) theory was proposed, which is very helpful
to understand the forces controlling the adhesion process (Chen et al.,
2017; Ding et al., 2013; Hoek and Agarwal, 2006; van Oss, 1995). The
major problem of XDLVO theory is its limitation in application of
quantifying the interactions regarding rough surface (Azeredo et al.,
1999; Hoek and Agarwal, 2006; Shen et al., 2015). Whereas, the sur-
faces of almost all the membranes in applications are naturally rough
(Brant and Childress, 2002; Hoek and Agarwal, 2006; Wang et al.,
2013; Zhang et al., 2016). This contrast indicates the demand to de-
velop XDLVO theory so that it can be applicable for rough surfaces.
With the gigantic eﬀorts, recent studies (Chen et al., 2012; Hoek and
Agarwal, 2006) have proposed the advanced XDLVO approach by in-
troducing diﬀerential geometry and computing technique into the
XDLVO theory, which enables to simulate randomly rough surface and
to compute the interfacial interactions within two surfaces with arbi-
trary morphology. Introduction of these techniques signiﬁcantly ex-
tended XDLVO theory and oﬀered novel insights into fouling behaviors.
However, the overall computation involved excessively computational
time consumption. For instance, it generally requires several hours to
compute the interfacial interactions with a rough surface membrane at
a given separation distance by using a computer workstation (Teng
et al., 2018a, 2019). Therefore, it will take too much time to ﬁgure out
the proﬁle of the interfacial interactions during the full range of se-
paration distance. The high time consumption has been the major
barrier limiting the application of the advanced XDLVO approach in
calculation of the interfacial interactions, which calls for alternative
methods.
The high time consumption of the advanced XDLVO approach can
be attributed to the complicated nonlinear relationships between sur-
face topography and interfacial interactions (Cai et al., 2017a; Teng
et al., 2018a; Zhao et al., 2015). In view of this, as an eﬀective tool to
predict nonlinear systems, artiﬁcial neural networks (ANN) may act as
an alternative measure to eﬃciently predict the interfacial interactions
with complicated rough surface. ANN is a statistical tool inspired by the
nervous system of human brain (Iliyas et al., 2013). The network
structure in ANN would be adapted during training phase. In essence,
ANN is a self-learning technique enabling to obtain most accurate ap-
proximate solution of the nonlinear relationships (Curcio et al., 2006;
Dreiseitl and Ohno-Machado, 2002; Iliyas et al., 2013; Sun et al., 2018).
In recent years, successful use of ANN in ultraﬁltration (UF)/micro-
ﬁltration (MF) process control (Basheer and Hajmeer, 2000; Madaeni
et al., 2010), prediction of UF/MF performance (Chew et al., 2018;
Curcio et al., 2006), correlation of various factors with membrane
fouling (Corbatón-Báguena et al., 2016) and optimization of membrane
fabrication (Schmitt et al., 2018) have been reported. It is, therefore,
hypothesized that, ANN can describe the complex relationships be-
tween various factors and interfacial interactions involved into adhe-
sion process. A trained ANN can directly output the results, and
therefore, signiﬁcantly reduce computing burden. To our knowledge,
there is no speciﬁc report on the utilization of ANN for prediction of
interfacial interactions related with membrane fouling.
The purpose of this study is, therefore, to develop an ANN model for
prediction of the interfacial interactions with rough surface membrane
within the full short-range of separation distance (0.158–10 nm).

2. Materials and methods
2.1. Preparation of samples
The diagram of the lab-scale MBR used in this study is presented in
Supplementary information. The MBR system primarily consisted of the
sludge suspension and a membrane unit. The unit was comprised of
four ﬂat-sheet PVDF membrane elements (SINAP Co. Ltd., Shanghai)
with normalized pore size of 0.10 µm. In this study, the particulate
sludge in the sludge suspension sampled in the steady operation stage of
MBR was used as typical foulants. The foulant samples for contact angle
measurements were prepared by ﬁltration of sludge suspension to form
a cake layer followed by fastening the cake layer within two glass slides
and drying them in a desiccator (40 °C) for 24 h. Accordingly, the
membrane samples for contact angle measurements were prepared by
similar procedure.
2.2. Analytical methods
Contact angles of the three probe liquids on the sludge layer and the
membrane surface were tested by using a contact angle meter (Kino,
USA) according to the dropping method. Surface zeta potential of the
particulate sludge in the sludge suspension was measured by using a
Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS instrument. In contrast, zeta potential of the
membrane surface was measured using a NanoBrook 90Plus Zeta
Particle Size Analyzer. Images of the original PVDF membrane surface
were captured by an atomic force microscopy (AFM) (NT-MDT, USA).
The statistical height and root mean square roughness of the AFM
images were calculated by the software NanoScope analysis V1.80.

2.4. Radial basis function (RBF) ANN
ANN is a mathematical model similar to the structure of brain sy-
naptic connections for information processing. The outstanding ad-
vantages of ANN include self-study, storage and high-speed search
capabilities (Iliyas et al., 2013; Sreekanth et al., 2010). In addition,
feedforward neural networks are very popular in engineering applica-
tions (Basheer and Hajmeer, 2000). RBF ANN is a typical feedforward
neural network model, which has been proven to possess the universal
approximation ability and no local minimum problem (Bakire et al.,
2018; Dreiseitl and Ohno-Machado, 2002). Moreover, compared to
other neural networks, it has a simpler structure and a faster learning
algorithm (Basheer and Hajmeer, 2000; Sreekanth et al., 2010). Fig. 1
shows the structure of a typical RBF network with a single output. The
network consists of three layers, namely, the input layer, the hidden
layer and the output layer. A large amount of nonlinear information is
accepted by the input layer, and then transmitted through the hidden
layer. Finally, the output result is produced from the output layer.
While many activation functions are available for radial-based neurons,
Gaussian function is the most common one given as (Dreiseitl and
Ohno-Machado, 2002):

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Quantiﬁcation of interfacial interactions by the advanced XDLVO
approach
The AFM image in in Supplementary information shows the surface
morphology of the natural PVDF membrane characterized by the AFM.
It is evident that the natural PVDF membrane surface is extremely
rough where numerous asperities randomly distribute. In order to
quantify the interfacial interactions with the randomly rough mem-
brane surface, the membrane surface morphology should be simulated.
Recently, two methods have been successfully proposed to simulate the
randomly rough surface. One is the fractal method (Zhang et al., 2016)
and the other is the Gaussian method (Qu et al., 2018a). The surfaces
simulated by these two methods are just statistically similar rather than
similar in morphology to the real one, and therefore, are improper for
quantiﬁcation of interactions representing those of the real one.
Whereas, AFM characterization combined with triangulation technique
emerging as an alternative method has been recently proposed (Teng
et al., 2018a). The simulated membrane surface morphology by using
the aforementioned method is shown in Supplementary information. It
can be seen that, the simulated morphology is a continuous surface.
Moreover, the two surfaces have same statistical roughness values
(average
roughness = 26.7 nm,
root–mean–square
rough-
ness = 34.2 nm). It is therefore concluded that, the simulated mor-
phology well represents the real one.
Meanwhile, the surface properties of the membrane surface and
particulate sludge were characterized, and the results are given in
Table 1. Both the simulated surface and the measured surface properties
were used to quantify the interfacial interactions between the particu-
late sludge and the rough membrane based on the advanced XDLVO
approach provided in Section 2.3. With aid of supercomputing, the
interfacial interactions at a series of separation distances (60 points)
evenly distributed in range of 0.158–12 nm were quantiﬁed, and the
results are presented in Fig. 2. It is clear that the AB interaction is most
strengthen as compared with LW and EL interactions. Meanwhile, the
total interaction is negative, indicating the attractive force between the
sludge foulant and membrane when they approach to each other. Many
studies (Chen et al., 2012; Ding et al., 2013; Qu et al., 2018b; Teng
et al., 2018a) have also reported that AB interaction was decisive force
in total interaction between foulants and membranes. In the experi-
ment, it was observed that sludge foulants tended to adhere to mem-
brane surface to form a cake layer with the MBR operation. The con-
sistence of the calculation results with the literature results and the
experimental observation suggests the feasibility of the advanced
XDLVO approach in quantiﬁcation of interactions with rough mem-
brane surface. However, the calculation process by this method was
really time-consuming, which signiﬁcantly reduces the applicability of
this method. The detailed information regarding the time consumption
is presented in the latter section, and will compare with that for the RBF
ANN method.
3.2. Quantiﬁcation of interfacial interactions by RBF network
An RBF network need to be trained prior to application. Diﬀerent
number of interaction data obtained by the advanced XDLVO approach
were used for training. Fig. 3 shows the predicted interfacial interac-
tions with separation distance by using the RBF networks trained by the
diﬀerent number of interaction data (5, 10, 15 and 20 samples). It is
clear that, the more training sample number is, the higher accuracy of
the predicted results obtains. When the training sample number is only
5, the predicted interactions are far from those in Fig. 2. Whereas, in-
creasing training sample number to 10 corresponds to an almost same
interaction proﬁle to that in Fig. 2. These results suggest the high
suitability of RBF network for prediction of the interfacial interactions
with randomly rough membrane surface. Under conditions in this
study, 10 training samples are enough to get a relatively reliable net-
work for interaction prediction. Further increasing the training sample
number will improve the reliability of the trained network, but at the
expense of high computing time consumption. Fig. 3 indicates that RBF
ANN can be an alternative method to the advanced XDLVO method for
quantiﬁcation of the interfacial interactions with randomly rough

Fig. 4 compares the computing time consumption between the ad-
vanced XDLVO approach and the RBF network predictions with dif-
ferent training samples. Calculations by these two methods were exe-
cuted in platform of Matlab 2017b running in a computer workstation
(HP Z8 G4). It can be seen that, with the same platform, calculations by
the advanced XDLVO approach take 913 min, whereas, calculations by
RBF network predictions only took 84.1, 115.1, 225 and 291.5 min for
the situations of training samples of 5, 10, 15 and 20, respectively. Even
for the situations of the largest training samples (20) in this study, the
time consumption for RBF method is only about one-third of that for the
advanced XDLVO approach. It should be noted that, the double in-
tegrals involved into the advanced XDLVO approach were approxi-
mated based on the composite Simpson’s rule (Lin et al., 2014a). For
saving the calculation time, only 500 segments were assigned to the two
variables in the double integrals in this study. Generally, 2000 segments
are necessary to get passable and reliable results (Chen et al., 2015),
which will increase the calculation time for 16 times in theory. It may
be deduced that, time consumption of the RBF network even with the
largest training samples (20) was only about 1/50 of that of the ad-
vanced XDLVO approach in this study, showing the great advantage of
RBF network over the advanced XDLVO approach.
3.3. Calculation accuracy of the RBF ANN method
Calculation accuracy of the RBF ANN method was assessed by re-
lative error of the predicted interfacial interaction data according to Eq.
(14). Fig. 5 shows the relative error frequency of the predicted inter-
facial interaction data for the RBF networks trained by diﬀerent
training sample number of 5, 10, 15 and 20, respectively. It can be seen
from Fig. 5a that, the relative error is mainly in range from −0.293 to
0.386 for the situation of 5 training samples. The range of relative er-
rors are rather big, indicating that 5 training samples are not enough to
get a reliable RBF network for interfacial interaction prediction. In
contrast, training samples of no less than 10 correspond to small re-
lative errors (mainly in range from −0.03 to 0.1 shown in Fig. 5b–d).
The generally low relative errors suggest the feasibility of the trained
RBF network to predict interfacial interactions with rough membrane
surface. Considering the importance of interfacial interactions in
membrane fouling process, the proposed RBF ANN method has broad
application prospects in membrane fouling and interface behavior re-
search.
4. Conclusions
An RBF network was proposed to predict the interfacial interactions
with randomly rough membrane surface related with membrane
fouling. For training the network, the interactions with rough mem-
brane surface were also quantiﬁed by the advanced XDLVO approach.
The computing time consumption for interaction prediction by the RBF
network was only about 1/50 of that by the advance XDLVO approach
under same conditions, indicating high eﬃciency of the RBF ANN
method. Meanwhile, the RBF networks trained by no less than 10
