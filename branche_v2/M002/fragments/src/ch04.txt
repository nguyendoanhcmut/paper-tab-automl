3. Results and discussion

3.1. Quantiﬁcation of interfacial interactions by the advanced XDLVO
approach
The AFM image in in Supplementary information shows the surface
morphology of the natural PVDF membrane characterized by the AFM.
It is evident that the natural PVDF membrane surface is extremely
rough where numerous asperities randomly distribute. In order to
quantify the interfacial interactions with the randomly rough mem-
brane surface, the membrane surface morphology should be simulated.
Recently, two methods have been successfully proposed to simulate the
randomly rough surface. One is the fractal method (Zhang et al., 2016)
and the other is the Gaussian method (Qu et al., 2018a). The surfaces
simulated by these two methods are just statistically similar rather than
similar in morphology to the real one, and therefore, are improper for
quantiﬁcation of interactions representing those of the real one.
Whereas, AFM characterization combined with triangulation technique
emerging as an alternative method has been recently proposed (Teng
et al., 2018a). The simulated membrane surface morphology by using
the aforementioned method is shown in Supplementary information. It
can be seen that, the simulated morphology is a continuous surface.
Moreover, the two surfaces have same statistical roughness values
(average

ness = 34.2 nm). It is therefore concluded that, the simulated mor-
phology well represents the real one.
Meanwhile, the surface properties of the membrane surface and
particulate sludge were characterized, and the results are given in
Table 1. Both the simulated surface and the measured surface properties
were used to quantify the interfacial interactions between the particu-
late sludge and the rough membrane based on the advanced XDLVO
approach provided in Section 2.3. With aid of supercomputing, the
interfacial interactions at a series of separation distances (60 points)
evenly distributed in range of 0.158–12 nm were quantiﬁed, and the
results are presented in Fig. 2. It is clear that the AB interaction is most
strengthen as compared with LW and EL interactions. Meanwhile, the
total interaction is negative, indicating the attractive force between the
sludge foulant and membrane when they approach to each other. Many
studies (Chen et al., 2012; Ding et al., 2013; Qu et al., 2018b; Teng
et al., 2018a) have also reported that AB interaction was decisive force
in total interaction between foulants and membranes. In the experi-
ment, it was observed that sludge foulants tended to adhere to mem-
brane surface to form a cake layer with the MBR operation. The con-
sistence of the calculation results with the literature results and the
experimental observation suggests the feasibility of the advanced
XDLVO approach in quantiﬁcation of interactions with rough mem-
brane surface. However, the calculation process by this method was
really time-consuming, which signiﬁcantly reduces the applicability of
this method. The detailed information regarding the time consumption
is presented in the latter section, and will compare with that for the RBF
ANN method.

3.2. Quantiﬁcation of interfacial interactions by RBF network
An RBF network need to be trained prior to application. Diﬀerent
number of interaction data obtained by the advanced XDLVO approach
were used for training. Fig. 3 shows the predicted interfacial interac-
tions with separation distance by using the RBF networks trained by the
diﬀerent number of interaction data (5, 10, 15 and 20 samples). It is
clear that, the more training sample number is, the higher accuracy of
the predicted results obtains. When the training sample number is only
5, the predicted interactions are far from those in Fig. 2. Whereas, in-
creasing training sample number to 10 corresponds to an almost same
interaction proﬁle to that in Fig. 2. These results suggest the high
suitability of RBF network for prediction of the interfacial interactions
with randomly rough membrane surface. Under conditions in this
study, 10 training samples are enough to get a relatively reliable net-
work for interaction prediction. Further increasing the training sample
number will improve the reliability of the trained network, but at the
expense of high computing time consumption. Fig. 3 indicates that RBF
ANN can be an alternative method to the advanced XDLVO method for
quantiﬁcation of the interfacial interactions with randomly rough

Fig. 4 compares the computing time consumption between the ad-
vanced XDLVO approach and the RBF network predictions with dif-
ferent training samples. Calculations by these two methods were exe-
cuted in platform of Matlab 2017b running in a computer workstation
(HP Z8 G4). It can be seen that, with the same platform, calculations by
the advanced XDLVO approach take 913 min, whereas, calculations by
RBF network predictions only took 84.1, 115.1, 225 and 291.5 min for
the situations of training samples of 5, 10, 15 and 20, respectively. Even
for the situations of the largest training samples (20) in this study, the
time consumption for RBF method is only about one-third of that for the
advanced XDLVO approach. It should be noted that, the double in-
tegrals involved into the advanced XDLVO approach were approxi-
mated based on the composite Simpson’s rule (Lin et al., 2014a). For
saving the calculation time, only 500 segments were assigned to the two
variables in the double integrals in this study. Generally, 2000 segments
are necessary to get passable and reliable results (Chen et al., 2015),
which will increase the calculation time for 16 times in theory. It may
be deduced that, time consumption of the RBF network even with the
largest training samples (20) was only about 1/50 of that of the ad-
vanced XDLVO approach in this study, showing the great advantage of
RBF network over the advanced XDLVO approach.

3.3. Calculation accuracy of the RBF ANN method
Calculation accuracy of the RBF ANN method was assessed by re-
lative error of the predicted interfacial interaction data according to Eq.
(14). Fig. 5 shows the relative error frequency of the predicted inter-
facial interaction data for the RBF networks trained by diﬀerent
training sample number of 5, 10, 15 and 20, respectively. It can be seen
from Fig. 5a that, the relative error is mainly in range from −0.293 to
0.386 for the situation of 5 training samples. The range of relative er-
rors are rather big, indicating that 5 training samples are not enough to
get a reliable RBF network for interfacial interaction prediction. In
contrast, training samples of no less than 10 correspond to small re-
lative errors (mainly in range from −0.03 to 0.1 shown in Fig. 5b–d).
The generally low relative errors suggest the feasibility of the trained
RBF network to predict interfacial interactions with rough membrane
surface. Considering the importance of interfacial interactions in
membrane fouling process, the proposed RBF ANN method has broad
application prospects in membrane fouling and interface behavior re-
search.
