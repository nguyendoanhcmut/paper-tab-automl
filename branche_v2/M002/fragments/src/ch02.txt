1. Introduction
Membrane technology has been much popularly used in water and
wastewater treatment. However, it is confronted with a serious problem
of membrane fouling (Lin et al., 2012; Shen et al., 2019; Zhang et al.,
2017, 2019). Currently, knowledge of membrane fouling in water
processing applications, especially in membrane bioreactor (MBR)
systems, is still quite insuﬃcient (Lin et al., 2014b; Meng et al., 2017;
Teng et al., 2018b; Zhang et al., 2018), which encourages the related
research communities worldwide to further explore it.
It is widely accepted that, membrane fouling in MBRs is mainly
caused by adhesion of various foulants on membrane surface (or foulant

layer formation) (Chen et al., 2016, 2019; Wang et al., 2014; Zhang
et al., 2013). MBR provides a very complicated scenario for foulant
adhesion. Though hydrodynamic force from aeration, stirring and/or
ﬁltration would transport various foulants near membrane surface, ﬁnal
adhesion of the foulants is determined by the short-range interfacial
interactions of foulants with membrane surface (Chen et al., 2015,
2012; Hoek and Agarwal, 2006; Wang et al., 2013). Quantiﬁcation of
these interactions is, therefore, crucial for membrane fouling under-
standing. To realize this quantiﬁcation, extended Derjaguin–Landau–-
Verwey–Overbeek (XDLVO) theory was proposed, which is very helpful
to understand the forces controlling the adhesion process (Chen et al.,
2017; Ding et al., 2013; Hoek and Agarwal, 2006; van Oss, 1995). The
major problem of XDLVO theory is its limitation in application of
quantifying the interactions regarding rough surface (Azeredo et al.,
1999; Hoek and Agarwal, 2006; Shen et al., 2015). Whereas, the sur-
faces of almost all the membranes in applications are naturally rough
(Brant and Childress, 2002; Hoek and Agarwal, 2006; Wang et al.,
2013; Zhang et al., 2016). This contrast indicates the demand to de-
velop XDLVO theory so that it can be applicable for rough surfaces.
With the gigantic eﬀorts, recent studies (Chen et al., 2012; Hoek and
Agarwal, 2006) have proposed the advanced XDLVO approach by in-
troducing diﬀerential geometry and computing technique into the
XDLVO theory, which enables to simulate randomly rough surface and
to compute the interfacial interactions within two surfaces with arbi-
trary morphology. Introduction of these techniques signiﬁcantly ex-
tended XDLVO theory and oﬀered novel insights into fouling behaviors.
However, the overall computation involved excessively computational
time consumption. For instance, it generally requires several hours to
compute the interfacial interactions with a rough surface membrane at
a given separation distance by using a computer workstation (Teng
et al., 2018a, 2019). Therefore, it will take too much time to ﬁgure out
the proﬁle of the interfacial interactions during the full range of se-
paration distance. The high time consumption has been the major
barrier limiting the application of the advanced XDLVO approach in
calculation of the interfacial interactions, which calls for alternative
methods.
The high time consumption of the advanced XDLVO approach can
be attributed to the complicated nonlinear relationships between sur-
face topography and interfacial interactions (Cai et al., 2017a; Teng
et al., 2018a; Zhao et al., 2015). In view of this, as an eﬀective tool to
predict nonlinear systems, artiﬁcial neural networks (ANN) may act as
an alternative measure to eﬃciently predict the interfacial interactions
with complicated rough surface. ANN is a statistical tool inspired by the
nervous system of human brain (Iliyas et al., 2013). The network
structure in ANN would be adapted during training phase. In essence,
ANN is a self-learning technique enabling to obtain most accurate ap-
proximate solution of the nonlinear relationships (Curcio et al., 2006;
Dreiseitl and Ohno-Machado, 2002; Iliyas et al., 2013; Sun et al., 2018).
In recent years, successful use of ANN in ultraﬁltration (UF)/micro-
ﬁltration (MF) process control (Basheer and Hajmeer, 2000; Madaeni
et al., 2010), prediction of UF/MF performance (Chew et al., 2018;
Curcio et al., 2006), correlation of various factors with membrane
fouling (Corbatón-Báguena et al., 2016) and optimization of membrane
fabrication (Schmitt et al., 2018) have been reported. It is, therefore,
hypothesized that, ANN can describe the complex relationships be-
tween various factors and interfacial interactions involved into adhe-
sion process. A trained ANN can directly output the results, and
therefore, signiﬁcantly reduce computing burden. To our knowledge,
there is no speciﬁc report on the utilization of ANN for prediction of
interfacial interactions related with membrane fouling.
The purpose of this study is, therefore, to develop an ANN model for
prediction of the interfacial interactions with rough surface membrane
within the full short-range of separation distance (0.158–10 nm).
