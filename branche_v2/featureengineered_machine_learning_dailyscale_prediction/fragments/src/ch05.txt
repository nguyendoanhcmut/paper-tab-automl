This study presented an interpretable, feature-engineered machine-learning framework for daily-scale prediction and oper­
ational optimization of effluent T-P in a full-scale DAF system. Overall, the results demonstrate that the proposed 
framework effectively integrates long-term operational data, process-informed feature engineering, and explainable machine 
learning to provide both reliable prediction and actionable operational insight under realistic wastewater treatment plant 
conditions.
From a predictive perspective, the Random Forest model exhibited robust and reliable performance, achieving a test R2 
of 0.82 and an RMSE of 0.032 mg/L, corresponding to a prediction error within 20% of the regulatory discharge limit 
(0.2 mg/L). This level of performance indicates that the model can sufficiently distinguish normal operating conditions 
from potential compliance-risk scenarios on a daily decision-making horizon, where laboratory-based measurements and 
routine operational adjustments dominate.
Interpretability analysis using SHAP further revealed that influent T-P concentration and coagulant dosage are the domi­
nant drivers of effluent T-P variability, while short-term variation features and seasonal indicators modulate system response 
under dynamic loading and environmental conditions. The consistency of these feature contributions across seasons 
suggests that the learned relationships are physically consistent with established DAF process mechanisms, thereby enhan­
cing model transparency and supporting operator confidence.

Building on these predictive and interpretative results, a sensitivity-based, autoregressive simulation framework was 
applied to evaluate coagulant dosing strategies. The simulation results indicate that conditionally optimized dosing levels 
could reduce coagulant consumption by 32–51% across seasons while simultaneously narrowing the effluent T-P distri­
bution toward the target compliance range. The corresponding annual coagulant cost saving was estimated at 
approximately 1.53 billion KRW, based solely on coagulant purchasing costs. These optimized dosing levels represent 
model-based recommendations derived from scenario simulations rather than guaranteed outcomes from field implemen­
tation, highlighting the role of the framework as a decision-support tool rather than a prescriptive control system.
Taken together, the results indicate that combining daily-scale prediction, explainable machine learning, and sensitivity- 
based optimization provides a practical and scalable alternative to traditional experience-driven or fixed-ratio dosing strat­
egies. The proposed framework is particularly suited to full-scale facilities where online T-P sensors or fully automated 
control systems are limited, yet stable regulatory compliance and chemical efficiency are required. Future work will focus 
on field validation, integration with upstream–downstream process interactions, and extension toward digital-twin and 
reinforcement-learning–based operational control.
