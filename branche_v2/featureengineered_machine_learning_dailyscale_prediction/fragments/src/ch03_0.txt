This study developed a daily-scale prediction and scenario-based optimization framework for effluent T-P in a full-scale DAF 
process. The overall methodological workflow is organized into three sequential stages, as illustrated in Figure 1.
The first stage focuses on data preprocessing, including multi-source data acquisition, outlier screening, missing-value 
imputation, and feature engineering to represent process mechanisms, short-term variability, and seasonal effects in daily 
DAF operation. The second stage involves model development and evaluation, in which machine-learning models are 
trained using the engineered feature set, optimized through Bayesian hyperparameter tuning, and assessed using standard 
regression performance metrics. The third stage integrates model interpretation and operational optimization, where SHap­
ley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) are applied to quantify feature contributions and interactions, and a scenario-based 
autoregressive simulation framework is used to evaluate the sensitivity of effluent T-P to coagulant dosing. Together, 
these stages provide an interpretable and actionable decision-support framework for full-scale DAF operation.
