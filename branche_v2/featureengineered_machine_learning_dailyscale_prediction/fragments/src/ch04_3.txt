Figure 5 presents the Pearson correlation coefficients between effluent T-P and the candidate input features considered for 
model development. Overall, the correlations reveal meaningful linear associations that reflect the mechanistic behaviour of 
the DAF system.
Among the variables, influent T-P exhibited the strongest positive correlation with effluent T-P, indicating that higher 
upstream phosphorus loading tends to increase the residual phosphorus concentration after flotation. Influent SS also 
showed a moderate positive correlation, consistent with the role of particulate matter as a carrier of phosphorus species 
that affects coagulation and flotation performance.
In contrast, coagulant dosage demonstrated a clear negative correlation with effluent T-P. This relationship reflects the 
expected chemical-removal mechanism wherein higher doses of ferric coagulant enhance phosphorus precipitation and 
reduce the remaining phosphorus in the effluent. The strength of this negative correlation underscores the operational 
importance of appropriate dosing control.
Hydraulic indicators such as DAF inflow and IRFR showed only weak correlations with the target variable, implying that 
flow fluctuations or dissolved-air recirculation ratios have limited direct linear influence on effluent T-P under normal oper­
ational conditions. Similarly, DAF_A/F exhibited little linear association, which is reasonable given its narrow operational 
range in full-scale practice.
Meteorological variables including air temperature and rainfall showed weak but directionally consistent correlations. Air 
temperature displayed a slightly positive association, possibly reflecting seasonal variations in reaction kinetics or influent 
loading patterns. Rainfall exhibited a weak positive correlation as well, aligning with dilution and wash-off effects during 
wet-weather events.
The month variable showed only minimal correlation with the target, indicating that seasonality alone does not strongly 
determine effluent T-P levels unless coupled with other factors such as dosing or influent variability.
