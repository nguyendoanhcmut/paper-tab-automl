The proposed sensitivity-based optimization framework resulted in substantial reductions in coagulant usage throughout the 
test period. Based on predictive simulations using the trained model, as summarized in Table 7, the optimized strategy 
decreased the average daily dosing requirements across all seasons by 32% in spring, 49% in summer, 51% in autumn, 
and 44% in winter. When extrapolated to an annual scale, these simulation-based reductions correspond to an estimated 
chemical cost savings of approximately 1.53 billion KRW per year, calculated using the actual coagulant purchasing price 
applied at the study site (24,840 KRW per 20 kg, equivalent to 1,242 KRW/kg), underscoring the strong economic benefits 
of data-driven dosing control.
The reported cost savings reflect chemical purchasing costs only and do not include secondary operational expenses such 
as sludge handling, polymer consumption, or downstream treatment impacts. It should be noted that these optimized dosing 
levels represent recommended operating targets derived from model-based scenario simulations, rather than guaranteed out­
comes from field implementation. These results suggest that the conventional operation relied on excessive or inconsistent 
chemical addition, whereas the optimized algorithm identified a conditionally optimal dosage range under the assumed 
model accuracy that is sufficient to sustain treatment performance.
