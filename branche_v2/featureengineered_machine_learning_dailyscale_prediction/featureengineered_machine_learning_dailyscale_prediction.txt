ABSTRACT
Effective phosphorus control in dissolved air flotation (DAF) systems is essential for regulatory compliance in full-scale wastewater treat­
ment plants, yet operational decisions are often constrained to daily time scales due to limited online sensing. This study proposes an 
interpretable, feature-engineered machine-learning framework for daily prediction of effluent total phosphorus (T-P) and sensitivity- 
based optimization of coagulant dosing in a full-scale municipal DAF system (410,000 m3/day). Long-term operational, water-quality, and 
meteorological data (1,096 days) were preprocessed using three-sigma outlier screening and multivariate imputation by chained equations 
(MICE). Mechanistically informed features capturing influent loading, operational conditions, short-term variability of effluent T-P (1–3 day 
difference-based features), and seasonal effects were incorporated. Among the evaluated models, Random Forest achieved the best per­
formance (Test R2 = 0.818; RMSE = 0.032 mg/L), corresponding to a prediction error within 20% of the discharge limit (0.2 mg/L). SHAP 
analysis identified influent T-P, coagulant dosage, and short-term variation as dominant drivers across seasons. A sensitivity-based auto­
regressive simulation indicated that optimized dosing could reduce coagulant consumption by 32–51%, yielding an estimated annual 
cost saving of 1.53 billion KRW while improving effluent stability. The proposed framework demonstrates the practical value of daily- 
scale, interpretable machine learning for data-driven DAF operation.
Key words: coagulant optimization, DAF effluent total phosphorus prediction, data-driven tertiary treatment management, feature- 
engineered machine learning, SHAP-based model interpretability

GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT
1. INTRODUCTION
Excessive phosphorus discharge from wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs) is widely recognized as a major contributor to 
eutrophication in receiving water bodies. To mitigate this environmental risk, South Korea incorporated total phosphorus 
(T-P) into national effluent standards in 1996 and subsequently strengthened region-specific discharge limits to 0.2–0.5 mg/L 
from 2012 onward (Kim et al. 1996; Seo et al. 2011; Kim & Chung 2022). These regulatory changes have substantially 
increased the operational pressure on municipal WWTPs, making stable compliance with effluent standards a fundamental 
operational requirement.
To meet tightened T-P discharge limits, physico-chemical phosphorus removal processes have been increasingly adopted 
to complement biological nutrient removal, which alone is often insufficient under stringent regulatory conditions (Hu et al. 
2012; Bunce et al. 2018; Abdoli et al. 2024; Alatabe 2024). Among these technologies, dissolved air flotation (DAF) has been 
widely applied as an advanced or tertiary treatment due to its high clarification efficiency, compact footprint, and suitability 
for retrofitting in space-limited facilities (Edzwald 2010; Kwak & Lee 2015; Lee et al. 2016). Both pilot- and full-scale studies 
have demonstrated that, when appropriate coagulation and flotation conditions are maintained, DAF can achieve consist­
ently low effluent T-P concentrations, confirming its effectiveness for phosphorus control in municipal WWTPs (Maeng et al. 
2017; Väänänen et al. 2023). In addition, pilot-scale studies integrating DAF with complementary physico-chemical or bio­
logical processes, such as algal photo-bioreactors, have demonstrated effective removal of chemical oxygen demand (COD), 
nutrients, and suspended solids, highlighting the flexibility of DAF as part of hybrid advanced treatment systems (Badawi 
et al. 2022).
Despite its proven performance, DAF operation is highly sensitive to influent water quality variability and coagulant 
dosing conditions. Insufficient coagulant dosing frequently results in effluent T-P exceedances, whereas excessive dosing 
leads to unnecessary chemical consumption and increased sludge production (Di Bella et al. 2014; Khettaf et al. 2021; 
Narges et al. 2021; Wang et al. 2021a). Consequently, determining an optimal coagulant dosage represents a critical oper­
ational challenge from both economic and regulatory perspectives. Nevertheless, many WWTPs still rely on empirical 
strategies such as fixed-ratio or flow-proportional dosing, which often deviate from optimal process conditions when influent 
phosphorus concentration, turbidity, and hydraulic loading fluctuate rapidly (Álvarez Díez et al. 2024).
To address these limitations, recent studies have explored data-driven and intelligent approaches for coagulation process 
optimization (Liu et al. 2024; Lu et al. 2024; Sheik et al. 2024). In particular, machine-learning models have been success­
fully applied to large-scale, long-term operational datasets from full-scale WWTPs to predict effluent total phosphorus,

demonstrating the feasibility of data-driven water-quality forecasting under highly variable influent and process conditions 
(Ly et al. 2022). Machine-learning-based models have been proposed to predict optimal coagulant dosage using historical 
and operational data, demonstrating improved robustness and efficiency compared with conventional empirical control 
strategies (Wang et al. 2021b; Fang et al. 2022; Tochio et al. 2023; Kim et al. 2024a, b).
Despite these advances, the practical deployment of real-time control strategies operating at minute- or hourly-scale 
remains limited in full-scale municipal WWTPs. This is mainly due to sensor reliability issues, maintenance demands, and 
the difficulty of integrating advanced control systems with existing operational infrastructures (Pilz et al. 2024; Li et al. 
2025). As a result, most municipal WWTPs are not operated under continuous real-time control but rather rely on daily lab­
oratory-based effluent measurements and routine operational records for process management (Alsulaili & Refaie 2021; 
Afan et al. 2024). Under such realistic operating conditions, operational decisions related to chemical dosing are typically 
made at a daily or shift-based time scale.
From this perspective, daily-scale prediction models represent a pragmatic and operationally relevant alternative to 
minute- or hourly-scale real-time optimization strategies, particularly in facilities where online phosphorus sensors or 
fully automated dosing systems are not yet implemented (Guo et al. 2020; Cechinel et al. 2024; Lai 2025). However, 
many existing daily-scale prediction studies rely on black-box models with limited interpretability, which restricts their appli­
cability to practical decision-making in WWTP operation.
The primary objective of this study is to develop an interpretable machine-learning framework for daily prediction of DAF 
effluent T-P concentrations in full-scale municipal wastewater treatment plants. The proposed framework integrates water- 
quality factors, DAF operational factors, and meteorological factors with engineered features that capture short-term phos­
phorus variability and seasonal patterns, thereby reflecting process dynamics under real operating conditions. Rather than 
focusing solely on predictive accuracy, this study emphasizes model interpretability to identify key operational drivers and to 
support informed decision-making in coagulant dosing management. In addition, the predictive framework is used to exam­
ine the sensitivity of effluent T-P concentrations to changes in coagulant dosage, illustrating its potential to reduce chemical 
consumption while maintaining stable regulatory compliance. By combining daily-scale prediction, interpretability, and 
operational relevance, this study aims to advance the practical application of machine learning in physicochemical waste­
water treatment and sustainable utility management.
2. METHODS
This study developed a daily-scale prediction and scenario-based optimization framework for effluent T-P in a full-scale DAF 
process. The overall methodological workflow is organized into three sequential stages, as illustrated in Figure 1.
The first stage focuses on data preprocessing, including multi-source data acquisition, outlier screening, missing-value 
imputation, and feature engineering to represent process mechanisms, short-term variability, and seasonal effects in daily 
DAF operation. The second stage involves model development and evaluation, in which machine-learning models are 
trained using the engineered feature set, optimized through Bayesian hyperparameter tuning, and assessed using standard 
regression performance metrics. The third stage integrates model interpretation and operational optimization, where SHap­
ley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) are applied to quantify feature contributions and interactions, and a scenario-based 
autoregressive simulation framework is used to evaluate the sensitivity of effluent T-P to coagulant dosing. Together, 
these stages provide an interpretable and actionable decision-support framework for full-scale DAF operation.
2.1. Data acquisition
The target municipal WWTP has a design capacity of 410,000 m3/day and operates with two parallel treatment lines. The 
main biological treatment process follows the Modified Ludzack–Ettinger (MLE) configuration, after which the secondary 
effluent is conveyed to tertiary treatment units comprising a micro disk filter (MDF) and a DAF system operated in parallel.
Among these tertiary treatment processes, the present study focuses on the full-scale DAF system, which serves as the pri­
mary physicochemical unit for T-P removal. DAF is a physicochemical solid–liquid separation process in which air is 
dissolved into water under high pressure and subsequently released to generate fine microbubbles through rapid depressur­
ization. In accordance with Henry’s law, increased pressure enhances air solubility, allowing the treated water to become air- 
saturated in the pressurization unit. The generated microbubbles attach to chemically destabilized colloidal particles and 
coagulated flocs formed by ferric sulfate dosing, imparting buoyancy to the aggregates. These bubble–floc complexes rise

To develop a daily-scale prediction model capable of capturing the complex behaviour of effluent T-P in the DAF process, 
an extensive feature-engineering strategy was implemented to incorporate both mechanistic process knowledge and the stat­
istical characteristics of real plant operation. In full-scale systems, effluent T-P exhibits pronounced variability arising from 
multiple interacting factors, including fluctuations in coagulant dosing here, ferric sulfate (Fe2(SO4)3) used as the primary 
coagulant rainfall-driven changes in influent dilution and particulate loading, seasonal variations in temperature that 
modify coagulation and flotation reaction kinetics, operational conditions related to mixing intensity, micro-bubble avail­
ability, and solids loading, as well as event-driven disturbances in influent quality.
To represent these nonlinear and multiscale dynamics more effectively than with simple lag inputs alone, the feature- 
engineering process incorporated short-term variation metrics based on one-, two-, and three-day differences in effluent 
T-P. These difference-based features capture the direction and magnitude of rapid fluctuations that commonly arise from dis­
turbance-driven conditions such as rainfall-induced influent changes, temporary under-dosing of coagulants, or shifts in 
particulate characteristics. By summarizing immediate transitions and short-horizon trends, these descriptors allow the 
model to better detect localized instability and deviation from typical operating patterns.
Importantly, all short-term variation features were computed exclusively using information available up to the prediction 
timestamp, ensuring that no future observations were used in feature construction and thereby avoiding information leakage 
in the daily operational setting. As illustrated in the feature-engineering schematic, the final input set therefore consisted of 
short-term T-P variation features (ΔT-P at 1–3 day lags), which capture rapid disturbance-driven dynamics, together with a 
seasonal indicator (month) to represent longer-term cyclic effects in DAF operation. This design is consistent with variable 
delay-time and sensitivity analyses reported in related water and groundwater modelling studies, where explicitly screening 
lagged and differenced inputs has been shown to improve predictive robustness while preserving operational realism 
(Sharghi et al. 2025). As a result, they enhance the model’s sensitivity to abrupt changes and improve its ability to distinguish 
stable periods from disturbance-prone intervals in daily DAF operation.
In addition, process-mechanistic operational indicators including the air-to-inflow ratio (DAF_A/F), ferric-sulfate dosing 
rate, influent flow, and the IRFR were incorporated to reflect structural relationships among operational conditions, bubble 
saturation, mixing performance, and phosphorus separation efficiency.
External environmental drivers such as air temperature, rainfall intensity, and a monthly seasonal index were also 
included to capture climate-dependent variations in hydraulic loading and coagulation–flotation behaviour. Finally, raw- 
water quality indicators such as influent T-P and influent SS were integrated to account for upstream water-quality con­
ditions that directly influence coagulant demand and flotation efficiency. Collectively, this feature-engineering framework 
provides a unified representation of process mechanisms, short-term dynamics, seasonality, and disturbance signals, effec­
tively addressing the inherent non-stationarity and high variability of daily DAF operation and thereby improving the 
robustness, predictive performance, and interpretability of the machine-learning models developed in this study.
2.3. Development of ML-based models for DAF effluent T-P prediction model
To construct a reliable daily-scale prediction model for DAF effluent T-P, four representative machine-learning algorithms 
Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost and LightGBM were developed and evaluated. These models were selected to com­
pare different tree-based learning paradigms, including a single interpretable tree structure (Decision Tree), a bootstrap 
aggregation ensemble (Random Forest), a gradient-boosting framework with regularization (XGBoost), and a histogram- 
based gradient-boosting algorithm optimized for large-scale tabular environmental data (LightGBM).
Hyperparameter tuning was performed using a Bayesian optimization framework to efficiently explore the parameter 
space while minimizing computational cost. The search space for each model followed the ranges summarized in 
Table 2. For all models, the objective was to minimize the mean squared error (MSE) between observed and predicted efflu­
ent T-P concentrations.

tion errors and is particularly sensitive to large deviations, thereby reflecting the model’s ability to avoid substantial under- or 
over-prediction. MAE represents the average absolute difference between observed and predicted values and provides a

For the optimal model, two SHAP outputs were analysed: (1) Global feature contribution, based on mean absolute Shap­

values, to examine nonlinear dependencies between major operational factors. This SHAP-based analysis enabled a trans­

insight into the DAF process.
2.6. Sensitivity-based optimization of coagulant dosing
In this study, a sensitivity-based optimization framework was implemented to evaluate how changes in coagulant dosage 
influence effluent T-P concentrations and to identify the minimum dosing level required to satisfy the target effluent stan­
dard. Using the machine-learning-based DAF effluent T-P prediction model, dynamic simulations were conducted to 
quantify the system’s response to incremental adjustments in coagulant dosing intensity.
To simulate the effect of dosing modifications, a multiplier m was applied to the baseline coagulant dosage at each time 
step t, generating a series of hypothetical operational scenarios. The autoregressive structure was then employed to propa­
gate the impact of dosage adjustments over time. Specifically, short-term variation features were recalculated at each 
prediction step using the model’s preceding predicted values rather than observed measurements. This recursive updating 
scheme enables the simulation to capture cumulative process responses and nonlinear feedback effects that arise when 
dosing levels deviate from the baseline operational condition.
Through this autoregressive, multiplier-based simulation approach, the sensitivity of effluent T-P to different dosing inten­
sities was quantified, enabling the determination of optimal and suboptimal dosing ranges that balance regulatory 
compliance and chemical usage.
3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1. Statistical analysis of characteristics of the target DAF system
Figure 3 presents the violin plots and histograms of major hydraulic and water-quality parameters including DAF inflow, 
influent pH, influent SS, influent T-P, and effluent T-P before and after outlier removal based on the three-sigma rule. Over­
all, the cleaned distributions exhibit reduced extreme values and smoother probability densities, indicating that abnormal 
spikes caused by operational noise, laboratory errors, or temporary disturbances were effectively removed. Importantly, 
the overall shapes of the histograms demonstrate that the outlier filtering procedure did not distort the original data struc­
ture; instead, it selectively removed anomalous points while preserving the inherent distributional characteristics of the 
dataset.
Consistent with this, the DAF inflow distribution shows only minimal changes after cleaning, reflecting the inherent stab­
ility of hydraulic loading data and confirming that outlier removal did not alter the natural variability of flow patterns. In 
contrast, more noticeable improvements were observed in influent SS, influent T-P, and effluent T-P, where long-tailed 
and skewed distributions became more compact and symmetric after removing extreme observations. These refinements 
indicate that the applied filtering effectively removed unrepresentative spikes without modifying the central tendency or typi­
cal operating ranges.
Taken together, the results demonstrate that the three-sigma–based outlier removal method successfully eliminated irregu­
lar measurement spikes while maintaining the essential statistical characteristics of the original dataset, ensuring reliable and 
unbiased input for downstream machine-learning modelling.
Table 3 summarizes the extent of missing data and the number of observations removed as outliers for each candidate 
variable. Overall, the proportion of outliers identified by the three-sigma rule was low across all variables, indicating that 
extreme values were rare and limited to a small number of observations. Missing data was mainly concentrated in influent 
pH, reflecting intermittent sensor downtime, whereas most hydraulic, operational, and meteorological variables exhibited 
negligible missingness. These results indicate that the applied outlier removal procedure was limited in scope and did not 
substantially alter the overall data distribution.

3.3. Development of the optimal DAF effluent T-P prediction model
Figure 5 presents the Pearson correlation coefficients between effluent T-P and the candidate input features considered for 
model development. Overall, the correlations reveal meaningful linear associations that reflect the mechanistic behaviour of 
the DAF system.
Among the variables, influent T-P exhibited the strongest positive correlation with effluent T-P, indicating that higher 
upstream phosphorus loading tends to increase the residual phosphorus concentration after flotation. Influent SS also 
showed a moderate positive correlation, consistent with the role of particulate matter as a carrier of phosphorus species 
that affects coagulation and flotation performance.
In contrast, coagulant dosage demonstrated a clear negative correlation with effluent T-P. This relationship reflects the 
expected chemical-removal mechanism wherein higher doses of ferric coagulant enhance phosphorus precipitation and 
reduce the remaining phosphorus in the effluent. The strength of this negative correlation underscores the operational 
importance of appropriate dosing control.
Hydraulic indicators such as DAF inflow and IRFR showed only weak correlations with the target variable, implying that 
flow fluctuations or dissolved-air recirculation ratios have limited direct linear influence on effluent T-P under normal oper­
ational conditions. Similarly, DAF_A/F exhibited little linear association, which is reasonable given its narrow operational 
range in full-scale practice.
Meteorological variables including air temperature and rainfall showed weak but directionally consistent correlations. Air 
temperature displayed a slightly positive association, possibly reflecting seasonal variations in reaction kinetics or influent 
loading patterns. Rainfall exhibited a weak positive correlation as well, aligning with dilution and wash-off effects during 
wet-weather events.
The month variable showed only minimal correlation with the target, indicating that seasonality alone does not strongly 
determine effluent T-P levels unless coupled with other factors such as dosing or influent variability.

that linear approaches are not suitable for wastewater treatment processes characterized by strong nonlinearities and com­
plex interactions among hydraulic, operational, and water-quality variables. In contrast, tree-based ensemble models 
substantially improved predictive accuracy by effectively capturing these nonlinear behaviours. Among them, Random 
Forest achieved the best overall performance, recording the highest test R2 (0.8175) and the lowest RMSE (0.0324 mg/L)

Overall, influent T-P emerged as the most influential factor, exhibiting the highest mean absolute SHAP value. This indi­
cates that upstream phosphorus loading acts as the primary driver of daily effluent T-P variability in the DAF process. 
Coagulant dosage ranked second, with a mean absolute SHAP value of approximately 0.005–0.006 mg/L, confirming that 
chemical dosing intensity directly governs phosphorus precipitation efficiency and thereby strongly affects the predicted 
effluent concentration (Wang et al. 2021b).

dosing and influent T-P to shape overall DAF performance. In contrast, IRFR and rainfall showed comparatively minor influ­

3.5. Sensitivity-based optimization of coagulant dosing determination
The proposed sensitivity-based optimization framework resulted in substantial reductions in coagulant usage throughout the 
test period. Based on predictive simulations using the trained model, as summarized in Table 7, the optimized strategy 
decreased the average daily dosing requirements across all seasons by 32% in spring, 49% in summer, 51% in autumn, 
and 44% in winter. When extrapolated to an annual scale, these simulation-based reductions correspond to an estimated 
chemical cost savings of approximately 1.53 billion KRW per year, calculated using the actual coagulant purchasing price 
applied at the study site (24,840 KRW per 20 kg, equivalent to 1,242 KRW/kg), underscoring the strong economic benefits 
of data-driven dosing control.
The reported cost savings reflect chemical purchasing costs only and do not include secondary operational expenses such 
as sludge handling, polymer consumption, or downstream treatment impacts. It should be noted that these optimized dosing 
levels represent recommended operating targets derived from model-based scenario simulations, rather than guaranteed out­
comes from field implementation. These results suggest that the conventional operation relied on excessive or inconsistent 
chemical addition, whereas the optimized algorithm identified a conditionally optimal dosage range under the assumed 
model accuracy that is sufficient to sustain treatment performance.

4. CONCLUSIONS
This study presented an interpretable, feature-engineered machine-learning framework for daily-scale prediction and oper­
ational optimization of effluent T-P in a full-scale DAF system. Overall, the results demonstrate that the proposed 
framework effectively integrates long-term operational data, process-informed feature engineering, and explainable machine 
learning to provide both reliable prediction and actionable operational insight under realistic wastewater treatment plant 
conditions.
From a predictive perspective, the Random Forest model exhibited robust and reliable performance, achieving a test R2 
of 0.82 and an RMSE of 0.032 mg/L, corresponding to a prediction error within 20% of the regulatory discharge limit 
(0.2 mg/L). This level of performance indicates that the model can sufficiently distinguish normal operating conditions 
from potential compliance-risk scenarios on a daily decision-making horizon, where laboratory-based measurements and 
routine operational adjustments dominate.
Interpretability analysis using SHAP further revealed that influent T-P concentration and coagulant dosage are the domi­
nant drivers of effluent T-P variability, while short-term variation features and seasonal indicators modulate system response 
under dynamic loading and environmental conditions. The consistency of these feature contributions across seasons 
suggests that the learned relationships are physically consistent with established DAF process mechanisms, thereby enhan­
cing model transparency and supporting operator confidence.

Taken together, the results indicate that combining daily-scale prediction, explainable machine learning, and sensitivity-

control systems are limited, yet stable regulatory compliance and chemical efficiency are required. Future work will focus 
on field validation, integration with upstream–downstream process interactions, and extension toward digital-twin and 
reinforcement-learning–based operational control.
