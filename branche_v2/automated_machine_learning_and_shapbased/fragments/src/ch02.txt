1. Introduction
Perfluorooctanoic acid (PFOA), a legacy member of the per- and 
polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) family, is among the most persistent 
environmental pollutants identified to date. Its exceptional chemical 
stability, rooted in the strength of its carbon–fluorine bonds, has enabled 
widespread contamination of aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems [1,2]. 
PFOA’s bioaccumulation and its links to immunotoxicity, liver 
dysfunction, cardiovascular disease, and cancer have triggered esca­
lating regulatory scrutiny [3,4]. The International Agency for Research 
on Cancer now classifies PFOA as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1) [5], 
and the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency has set maximum 
contaminant levels as low as 4 ng/L in drinking water [6]. These de­
velopments have catalyzed an urgent search for effective, scalable, and 
sustainable remediation technologies capable of not just removing, but 
fully mineralizing PFOA into benign end products such as carbon di­
oxide and fluoride ions. This challenge mirrors a broader trajectory in 
wastewater treatment, where established processes are being optimized 
to more effectively degrade persistent industrial pollutants [7,8].
Electrochemical oxidation (EO) has emerged as one of the most 
promising destructive technologies for PFAS remediation, offering the 
potential to mineralize PFOA into benign end products such as carbon 
dioxide and fluoride ions [9,10]. The process operates through two 
principal degradation pathways: direct electron transfer at the anode 
surface, which generates perfluoroalkyl radicals and initiates 
chain-shortening reactions, and hydroxyl radical-mediated oxidation via 
water electrolysis [11,12]. However, optimizing EO systems presents 
formidable challenges due to the complex interplay of multiple opera­
tional parameters, including current density, electrode spacing, elec­
trolyte composition, pH, temperature, initial PFOA concentration, 
electrolysis time, and water matrix characteristics [13-15]. The high 
dimensionality and nonlinear interactions among these variables create 
a multifaceted optimization landscape where changes in one parameter 
can significantly influence the effectiveness of others, rendering

Machine learning (ML) approaches have demonstrated significant 
potential for addressing optimization challenges in electrochemical 
systems for water treatment applications [17,18]. Recent studies have 
successfully applied various ML algorithms to predict electrochemical 
process performance, with applications ranging from general electro­
chemical oxidation processes to specific PFAS treatment scenarios [19, 
20]. Advanced ML frameworks have shown particular promise in elec­
trochemical process optimization, with studies demonstrating the ability 
to achieve predictive accuracies exceeding R2 = 0.84 while providing 
mechanistic insights through feature importance analysis [17]. The 
integration of interpretability tools such as SHAP (SHapley Additive 
exPlanations) has enabled researchers to extract actionable insights 
from complex electrochemical datasets, revealing key parameter re­
lationships and guiding process optimization strategies [17,21]. How­
ever, most existing ML applications in this domain have relied on 
conventional modeling approaches requiring extensive manual hyper­
parameter tuning and expert-driven algorithm selection, limiting their 
accessibility and scalability for environmental practitioners [18,22,23].
Automated machine learning (AutoML) frameworks represent a 
transformative approach to data-driven process optimization by sys­
tematically exploring algorithm selection, hyperparameter optimiza­
tion, and feature engineering without human intervention [24-26]. 
FLAML (Fast Lightweight AutoML), developed by Microsoft Research, 
implements cost-aware Bayesian optimization that dynamically allo­
cates computational resources while prioritizing low-complexity models 
for rapid convergence [27]. Recent applications of AutoML in environ­
mental modeling have demonstrated the ability to significantly reduce 
computational cost while achieving predictive performance that sur­
passes traditional, manually tuned models [28,29]. For instance, 
gradient boosting algorithms selected via AutoML outperformed con­
ventional neural networks in predicting the impacts of microplastics on 
methane production in anaerobic digestion (MSE = 17.0 vs. 58.0) [29], 
while AutoML models also successfully captured complex antibiotic 
removal dynamics in constructed wetlands across varying training du­
rations (MAE = 9.94–13.68, R2 = 0.780–0.877) [28]. The integration of 
efficient AutoML frameworks such as FLAML with explainable AI tech­
niques like SHAP directly addresses two critical requirements of envi­
ronmental engineering applications: delivering high predictive accuracy 
and generating transparent, interpretable insights that support regula­
tory compliance, operational optimization, and stakeholder confidence 
[28,29].
In this study, a reproducible and interpretable AutoML workflow is 
developed for electrochemical oxidation of PFOA. The aim is to show 
that cost-aware automated model selection and tuning (FLAML), 
coupled with SHAP-based explanation, can deliver (i) reliable predictive 
performance, (ii) transparent, model-based attribution of operational 
drivers, and (iii) a portable pipeline that reduces manual trial-and-error. 
Use of a previously curated dataset enables direct comparability while 
isolating the methodological contribution (automation plus interpret­
ability). Throughout, FLAML is treated as an optimization framework 
distinct from the final predictive learner (XGBoost), and performance is 
evaluated under repeated holdout with statistical testing and learning- 
curve diagnostics to assess generalization.
