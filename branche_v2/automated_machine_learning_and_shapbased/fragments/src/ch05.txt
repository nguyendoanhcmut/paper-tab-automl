4. Conclusions
This study demonstrates a reproducible AutoML-based workflow for 
integrating automated machine learning with electrochemical oxidation 
processes targeting persistent environmental contaminants. The FLAML- 
driven XGBoost model outperformed conventional manually optimized 
ML approaches, delivering significantly enhanced predictive accuracy 
and interpretability, as evidenced by rigorous benchmarking and sta­
tistical validation. The analysis identified electrolysis time and anode 
material as primary operational drivers, offering actionable insights 
grounded in fundamental electrochemical principles and validated 
through SHAP-based interpretability. Crucially, the automated FLAML 
framework substantially reduced computational demands (72 % 
reduction in tuning time), thereby enhancing the accessibility and 
reproducibility of advanced ML techniques for environmental practi­
tioners. By demonstrating superior predictive power alongside scientif­
ically robust mechanistic insights, this research directly addresses 
critical methodological limitations in the current literature. The 
containerized, version-pinned pipeline ensures reproducibility and 
transparency, promoting wider adoption and methodological stan­
dardization within environmental remediation. Moving forward, inte­
grating the developed methodology with hybrid physics-based models, 
real-time adaptive process controls, and broader PFAS compound sce­
narios can further elevate electrochemical treatment efficacy and
