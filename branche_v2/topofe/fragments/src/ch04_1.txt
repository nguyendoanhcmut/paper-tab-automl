3.1.1
Multi-island Decomposition
The feature program space P is not homogeneous: programs naturally cluster into semantically coherent regions
according to the class of operations they employ, each encoding a distinct inductive bias about how predictive signal is
constructed from raw features.
Definition 1 (Transformation Family). A transformation family Pi ⊆P is a semantically coherent subset of feature
programs sharing the same inductive bias about how predictive signal is constructed from raw features, i.e., programs
that apply the same class of primitive operations.
We consider five canonical transformation families: arithmetic interactions P1 (products, ratios, and differences),
statistical aggregates P2 (group-level means, standard deviations, counts, and medians), temporal features P3 (lags,
rolling windows, and exponential moving averages), relational encodings P4 (frequency counts, target encodings,
and rank-based transforms), and nonlinear univariate maps P5 (logarithmic, exponential, binned, and polynomial
transformations). These families may overlap (i.e., Pi ∩Pj̸ = ∅in general) but each represents a structurally distinct
region of P. The partition is also extensible to additional transformation families as needed. Together, they induce an
approximate cover P ≈SM
i=1 Pi, which decomposes the monolithic search space into M semantically typed subspaces,
each amenable to specialized search.
However, in a single evolutionary population, high-scoring programs from a dominant family rapidly saturate the
experience buffer, confining subsequent LLM proposals to that family’s neighbourhood and leaving orthogonal families
permanently unexplored. TOPOFE addresses this through a structured multi-island decomposition that assigns a
dedicated subpopulation to each Pi, preserving family-specific inductive biases and preventing any single family from
monopolizing the search budget.
