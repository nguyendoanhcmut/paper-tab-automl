Related Work

6.1
Automatic Feature Engineering
Automated feature engineering (AutoFE) has evolved through three broad paradigms. Search-based methods treat
feature construction as a combinatorial optimization problem, through genetic programming [Olson and Moore, 2016],
exhaustive [Horn et al., 2019] and greedy search [Zhang et al., 2023a]. These methods are computationally expensive
and domain-agnostic without semantic guidance. LLM-guided methods use pretrained LMs to propose features [Han
et al., 2024, Ko et al., 2025, Nam et al., 2024] from task descriptions and dataset metadata [Hollmann et al., 2023a].
Recent extensions apply Tree-of-Thought reasoning [Zhang et al., 2025], multi-agent frameworks [Ouyang et al., 2025],
and ReAct-based agents [Burghardt et al., 2026] to guide feature discovery. However, these methods reply on prompting,
leading to repeated proposals, no dataset-specific adaptation, and no cross-family composition. LLM-evolutionary
methods combine LLM-based generation with evolutionary search [Abhyankar et al., 2025, Gong et al., 2025, Batista,
2025]. While these methods improve over static prompting, they operate over a single population without structural
decomposition of the program space, precluding cross-family hybridization and leaving the search vulnerable to
premature convergence within a dominant transformation family. TOPOFE addresses these limitations directly through
multi-island decomposition, prompt adaptation memory, and topology-guided cross-family transfer.

6.2
LLMs for Tabular Data Learning
Recent advances in tabular learning have converged toward two main paradigms. Tabular foundation models pursue
cross-table transferability through large-scale pretraining, ranging from Bayesian inference approximation via Prior-
Data Fitted Networks [Hollmann et al., 2023b, 2025] and architecture-level scaling for in-context learning [Gardner
et al., 2024, Arazi et al., 2026, Qu et al., 2025], to retrieval-augmented inference without task-specific fine-tuning [Ma
et al., 2025]. LLM-based tabular methods repurpose pretrained LMs for few-shot prediction [Hegselmann et al., 2023,
Dinh et al., 2022], semantic generalization via textual metadata [Ye et al., 2024a, Kim et al., 2024, Yan et al.], and
auxiliary tasks including data augmentation [Zhang et al., 2023b, Borisov et al.], data cleaning [Bendinelli et al.],
and feature generation [Hollmann et al., 2023a]. Despite these advances, existing approaches largely rely on per-task
adaptation and lack principled search mechanisms over the feature space, which TOPOFE is specifically designed to
provide.

6.3
LLM-guided Evolutionary Search
A growing body of work integrates LLMs as evolutionary operators across prompt optimization [Guo et al., 2023,
Fernando et al., Suzgun et al., 2026, Agrawal et al.], program and algorithm discovery [Šurina et al., Assumpção et al.,
2025, Novikov et al., 2025, Sharma, Lange et al., 2025], and scientific discovery [Shojaee et al., Yang et al., 2025b,
Chen et al., 2026, Abhyankar et al., 2026]. These systems vary primarily in population dynamics, employing MAP-
Elites [Novikov et al., 2025], island-based evolution [Assumpção et al., 2025], Pareto frontier optimization [Agrawal
et al.], and diversity-driven selection [Sharma], with more advanced frameworks incorporating co-evolution and
meta-reflection [Liu et al., Ye et al., 2024b] or iterative agent evolution [Yuan et al., 2025, Shang et al.]. Despite
their effectiveness, existing methods treat the search space as structurally uniform and do not incorporate semantic
decomposition, task-adaptive proposal conditioning, or saturation-triggered transfer, all of which are central to TOPOFE.
