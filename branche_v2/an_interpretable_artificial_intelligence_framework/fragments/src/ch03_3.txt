2.3. Data preprocessing and standardisation
Following quality control, input features and output targets were 
standardised using z-score normalization to ensure that all variables 
contributed equally to model training, regardless of their original 
measurement scales. The visualization and distribution of features 
before and after normalization are shown in Fig. 2.
Standardisation parameters were computed exclusively from the 
training partition and applied to the test set to prevent information 
leakage; the formulation is given in Supplementary Section S2. The step 
also clarifies the structure of the record. The time series of Flow, TMP 
and Level show substantial short-term variability together with slower 
drift, so the system exhibits both gradual operational movement and 
abrupt transitions across all three outputs (Fig. 2A). Standardisation 
preserves the shape of each empirical distribution, including skewness, 
multimodality and tail behaviour (Fig. 2B), while placing variables

The Pearson correlation matrices before and after normalization 
confirm that z-scoring leaves the linear relationships among variables 
unchanged (Fig. 3), preserving dependencies such as the negative as­
sociation between TMP and Flow, the coupling between Level and 
loading-related variables, and the hydraulic relationships involving HRT 
and SRT that jointly constrain Flow and Level. The correlations 
involving Level emphasise its role as an integrative state variable 
reflecting both hydraulic conditions and biomass accumulation rather 
than a purely independent target.
