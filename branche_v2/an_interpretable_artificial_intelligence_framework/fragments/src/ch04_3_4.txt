3.3.4. Feature importance shifts and real-time control implications
Confining the SHAP analysis to the feasible region changes several 
importance rankings in ways that carry operational meaning 
(Tables S5–S7). These shifts are not artefacts: within the viable regime 
the most damaging conditions, namely SRT above 100 days, MLSS above 
7500 mg/L and HRT above 10 h, are excluded by the output constraints, 
so shorter-timescale sources of variability become dominant. The 
clearest case is F/M, whose importance for water level rises by 87% from 
rank 5 to rank 4. In the full dataset, extreme SRT and high MLSS 
dominate level variance and mask the short-timescale contribution of 
organic loading; within the feasible region, where both are moderate, F/

The recommendations in Table 4 do not rest on a single analytical 
method. Each is supported by the cross-model SHAP directional evi­
dence of Section 3.2 across all 16 models, by the conditional feasibility 
surface of the manifold-constrained sample, and by the measured 
attainment rate in the plant's own record, with the primary levers 
additionally validated out of time. This convergence of model-based and 
measurement-based evidence, rather than the use of any one technique, 
is what distinguishes the present protocol from previous MBR optimi­
sation studies, which typically report either importance rankings, or 
directional effects, or a feasible range, but do not reconcile all three 
against what the plant has actually achieved.
