Conclusions
This study successfully developed and compared computational intelligence models to predict and optimize the 
removal of Ammonia Nitrogen and Phosphate in a microalgae membrane bioreactor (MBR). The comparative 
analysis demonstrated that the Artificial Neural Network (ANN) model significantly outperformed the Response 
Surface Methodology (RSM), achieving an R2 > 0.99 and lower RMSE values for all target variables. This confirms 
that the non-linear architecture of ANN is superior for capturing the complex biological dynamics of wastewater 
treatment compared to the polynomial limitations of RSM.

Novelty and contribution
The primary novelty of this work lies in the integrated application of ANN, RSM, and NSGA-II to form a holistic 
decision-support framework. Unlike prior studies that focused on single-objective optimization, this research 
utilized NSGA-II to generate a Pareto optimal front, revealing the critical operational trade-offs between 
retention time and nutrient removal efficiency. This framework empowers plant operators to select flexible 
operating conditions—such as a pH range of 7.5–8.5 and dynamic HRT—that balance strict environmental 
compliance with operational feasibility.

Future research directions
Future work should focus on two key areas. First, a “human-in-the-loop” experimental validation is 
recommended, where the specific setpoints identified by the Pareto front are tested in a pilot-scale MBR to verify 
the predicted removal efficiencies. Second, there is significant potential to integrate this AI framework with real-
time monitoring systems. By coupling these predictive models with IoT sensors, future research could develop 
an automated feedback control system that dynamically adjusts pH and retention time in response to fluctuating 
influent loads, moving towards truly smart wastewater management.
