The recovery of clean water, energy, and nutrients from wastewater is a crucial part of a circular economy. 
Hydrolytic microorganisms can be found in interesting places in the natural world, like the stomach cavities 
of ruminant animals. Inorganic nitrogen, phosphorus, organic pollutants, and other pollutants are the main 
contaminants found in wastewater. Wastewater mainly contains inorganic nitrogen, phosphorous, organic 
pollutants, and other toxins1,2. Eutrophication will result if the waste is dumped directly into the water3. 
Furthermore, quite poisonous to ammonia and nitrite are aquatic life. There have been developed several 
methods for treating wastewater4–6. Environmentally friendly and more cost-effective, biological methods are 
preferred for treating wastewater. Recently, much focus has been placed on using microalgae-bacterial systems 
to treat domestic and industrial wastewater. In tropical and subtropical regions, they are more frequently used as 
an alternative biosystem. For more than 70 years, sewage has been treated with microalgae to recover nitrogen 
and phosphorus7–10.

Several studies have reported applying ANN, RSM, machine learning, and multi-objective optimization in 
various computational and experimental research studies14–19. The abovementioned techniques have been used 
for process and procedure modeling, prediction, and optimization. In this regard, Viet et al.20 developed an 
ANN method to model the osmotic membrane bioreactor. The reported models displayed solid performance 
in their testing. The R2 values for the membrane fouling simulations and water flux prediction were equal to 
0.93 and 0.92, respectively. Also, the obtained TOC removal model showed high efficiency, with R2 = 0.98. 
Mojiri et al.21 improved how microalgal photobioreactors removed the contamination in different research. The 
reactors’ performance was improved using an ANN, as shown by their low MSE and high R2 values (> 0.99). The 
toxicity test findings showed that longer contact durations (> 48 h) and higher pollutant concentrations (> 10 
mg/L) reduced the chlorophyll and protein contents of microalgae. In a different research, Taheri et al.22 used 
ANN to forecast significant variables influencing anaerobic membrane bioreactor-sequencing batch reactors. 
The prediction performance of the four-member Gauss membership function was shown to be superior. 
A nine-neuron Levenberg-Marquardt training process in the hidden layer was the ideal structure; a back-
propagation technique was also used in the feed-forward training of the ANN model. To better understand the 
phenomena of membrane fouling and improve the fouling parameters in a submerged membrane bioreactor, 
Alipourzadeh and colleagues23 used RSM. The CCD model was reported to be the best for predicting essential 
factors. The results showed that using RSM was successful for this purpose. In Sultana’s study24, using microalgae 
as a biosorbent, the RSM method was used to estimate optimum parameters for removing heavy metals. The 
reported hybrid RSM-CSA approach offered the globally optimal solution, and its outcomes were comparable 
to those achieved by the RSM-DF approach. Experiments in such environments validated the model’s predicted 
ideal circumstances’ consistency. Under ideal circumstances, the experimental removal efficacy (97%) was very 
close to the model-predicted value (with less than a 5% error). Membrane fouling in a membrane bioreactor was 
modeled by Chen et al.25 using back propagation (BP) and general regression neural network (GRNN) ANN. 
The well-known GRNN model was said to have more accuracy, while the back-propagation neural network 
(BPNN) models showed good regression coefficients and accuracies. This implies that ANN models may well 
capture the intricate non-linear mapping relationships between interfacial energy and other parameters. The 
BP ANN and the GRNN have much greater quantification efficiency than the extended Derjaguin–Landau–
Verwey–Overbeek (XDLVO) approach. However, the BP ANN outperformed the GRNN model regarding 
prediction accuracy. The integration of AI with diverse optimization strategies enables predictive models to 
yield the optimal combination of potential inputs. Numerous researchers have used the ANN methodology in 
prior engine-related studies to reduce operational complexity and achieve significant outcomes. Moreover, the 
RSM approach is used to provide accurate experimental data on the performance and emission characteristics of 
wastewater treatments in recent years. The literature survey highlights the prevalent use of data-driven models 
for forecasting wastewater treatment processes. However, distinct research gaps remain. First, most prior studies 
have relied on a single modeling approach—either RSM or ANN—in isolation. There is a lack of comprehensive 
comparative studies that rigorously evaluate both linear (RSM) and non-linear (ANN) methodologies on 
identical datasets to determine which is more suitable for the complex fluctuations of microalgae MBR systems.
Second, and more critically, previous optimization efforts have predominantly focused on single-objective 
techniques. These approaches often fail to capture the conflicting nature of bioreactor performance metrics, 
where maximizing the removal of one contaminant may inadvertently compromise another or require excessive 
retention times. There is a limited application of Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms, specifically the 
Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II), in this domain. This study addresses these gaps by 
establishing a holistic framework that not only compares the predictive accuracy of RSM and ANN but also 
integrates them with NSGA-II to generate a Pareto optimal front, providing a set of optimal trade-off solutions 
for simultaneous Ammonia Nitrogen and Phosphate removal.
This work introduces an innovative method for forecasting and enhancing the elimination of ammonia 
nitrogen, phosphate concentration, and TOC in a microalgae membrane bioreactor. This is achieved through 
the utilization of RSM and ANN modeling techniques. The present work intends to forecast and maximize in 
a microalgae membrane bioreactor ammonia nitrogen, phosphate concentration, and their removal efficiency. 
It has made use of Ding et al.‘s26 experimental data. TOC concentration, time, and pH are the independent 
variables applied for this work. RMS and ANN modeling have been used to estimate ammonia nitrogen and 
phosphate concentration and their removal efficiency behavior. Finally, the RSM and NSGA-II approaches 
helped maximize the given elements to identify the best processing mode. This paper presents a novel approach 
for microalgae membrane bioreactor ammonia nitrogen, phosphate concentration, and TOC forecasting and 
enhancement method. RSM and ANN modeling approaches help one to accomplish this. Important parameters 
influencing the efficiency of nutrient removal in a microalgae membrane bioreactor are TOC and pH, which 
the model demonstrates mastery in controlling time-varying variables. Furthermore, using RSM and NSGA-II 
in the optimization process demonstrated the model’s ability to accurately pinpoint the ideal circumstances for 
removing nutrients from the bioreactor.
