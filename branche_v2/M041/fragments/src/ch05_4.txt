Multi-objective optimization setup via NSGA-II
To determine the optimal operating conditions that simultaneously maximize nutrient removal, the Non-
dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) was utilized. Unlike single-objective optimization, NSGA-
II generates a set of optimal solutions known as the Pareto Front, representing the best trade-offs between 
conflicting objectives.
Problem Formulation:
The optimization problem was defined with three decision variables and two objective functions:
•	 Decision Variables: The search space was constrained by the experimental design boundaries:
	– 0 ≤ Time ≤ 20 (days).
	– 6 ≤ pH ≤ 9.
	– 40 ≤ TOC ≤ 120 (mg/L).
•	 Objective Functions: The regression equations derived from the RSM model (Eqs. 2 and 6 for Ammonia Ni­
trogen removal; Eqs. 4 and 8 for Phosphate removal) served as the fitness functions.
	– Objective 1: Maximize Ammonia Nitrogen Removal Efficiency (%).
	– Objective 2: Maximize Phosphate Removal Efficiency (%).
Algorithm Parameters:
The NSGA-II configuration was set as follows to ensure robust convergence:
•	 Population Size: 100.
•	 Maximum Generations: 200.
•	 Crossover Probability: 0.9.
•	 Mutation Probability: 0.1.
•	 Selection Operator: Tournament Selection.
