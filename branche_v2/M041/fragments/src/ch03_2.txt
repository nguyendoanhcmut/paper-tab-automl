ANN modeling results
This section used experimental data from Ding et al.26 on ammonia nitrogen, phosphate concentration, and 
removal efficiency as dependent factors. Time, pH, and TOC concentration were independent variables for 
ANN modeling. Table 1 shows the ANN with each model’s best structure and accuracy parameters. The optimal 
topology for the ANN models was determined through a trial-and-error procedure. The number of neurons 
in the hidden layer was iteratively varied from 1 to 20, and the architecture yielding the lowest MSE on the 
validation set was selected. The resulting best-fit architectures for each variable are summarized in Table 1.

Addressing model robustness and overfitting
Given the limited size of the experimental dataset (44 points), specific measures were implemented to ensure 
model interpretability and prevent overfitting. The primary mitigation strategy was the application of Early 
Stopping regularization. During the iterative training process, the MSE of the validation subset (15% of data) was 
continuously monitored. The training algorithm was configured to halt automatically if the validation error did 
not improve for six consecutive epochs. This mechanism ensures that the network learns the underlying general 
trends rather than memorizing the noise in the training subset.
Furthermore, the Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) backpropagation algorithm was selected for weight 
optimization. The LM algorithm is widely recognized for its damping parameter (µ), which naturally provides 
regularization by alternating between the Gradient Descent and Gauss-Newton methods, making it highly stable 
for smaller datasets1. The consistency of the R2 values across the Training (R2 > 0.99), Validation (R2 > 0.99), and 
Testing (R2 > 0.9) datasets, as detailed in Table 1 and visualized in Fig. 6, confirms that the model has achieved 
strong generalization capabilities on unseen data.
