NSGA-II optimization
Figure 10a,b, show a Pareto Front of 1/Removal Efficiency versus Ammonia Nitrogen Concentration based on the 
ANN models. The graph demonstrates how removal efficiency diminishes as ammonia nitrogen concentrations 
rise. This is so that the ANN model can be optimized for the lowest ammonia nitrogen concentration while 
achieving the best removal efficiency. According to the graph, the ideal ammonia nitrogen concentration is 
around 1.932 mg/L, with an 87% elimination effectiveness. In order to attain the best removal efficiency with the 
lowest ammonia nitrogen content, the RSM model is optimized with NSGA-II. According to the graph, the ideal 
ammonia nitrogen concentration is around 1.722 mg/L, with an 87% elimination effectiveness.
Figure 10c,d, depict a Pareto Front of 1/Removal Efficiency versus Phosphate Concentration based on the 
RSM models. The graph demonstrates that removal efficiency reduces as phosphate concentrations rise. In order 
to attain the best removal efficiency with the lowest phosphate content, the ANN model is tuned with NSGA-II. 
According to the graph, a phosphate concentration of 9.5 mg/L with a removal efficiency of 99% is ideal. In order 
to attain the best removal efficiency with the lowest phosphate content, optimize the RSM model with NSGA-II. 
According to the graph, a phosphate content of 0.002 mg/L with a clearance efficiency of 99% is ideal.
