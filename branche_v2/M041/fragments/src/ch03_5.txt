Practical implications and environmental relevance
The optimization results obtained from the ANN-NSGA-II framework provide critical insights for the scale-
up and operation of microalgae MBRs. The model identified the optimal pH range for nutrient removal to be 
between 7.5 and 8.5. From a practical engineering perspective, this is a highly favorable operational window. 
Microalgae cultivation naturally tends to elevate the pH of the mixed liquor due to the consumption of dissolved 
CO2 during photosynthesis. Consequently, maintaining the bioreactor within this slightly alkaline range 
requires minimal addition of buffering chemicals (acids or bases), thereby significantly reducing the Operational 
Expenditure (OPEX) and preventing secondary chemical contamination of the effluent.
Furthermore, the analysis of the “Time” variable, representing the HRT, reveals a critical operational trade-
off. The model predicts that maximizing removal efficiency (> 90%) requires retention times exceeding 12 
days. While this ensures compliance with strict environmental discharge standards for sensitive water bodies 
(preventing eutrophication), it implies the need for larger reactor volumes, which increases Capital Expenditure 
(CAPEX).
This is where the multi-objective optimization (Pareto front) proves valuable for plant operators. Instead 
of adhering to a single rigid setpoint, operators can utilize the model to dynamically adjust the process. For 
instance, during periods of peak hydraulic load, the facility could opt for a “non-dominated” solution with a 
shorter retention time (e.g., 8–10 days) that still maintains acceptable removal limits, rather than forcing the 
maximum theoretical removal that would bottle-neck the system. This adaptability validates the proposed AI 
framework not just as a theoretical exercise, but as a robust decision-support tool for sustainable wastewater 
management.
