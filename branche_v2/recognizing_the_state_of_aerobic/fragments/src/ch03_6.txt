The prediction pattern was utilized to predict a new image using the 
trained model, which was loaded from a checkpoint file and provided 
with a fresh image for inference. To assess the actual predictive per­
formance of this model and validate its generalization capability, the 
AGS images throughout their entire life-cycle were collected from 
another MBR and categorized into seven batches of more than 50 images 
each, resulting in 362 valid data points for analysis. Subsequently, the 
acquired microscopic images were predicted by setting an Intersection 
over Union (IoU) threshold of 0.7 to select the best detection outcome in 
the candidate boxes with considerable overlap, and these results were 
then saved in a designated directory. Additionally, global variables were 
defined to record the number of categories predicted for each input 
image, while batch statistics on the accumulated number of categories 
across iterations were used as functions. By implementing intelligent 
identification and automatic statistical functions, the role of this model 
in practical application scenarios has been significantly enriched, and 
the monitoring efficiency over the entire life-cycle of AGS can be greatly 
improved.
