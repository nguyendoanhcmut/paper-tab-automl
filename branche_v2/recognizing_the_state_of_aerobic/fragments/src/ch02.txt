Aerobic granular sludge (AGS), characterized by its unique structure 
and high biomass density, offers significant advantages in the simulta­
neous removal of nutrients and organic pollutants within a single 
bioreactor, potentially surpassing the conventional activated sludge 
(CAS) process in terms of efficiency and cost-effectiveness (Bengtsson 
et al., 2019; Hu et al., 2024). The long-term stability of AGS is pivotal to 
sustaining these benefits, while the sequencing batch reactor (SBR) in 
batch mode has been the most successful method for AGS cultivation 
(Han et al., 2024), there is a burgeoning interest in continuous mode 
cultivation, especially when AGS is integrated with membrane bio­
reactors (MBRs). MBRs, which combine CAS with membrane filtration, 
are known for producing high-quality effluent and reducing wasted 
activated sludge (WAS) (Rahman et al., 2023). However, conventional 
MBRs often face issues with membrane fouling due to the deposition of 
flocculated activated sludge particles on the membrane surface (Morales 
et al., 2024). In contrast, AGS, with its aggregate form, has demonstrated 
an effective mitigation of membrane fouling, which is crucial for 
enhancing the practicality of MBR applications (Campo et al., 2021). 
Therefore, the AGS-MBR system stands out as an efficient, economical, 
and sustainable wastewater treatment technology that not only mini­
mizes membrane fouling but also ensures superior effluent quality and 
reduced WAS production.
In AGS-MBR system, the stability of AGS is paramount, with 
morphological integrity and structure being key to operational effi­
ciency. The AGS life-cycle, encompassing initial, growth, mature, and 
cleaved stages, significantly influences bioreactor performance (Yang

and size distribution, is essential for effective process control (de Sousa 
Rollemberg et al., 2018). Then, operational parameters like influent 
composition and temperature can induce variability in AGS, potentially 
leading to a transition from granular to flocculated sludge and system 
instability (Iorhemen and Liu, 2021). The self-cultivation of AGS in 
continuous flow MBR system has gained interest due to operational 
convenience and enhanced performance. However, the heterogeneity of 
AGS, with varying physicochemical, microbiological, and morpholog­
ical properties, remains a challenge (Yang et al., 2023). In the initial 
stage, the sludge particles are small; in the growth stage, the sludge 
particles have rough edges; in the mature stage, the sludge particles have 
a stable structure and smooth surface; in the cleaved stage, the sludge 
particles age, with a decrease in large particles and an increase in small 
ones. The characteristics of each stage are interrelated, reflecting the 
dynamic changes of AGS from formation to aging. Understanding the 
differences and features of these life-cycles is of great significance for 
optimizing the operation and management of AGS-MBR system, which 
can help us better control and adjust operational conditions to maintain 
the stability of AGS and improve the efficiency of wastewater treatment. 
In addition, this information also provides an important reference for 
the design of new bioreactors or the improvement of existing 
technologies.
Accurate state identification of AGS is crucial, yet methods like 
frozen section analysis, dissolved oxygen (DO) micro-electrodes, and 
CLSM have limitations. Invasive techniques can disrupt the internal 
micro-environment, while CLSM requires laborious sample preparation. 
These approaches may also be sensitive to AGS properties, leading to 
inaccuracies and a lack of reproducibility (Kirkland et al., 2020; Li et al., 
2014; Ranzinger et al., 2020). This underscores the uniqueness of AGS in 
MBR operations and the necessity for advanced diagnostic tools for its 
state assessment, which is vital for optimizing the performance of 
AGS-MBR system. The development of intelligent, non-invasive methods 
to analyze AGS states in real-time is therefore imperative, ensuring the 
long-term stability and efficiency of these system.
Artificial intelligence (AI) has demonstrated its prowess in waste­
water treatment modeling by offering enhanced learning capabilities, 
automation, and adaptability beyond traditional methods (Alvi et al., 
2023; Narayanan et al., 2024). AI-driven models can reveal complex 
data relationships and adapt to new data, enhancing understanding of 
wastewater treatment processes and aiding in prediction and control 
(Ignacz et al., 2025; P´erez-Beltr´an et al., 2024). Within the context of 
AGS system, artificial neural networks (ANNs) have been effectively 
utilized to predict critical process outcomes (Zhong et al., 2021), such as 
the removal rates of chemical oxygen demand, ammonia nitrogen, total 
nitrogen, and total phosphorus (Zaghloul and Achari, 2022). They have 
also been employed to predict sludge properties and water quality based 
on historical reactor data, enhancing the predictive accuracy and control 
of AGS processes (Mahmod and Wahab, 2017; Zaghloul et al., 2021). 
Despite these advancements, there is a significant gap in the application 
of AI for the image recognition and classification of aerobic granular 
sludge (AGS). You Only Look Once (YOLO) is a highly influential AI 
technology that, based on a deep learning framework, can efficiently 
identify the positions and categories of objects in images, demonstrating 
strong real-time performance and accuracy (Redmon et al., 2016). The 
YOLO algorithm has not only been widely applied in multiple fields such 
as agricultural disease monitoring (Gao et al., 2024), medical image 
diagnosis (Xu et al., 2025), infrastructure inspection (Santos and Car­
valho, 2025), and autonomous driving (Ul Islam et al., 2025), but also 
provides strong support for machine learning in target recognition tasks 
in complex scenarios due to its efficiency and adaptability. It is an 
indispensable excellent tool in the field of image recognition. Perhaps 
the YOLO algorithm could be a new approach to achieving the image 
recognition and classification of AGS.
Based on the aforementioned background, this study aimed to 
develop a machine learning approach for accurately identifying the 
states of AGS in a continuous-flow MBR over its entire life-cycle using 
the YOLOv8 object detection model. The model was trained following 
dataset screening, classification, and labeling, and then underwent 
hyperparameter tuning after pre-processing. Various evaluation metrics 
were calculated on the test set to quantify the model’s predictive 
effectiveness, guiding the optimization of model parameters based on 
key indices. Additionally, dimensionality reduction techniques were 
applied to the semantic features of all AGS images in the test set, pro­
jecting them into two-dimensional visualizations. Feature clustering 
analysis was conducted across different categories, and interpretability 
analysis was performed to identify key features within images, eluci­
dating differences and connections among them. Lastly, to facilitate real- 
world applications and enhance detection efficiency, a batch counting 
function was developed for each AGS stage, aiming to provide intelligent 
methods for obtaining timely statistical data to aid in the management 
and optimization of AGS-MBR processes.
