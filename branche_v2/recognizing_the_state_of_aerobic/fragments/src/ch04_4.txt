The t-SNE technique, a machine learning method for visualizing and 
reducing the dimensionality of data, excels in preserving the integrity of 
relationships and structures in high dimensional data when it is pro­
jected onto a lower dimensional space (Pouyet et al., 2018). This tech­
nique is commonly employed to visually explore complex datasets and 
uncover patterns or clusters that may not be immediately discernible in 
the original high-dimensional space (Silva and Melo-Pinto, 2023). In this 
section, it was used to analyze the clusters for all classes with the results 
shown in Fig. 6.
As shown in Fig. 6, the features of AGS in the test set across its four 
different life-cycle stages were simplified to two dimensions and pre­
sented as four clusters. Overall, all types exhibited independent spatial 
distributions with low levels of coupling, indicating that the experi­
mental classification criteria for the AGS life-cycle were reasonable. 
Specifically, CS showed the tightest distribution and a high degree of 
cohesion, suggesting a consistent pattern of morphological features. In 
contrast, GS exhibited the most dispersed distribution and showed 
specific coupling relationships with the other three categories. This 
implied that AGS, at various stages of development, diversified in 
appearance due to a variety of external conditions and internal devel­
opmental laws, leading to changes from light to dark colors and from 
rough to smooth edges. The corresponding complexity results confirmed 
that each class in the confusion matrix was predicted as GS. On the other 
hand, IS and MS only showed certain coupling relationships with adja­
cent life-cycle categories while maintaining similar morphological fea­
tures within their respective clusters. This distribution pattern indicated 
that, despite certain interactions between adjacent stages, each stage 
retained unique characteristics identifiable through cluster analysis. The 
spatial independence and specific coupling patterns provided valuable 
insights into the dynamics of AGS development and confirmed the 
robustness of the classification method used in this study.
