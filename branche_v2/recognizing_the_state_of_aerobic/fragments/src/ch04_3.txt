The precision rate measures the accuracy of a model’s predictions for 
positive samples, that is, the proportion of true positives among the 
predicted positive samples. The recall rate reflects the model’s ability to 
identify actual positive samples, which is the proportion of true positives 
among the actual positive samples. There is a trade-off between preci­
sion and recall; increasing precision may decrease recall, and vice versa. 
The confidence threshold is used to adjust this trade-off by setting a

When the confidence interval of IS and MS was 0.4–0.6, the precision 
gradually reached 1, while, the corresponding precision for GS and CS 
also gradually reached 1 for confidence interval of 0.8–1.0. Among 
them, IS showed the fastest growth rate, which indicated lower false 
detection rates for IS and MS features, making them easier to be accu­
rately recognized by the model. In the confidence interval of 0.8–1.0, 
compared to GS and CS, IS was found to be more distinguishable from 
other AGS stages, indicating a lower false detection rate. This observa­
tion aligns with the fact that IS and MS represent relatively smaller- 
grained AGS targets, which are more susceptible to background inter­
ference and partial occlusion in images (Qian et al., 2022). For IS and 
MS, a precision of 1 could be achieved even with a confidence level of 
0.4–0.6, suggesting that under certain specific conditions, models can 
still yield accurate detection results even with lower confidence levels. 
This highlights the importance of not solely relying on confidence levels 
to assess detection performance in practical applications, but rather 
integrating other metrics and actual scenarios for a comprehensive 
evaluation. However, when the confidence threshold was set at 0.942, 
all classes achieved the maximum value of 1 (Fig. 5(a)). As recall is 
generally negatively correlated with confidence levels, setting a 
threshold of 0 resulted in the maximum average recall for all classes 
(Fig. 5(b)). The smaller granule size and dense distribution of IS in im­
ages led to a higher recall rate for detecting smaller AGS granules when 
using low confidence intervals. When the confidence interval was set 
between 0.8 and 1.0, MS and CS had higher recall rates than IS and GS, 
with IS showing a significant decline. MS and CS, with their larger size 
and distinct features, resisted background noise and occlusion better, 
leading to higher recall rates. Conversely, IS had higher recall rates at 
lower confidence levels due to less stringent detection criteria, allowing 
more potential targets to be identified. However, at high confidence 
levels, IS’s recall rate dropped significantly, indicating the model’s 
limitation in recognizing smaller-grained IS targets, which are more 
affected by background interference and occlusion. This highlighted the 
need to balance the confidence threshold. Setting it too high increases 
false negatives, especially for small targets, while setting it too low in­
creases false positives (Thatikonda et al., 2024). Thus, finding a balance 
between recall and false positive rates is crucial to optimize the model’s 
performance. In summary, the higher recall rates for MS and CS at high 
confidence levels revealed the different characteristics of targets with 
varying granularities, while the significant drop in IS’s recall rate 
emphasized the importance of balancing the confidence threshold in 
practical applications.
In this study, we determined that the confidence threshold should be 
set at 0.671. Setting the confidence threshold at 0.671 means that only 
predictions with a confidence level of 67.1% or higher were considered 
as positive samples. This threshold was used to balance precision and 
recall, ensuring that the model’s predictions were both reliable and 
capable of capturing a sufficient number of actual positive instances. 
The average F1 score of 0.95 indicated that the model performed well in 
minimizing false positives and false negatives (Fig. 5(c)), thereby 
achieving a high level of overall performance. Therefore, it is recom­
mended to use a confidence threshold of 0.671 when making predictions 
on new images to ensure optimal prediction accuracy.
