Two parallel 10 L MBRs were inoculated with activated sludge sampled from an Anaerobic-Anoxic-Oxic wastewater treatment 
plant (Incheon, Korea) and operated for 235 days. Each contained two 0.56-μm SiC flat-sheet membranes (0.165 m² surface area; 
Fig. 1). Continuous aeration maintained DO at 5.1 ± 2.2 mg L⁻¹ . Greywater feeding and permeate extraction were conducted 
continuously, with timer-controlled relaxation (0.06 L min⁻¹). The operational strategy for the two parallel MBRs involved three 
phases of adjusted net flux and aeration to enhance greywater treatment, particularly ammonia removal (Fig. 1). Initially low ammonia 
removal under a flux of 2.9 L/(m²⋅h) and aeration of 2.0 L/min prompted an increase in aeration to 6.0 L/min in Phase 2 to improve 
nitrification efficiency. Phase 3 involved an increase in flux to 6.9 L/(m²⋅h) to further test hydraulic stability. Biocarriers (10 % 
packing ratio) were added to MBR-2 in phase 3 to try to reduce membrane fouling under high flux and promote anoxic zones for 
enhancing simultaneous nitrification-denitrification, enabling comparative performance evaluation with MBR-1. The 10 % packing 
ratio was selected to balance fouling control with operational safety, avoiding abrasion and energy issues associated with higher loads 
(Noor et al., 2023; Rahman et al., 2023). These intentional variations in flux, aeration, and biocarriers addition diversified operational 
conditions, enriching the dataset to enhance the machine learning model’s generalizability across multiple scenarios.
Influent, effluent and reactor water quality were analyzed daily as described in supplementary information (SI) 1.
