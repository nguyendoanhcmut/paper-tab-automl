5. Conclusions
This work demonstrates the viability of interpretable ML-based models (LR, RF, XGB) for monitoring MBR nitrification by using six 
sensor-compatible features: airflow, influent flow, TMP, and effluent concentrations of COD, NO₃⁻-N, and NH₄⁺-N. Strategic feature 
selection enabled robust classification (precision >0.85 for LR/XGB). RF excelled in cross-scenario testing (precision=0.87) for pre­
dicting biocarrier operations using training data from non-biocarrier systems, despite a decline in FPR due to feature distribution shifts. 
All models maintained TPR ≥0.80 and accuracy > 0.75 in cross-scenario testing, though limited data constrained stability. Results 
emphasized ML’s potential to enhance MBR control by balancing precision (minimizing FPs) with transferability across configurations. 
Post hoc analysis (feature importance, KDE, pairwise plots, and SHAP analysis) confirmed effluent NO₃⁻-N and NH₄⁺-N as dominant 
predictors. Wide 95 % confidence intervals reflected instability from small dataset scope.
Future research should prioritize systematic data acquisition and model training across diverse influent water quality and DO 
regimes. Concurrently, expanding boundary conditions—specifically targeted collection under high NO₃⁻-N/low NH₄⁺-N conditions is 
essential to optimize model architecture. This work establishes a framework for deploying interpretable data-driven models in 
decentralized greywater reuse, advancing intelligent MBR control while addressing transferability barriers in real-world 
implementations.
