4.1. Data limitation and data collection
Data scarcity remains a fundamental constraint in developing ML models for environmental applications, particularly when 
adapting pre-trained models to novel operational scenarios through transfer learning (Dansawad et al., 2023; Imen et al., 2023; Lowe 
et al., 2022; Zhang, 2022; Zhu et al., 2022). This dataset—collected from experimental operations instead of being designed for ML 
development—limited model evaluation despite > 0.8 test accuracy. Larger datasets spanning diverse operation conditions are 
essential for robust decision boundaries, particularly when distinguishing transient process states during simultaneous 
nitrification-denitrification.
In addition to the prediction overfitting due to this smaller dataset discussed in Section 3.3, data constraints impact optimization 
criteria. Although precision maximization (high TPR/low FPR) was prioritized, limited data hindered boundary definition for TPR-FPR 
optimization curves. The optimization curve, as demonstrated by Reynaert, et al. (2023), is a useful tool for visualizing the model 
calibration process and guiding the selection of an appropriate FPR to evaluate performance across the training, validation, and test 
sets. Beyond mathematical FPR determination, water reuse contexts require quantitative risk assessment of FPs. Identifying reasonable 
operating points before cross-scenario testing would benefit model validation and set-point adjustments. Meanwhile, wide confidence 
intervals further underscore the need for larger datasets to evaluate model stability and feature contributions comprehensively. 
Current reliance on effluent NH₄⁺-N/NO₃⁻-N should expand to include operational parameters, water quality dynamics, and temporal 
data for credible sensitivity analysis.

– Phase I: Synthetic → Synthetic (Current study)
– Phase II: Synthetic → Real (Next validation step)
– Phase III: Hybrid training (e.g., 60 % real + 40 % synthetic)
Incorporating more challenging operational conditions, such as system failures (e.g., aeration malfunction or membrane damage),
