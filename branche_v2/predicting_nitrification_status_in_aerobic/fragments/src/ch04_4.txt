3.4. Cross-scenario test
Building upon the demonstrated efficacy of the model in conventional nitrification systems (Section 3.3), its cross-scenario pre­
diction capability was further evaluated in MBRs incorporating biocarriers for enhanced simultaneous nitrification-denitrification. The 
base model trained on no-biocarrier operations was directly tested to predict nitrification status in biocarrier-augmented systems 
without retraining.
As shown in Table 1a, the cross-condition testing yielded three principal observations: 
1) Compared with prediction performance of the test group without biocarriers, the RF model exhibited improvement in both ac­
curacy (0.79 → 0.83) and precision (0.83 → 0.87) under biocarrier-amended conditions, suggesting superior adaptability to 
simultaneous nitrification-denitrification system complexities.
2) TPRs of all these three algorithms were greater than or equal to 0.8, especially that of LR, which reached 1, showing a significant 
increase compared with the test group without adding biological carriers.
3) Corresponding FPR values rose significantly, particularly for LR (FPR=0.50), indicating only 50 % negative sample identification 
accuracy.
Full confusion matrices are provided in SI 9. While these results are theoretically not directly comparable to those from non- 
biocarriers data (Table 1a) due to differences in operating conditions and dataset characteristics, these findings provide operational 
insights into cross-condition model behavior.
The comparatively high TPR and FPR observed in the test group of the MBR with biocarrier addition can be explained by the 
following reasons. First, feature consistency in the test set played a significant role. In the training set, the input feature "air flow rate" 
included two values (2 and 6 Lpm), whereas all samples in the test set had an air flow rate of 6 Lpm. This consistency simplified the 
model’s decision rules, reducing the likelihood of misclassifying negative label samples (as shown in the KDEs in Table 1b). Second, the 
limitations of the decision boundary also contributed to the results. Despite effective positive classification at 6 Lpm, the model 
retained residual misclassification tendencies for negative samples. This effect was amplified by the test set’s limited negative sample 
size (n = 8), rendering FPR measurements particularly sensitive to individual misclassifications.
Finally, bootstrap sampling (n = 1000) revealed that models without biocarriers exhibited high precision/accuracy but wide 95 % 
confidence intervals (indicating instability), whereas biocarrier addition narrowed confidence intervals—particularly for RF, which 
achieved optimal stability and performance (detailed confidence interval analysis for both scenarios is provided in SI 10).
