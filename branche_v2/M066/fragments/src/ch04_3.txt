3.3. Composition and functional roles of microbial communities
The microbial community in the AnMBR showed significant shifts 
over the operational period, closely following reactor performance and 
environmental variation. The microbial community analysis, including 
phylum/class-level composition and functional predictions, is shown in 
Fig. S3. Firmicutes remained abundant facilitating efficient decomposi­
tion of organic compounds. In contrast, the proportion of Proteobacteria 
decreased, reflecting reduced degradation of refractory organics and 
increased production of humic-like substances under shorter HRT. 
Meanwhile, the relative abundance of Chloroflexi rose from 6.6% to 
37.5%, while groups related to Anaerolineaceae became more abundant, 
which might indicate their involvement in sludge structure, EPS pro­
duction, biofilm formation and development of cake layers. These 
compositional changes in microbial community clearly indicate a strong 
interplay between microbial community structure and reactor operation 
in the AnMBR system [12,30]. Enrichment of functionally relevant mi­
crobial groups supported stable process performance even under 
elevated loading conditions. In relation to membrane fouling, these 
microbial shifts were interpreted together with TMP, EPS/SMP, EEM, 
particle-size, SEM, and cleaning-response results Fig. 2(b-e). The com­
bined evidence thus establishes a consistent pathway: operational stress 
(HRT reduction) → selective enrichment of fouling-associated taxa in 
the membrane-adhered sludge → enhanced EPS/SMP secretion → 
aggravated cake-layer formation and pore blocking → TMP rise.
