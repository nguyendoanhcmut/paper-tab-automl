3.1. Organic removal efficiency in AnMBR systems
The AnMBR system demonstrated consistent performance across all 
operational stages despite variations in HRT and OLR. During the start- 
up and adaptation phase (days 1–28, Fig. 1a), influent COD concentra­
tion increased while HRT decreased from 15 to 9.5 days. Effluent COD 
remained below 900 mg/L, with COD removal efficiency rising from 
90% to 99%, indicating effective microbial adaptation. Over this period, 
OLR increased from 0.42 to 4.65 kg/m³d. During the stable operation 
phase (days 29–103), HRT was reduced further to 6.5 days with 100% 
raw leachate as the influent. Despite intermittent fluctuations in influent 
COD concentration, removal efficiency remained remarkably high, 
suggesting that the reactor was approaching its treatment capacity. At 
this phase, the AnMBR exhibited robust and stable performance. Under 
the shock loading phase (days 104–120), the system experienced highly 
stressful conditions, with effluent COD peaking at approximately 
5500 mg/L and OLR ranging from 7.5 to 8.0 kg COD/m³ ⋅d. COD 
removal efficiency declined to 84–88%, which can be attributed to 
organic overload and potential leachate toxicity [19].
As presented in Fig. 1b, effluent NH4
+-N concentrations remained 
persistently high throughout the experiment, averaging 1300 mg/L. 
This implies that under strictly anaerobic conditions, the AnMBR 
exhibited very limited capacity for NH4
+-N removal [25], despite 
achieving effective organic matter removal [26]. Similar behaviour has 
been reported for various anaerobic membrane systems treating 
high-strength wastewater [27]. To overcome this limitation, integrating 
AnMBR with aerobic post treatment units, such as nitrification or partial 
ammonia oxidation processes, was thus recommended by previous 
studies [28,29] to enhance overall nitrogen removal performance.
