1. Introduction
Rapid urbanization and rising living standards have driven a sharp 
increase in municipal solid waste, placing significant strain on urban 
waste management systems. Urban waste management generally in­
volves three interconnected stages: front-end collection, mid-end 
transfer, and end-of-life treatment [1]. Waste transfer stations serve as 
a key link between waste collection and final disposal [2]. Leachate 
generated at these stations is compositionally complex, typically con­
taining high levels of organic matter, ammonia, soluble microbial 
products (SMP), suspended solids, and toxic substances [3,4]. Without 
proper treatment, such pollutants can severely impact soil, surface 
water, groundwater, and the atmosphere [5]. In practice, leachate 
management at transfer stations is frequently constrained by limited 
space [2], economic barriers to advanced treatment technologies, and, 
in some cases, insufficient regulatory enforcement. Consequently, 
transfer station landfill leachate is often stored inadequately or dis­
charged without minimal treatment, increasing environmental risks [1].
Anaerobic membrane bioreactor (AnMBR) technology has received 
increased attention as a promising approach for the treatment and reuse 
of industrial and municipal high-strength wastewaters [3], owing to its 
high-quality effluent, compact footprint, and potential for energy re­
covery [6,7]. However, membrane fouling remains the most critical 
challenge limiting the stable and cost-effective operation of AnMBR [8, 
9]. Fouling leads to elevated transmembrane pressure (TMP), reduced 
permeate flux, and increased maintenance cost, ultimately affecting the 
long-term feasibility of AnMBR application [10,11]. To mitigate mem­
brane fouling, various control strategies have been explored, including 
influent pre-treatment, increased aeration intensity, and chemical con­
ditioning of mixed liquor [9,12]. Nevertheless, these approaches are 
often energy-intensive, economically unattractive, or insufficient to 
highly variable waste streams like landfill leachate [13].
Recent advances in Machine learning (ML) have demonstrated 
strong capabilities in modelling complex, nonlinear relationships 
inherent in environmental systems. ML methods, such as artificial neural 
networks (ANNs) [14], support vector regression (SVR) [15], extreme 
gradient boosting (XGBoost), and random forest (RF) [12,16], have been 
widely applied to monitor and optimize wastewater treatment processes

quality, and real-time early warning and improved process control [19]. 
Despite these advances, most ML applications have focused on relatively 
stable municipal or industrial systems [3,20,8,21], with limited atten­
tion to highly variable, high-strength waste streams such as landfill 
leachates, where influent composition and loading conditions can fluc­
tuate sharply over time [18,13,14]. Moreover, existing models are often 
trained for interpolation within historical data ranges and may lack 
robustness under sudden loading fluctuations or operational distur­
bances [22]. These limitations are particularly critical in AnMBR sys­
tems, where treatment performance and membrane fouling evolution 
are strongly influenced by time-dependent interactions among influent 
quality, operating conditions, and microbial community dynamics [20, 
23]. Thus, there is a clear need for adaptive, dynamic modelling 
frameworks capable of integrating real-time sensor data and reliably 
predicting system behaviour under varying operating scenarios.
In response, this study integrates 120-day AnMBR operation for 
transfer-station leachate treatment with interpretable, data-driven ML 
models. The reactor was operated under systematically varied hydraulic 
retention time (HRT) and organic loading rate (OLR) conditions to 
evaluate COD removal, nitrogen transformation and membrane fouling 
behaviour. Fouling development was assessed through operational 
monitoring, antifouling performance evaluation, and characterization of 
foulant composition and particle size distribution. Microbial community 
structure and functional shifts were also analysed to better understand 
the linkages among microbial dynamics, reactor performance and 
fouling development. Three ML algorithms, namely RF, ANN and 
XGBoost, were developed to predict effluent COD and TMP using 
routinely monitored influent, effluent and operational parameters. 
Model hyperparameters were optimized using randomized search and 5- 
fold cross-validation. Model interpretation was performed using SHap­
ley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) to identify the variables most strongly 
associated with COD and TMP predictions. The aim of this study is 
therefore to provide a quantitative operational boundary, a reversed 
foulant predictor, and an interpretable, sensor-lite diagnostic framework 
for understanding and predicting AnMBR performance during the 
treatment of high-strength and highly variable transfer-station landfill 
leachate. By integrating long-term reactor operation, microbial com­
munity analysis and interpretable ML models, this work provides useful 
diagnostic insights into the OLR-HRT operating window, COD removal 
behaviour and membrane fouling development, thereby supporting 
more sustainable and robust AnMBR-based leachate management at 
waste transfer facilities.
