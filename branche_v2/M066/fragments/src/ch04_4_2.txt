3.4.2. Hyperparameter optimization results
To maximize predictive accuracy while preventing overfitting, 
hyperparameter optimization was performed using randomized search 
with 5-fold cross-validation (CV). This approach systematically explores 
the hyperparameter space to identify configurations that optimize 
generalization performance. Table 1 summarizes the optimized hyper­
parameters and corresponding mean cross-validation scores for both 
prediction targets.
For effluent COD prediction, RF demonstrated superior stability 
during CV, achieving the highest R² (0.94) and lowest RMSE 
(396.65 mg/L). XGBoost performed comparably (CV R² = 0.92), while 
ANN showed greater prediction variability (CV R² = 0.87). XGBoost 
exhibited comparable performance (cross-validation R² =
0.92), 
whereas the artificial neural network (ANN) showed relatively lower 
predictive stability (cross-validation R² = 0.87). For TMP prediction, 
XGBoost attained the highest cross-validation performance in the 
transformed target space, with R² = 0.69 and RMSE = 0.54 kPa, fol­
lowed by RF (R² = 0.62, RMSE = 0.59 kPa), while the ANN yielded 
weaker cross-validation results (R² = 0.47, RMSE = 0.73 kPa). However, 
the performance of XGBoost raises concerns regarding potential over­
fitting due to its model complexity compared to the small size of the TMP 
dataset. Thus, although XGBoost had slightly superior cross-validation 
performance, RF provided the strongest generalization for TMP 
prediction.
