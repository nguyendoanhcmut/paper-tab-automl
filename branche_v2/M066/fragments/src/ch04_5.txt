3.5. SHAP feature importance analysis
Since RF provided the strongest independent test-set performance for 
both effluent COD and TMP, SHAP analysis was applied to the final RF 
models to explain model-level predictive associations [19]. The mean 
absolute SHAP values are shown in Fig. S5, while the SHAP summary 
plots are presented in Fig. 5. For Effluent COD prediction, Fig. 5a, the 
important features in the prediction were ranked in the following order 
of importance: OLR > HRT > ORP > influent NH₄⁺-N > influent COD 
> influent pH. OLR showed the largest contribution to the model output, 
with high OLR values generally associated with positive SHAP values 
and higher predicted Effluent COD. Low OLR values mainly contributed 
negative SHAP values, indicating lower predicted Effluent COD. This 
pattern is consistent with the operational role of organic loading, where 
higher loading can increase the treatment burden on the reactor and 
reduce COD removal efficiency. HRT showed an opposite tendency, 
where longer HRT values were generally associated with negative SHAP 
values and lower predicted COD, consistent with longer contact time 
and improved biodegradation [20]. ORP also contributed to COD pre­
diction, with more reducing conditions generally associated with lower 
predicted COD, reflecting a more favourable anaerobic environment for 
organic matter degradation. Since OLR, HRT and influent COD are 
operationally related, these SHAP rankings were interpreted as 
model-learned associations rather than independent causal effects.
For TMP prediction, SHAP interpretation of the RF model also 
indicated a distinct hierarchy of feature importance (Fig. 5b). The var­
iables were ordered as follows: effluent NH4
+-N > effluent pH > ORP 
> influent pH > influent COD > influent NH4
+-N. The effluent NH4
+-N 
values were generally associated with positive SHAP values, indicating 
higher predicted TMP, while lower effluent NH4
+-N values contributed to 
lower predicted TMP. This association was interpreted cautiously as a 
diagnostic signal of the reactor’s biological and chemical state rather 
than direct evidence that NH4
+-N caused fouling. Ammonia and free 
ammonia have been reported to inhibit anaerobic microbial activity and 
affect sludge properties. Free-ammonia pretreatment can promote 
sludge solubilization and hydrolysis [32], while free-ammonia concen­
tration affects EPS production and sludge physicochemical properties 
[33]. In nitrogen-removal MBR systems, EPS and microbial metabolites, 
including proteins, polysaccharides and humic substances, contributed 
to more severe membrane fouling [34]. It is also reported that EPS 
composition and microbial community shifts were closely related to 
nitrogen-removal stability and sludge aggregation [35].
Effluent pH and ORP also contributed to TMP prediction, suggesting 
that acid-base conditions and redox status were associated with the 
fouling state captured by the model. pH reflects several indirect but 
operationally relevant fouling-related processes in high-strength 
AnMBR systems, including free ammonia equilibrium that may inhibit 
methanogenic activity and influence the release of SMP, surface charge/ 
gelation propensity of EPS that affect cake-layer compactness and 
filtration resistance, and inorganic precipitation that participate in 
composite organic-inorganic fouling layers. Since effluent NH4
+-N and 
effluent pH are downstream or synchronously measured variables, the 
TMP model should be interpreted as a diagnostic soft-sensing model 
rather than a strict forward-looking early-warning model. The SHAP 
results therefore provide interpretable associations between routinely 
monitored process variables and TMP behaviour, but they do not 
establish independent mechanistic causality.
While the experimental and ML based predictions yield encouraging 
results, several considerations warrant mention regarding the scope of 
this study. The models were developed using data from a single 
laboratory-scale reactor (4 L working volume) treating leachate from 
one transfer station thus, direct extrapolation to full-scale systems or 
different leachate compositions should be undertaken with caution. The 
120-day operational period, although informative, covers a narrower 
range of conditions than typical long-term industrial applications. 
Furthermore, the SHAP analysis is confined to the available input vari­
ables and does not encompass other biologically or physicochemical 
relevant parameters that may influence process behaviour. Another 
limitation is the presence of collinearity especially OLR, HRT and 
influent COD. This may affect the interpretation of feature importance 
therefore, SHAP results were used to support model interpretation 
rather than to establish causal mechanisms. Additionally, the current 
models predict effluent COD and TMP without directly incorporating 
microbial community dynamics or fouling composition data. Future 
efforts should prioritize data collection from diverse AnMBR configu­
rations to enhance model generalizability and enable transfer learning. 
Integrating multi-frequency data streams, online monitoring, and bio­
logical indicators would further improve the capacity to predict rapid 
performance transitions and membrane fouling characteristics.
