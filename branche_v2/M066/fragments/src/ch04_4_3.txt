with optimized configurations and subsequently evaluated on the in­
dependent test data set to assess true generalization capability. For 
effluent COD prediction (Fig. 4a-c and Table 2), all three models ach­

the 1:1 line. RF delivered the most stable performance, with low residual 
variance and symmetric residuals around zero (Fig. S4a-c), indicating 
robust generalization across the effluent COD range. XGBoost achieved 
similar R² = 0.96 but showed larger deviations at higher effluent COD 
values, suggesting reduced stability under extreme loading. ANN 
exhibited a wider prediction spread, particularly at mid- to high-COD 
levels, reflecting lower sensitivity to nonlinear trends, consistent with

TMP prediction is more challenging because membrane fouling is 
governed by nonlinear, dynamic and time-dependent processes 
involving biological activity, EPS accumulation, cake layer formation, 
and physicochemical interactions (Fig. 4d–f and Table 2). After model 
optimization, RF achieved the best TMP prediction performance (R² =
0.88, RMSE = 3.86 kPa), while XGBoost and ANN yielded weaker 
generalization, with R² values of 0.56 and 0.54 and RMSE values of 7.50 
and 7.59 kPa, respectively. The predicted versus observed plots showed 
that RF predictions aligned more closely with the 1:1 line than those 
from XGBoost and ANN. XGBoost closely fitted the training data but 
exhibited larger deviations on the test set, indicating overfitting and 
reduced transferability to unseen observations. ANN predictions were 
more dispersed, suggesting a limited capacity to capture the nonlinear 
TMP response under the available dataset.
Residual analysis confirmed these patterns (Fig. S4d–f). RF produced 
the most compact residual distribution for TMP among the three models, 
although some deviations remained at higher TMP magnitudes, indi­
cating increased uncertainty under more severe fouling conditions. 
XGBoost displayed larger residual errors despite its strong training 
performance, supporting the interpretation that the model captured 
training-specific patterns rather than stable fouling dynamics. ANN re­
siduals were also widely scattered, reflecting weaker generalization for 
TMP prediction. Overall, the residual spread was greater for TMP than 
for effluent COD, confirming that fouling prediction is more uncertain 
than COD prediction. This behaviour is consistent with previous mem­
brane fouling studies, where data-driven models may perform well 
under normal operating conditions but become less reliable during 
abrupt or severe fouling development [8,31].
Overall, effluent COD (R² > 0.90) proved more predictable than TMP 
(R² = 0.80–0.50), consistent with COD being governed by relatively 
deterministic, load-dependent kinetics, whereas TMP reflects highly 
nonlinear and stochastic fouling mechanisms influenced by biological 
activity, EPS accumulation, and physicochemical conditions [8]. RF is 
therefore best suited for COD monitoring due to its stability and accu­
racy, while ANN provides superior robustness for TMP prediction under 
variable loading. XGBoost poor TMP generalization underscores the 
importance of independent test-set validation for complex models 
applied to limited environmental datasets, a point emphasized in recent 
guidance on secure and accurate modelling of anaerobic membrane 
bioreactors. The combined use of predicted-versus-observed plots and 
residual analysis offers a comprehensive assessment of both accuracy 
and reliability.
Additionally, we evaluated extrapolation under unseen operating 
conditions, the chronological data splitting was applied. Data from 
Phases I–II were used for model training, while Phase III data served as 
an independent test set. After data cleaning and alignment, the training 
set contained 103 samples and the Phase III test set contained 16 sam­
ples. This test was used only to assess extrapolation under shock loading 
and was reported separately from the main random 80:20 split results. 
The Phase III shock-loading extrapolation test showed a marked decline 
in model performance compared with the random 80:20 split (Table S3). 
Effluent COD and TMP prediction by RF, ANN, and XGBoost produced 
Phase III R² negative values, indicating that the models could not reli­
