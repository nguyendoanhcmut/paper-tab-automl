3.2. Event detection in the intake of the Llobregat River (PCA of dataset 2)
The focus of this particular study is on the short-term, strong ﬂuctu-
ations, which can be assigned to possible pollution events, detected in
the PCA scores plots.
Dataset 2 with the 346 real experimental observations for the seven
parameters (which matched their time recordings) was analysed with
PCA to further investigate and validate the nature of the previously ob-
served events (in Dataset 1) related with the organic matter, inorganic
matter and pH component sources explained in the previous section.
These 346 observations spanned six months of monitoring, between
July and November 2014. PCA applied to the autoscaled data suggested
a model with three principal components, explaining as much as 81% of
the total data variance. The relative percentage variance contributions
of these components were: PC1 40% N PC2 25% N PC3 16%.
Analysis of PC1-PC3 loading plots (see Fig. 4A, C and E) conﬁrmed
that the same three factors previously explained in the analysis of

The following discussion is focused on three consecutive events ob-

(meteorological) or industrial (spills) phenomena using meteorological

Event 1 occurred at the river water intake on 03/08/2014 at 06:00 h.
The sensor readings at this hour recorded TOC 7.1 mg/L; NH4 1.91 mg/L;
TURB 567 UNF; UV254 15.8 abs/100; COND 784uS/cm; TEMP 22.9C and
pH 7.6. For this Event 1, the PCA model reported unusual sample Q-
residuals with extreme values (unexplained variance) for turbidity as
seen in Fig. 5B. On 01/08 and 02/082014, the information collected at
the weather webpage identiﬁed several rainfall episodes occurred in
24 h with recordings up to 30 mm of rainfall (see Fig. 6). Thus, this
event 1 was is an example of the effect of a sudden increase of the
washed out natural organic matter into the river ﬂow. Simultaneously,
large volumes of rain water leaded to strong dilution effects, also for
the minerals, resulting in a signiﬁcant reduction of COND and pH pa-
rameters respectively (found with negative loadings on PC1). The rest
of the event peaks with strong positive scores on PC1 were also associ-
ated to rainfall events during the six month period of investigation.
Events with high organic matter associated to industrial discharges on
PC1 were not detected.
Event 2 deﬁned the beginning of a new episode of heavy rain with a
sharp rise in the concentration of organic matter detected on PC1. This
organic matter event was gradually diminishing after some days on
this plot giving the beginning of the next event 3, observed as strong
event on PC2 and PC3 plots and explained further in the next section.
Event 2 occurred at the DWTP intake on 22/08/2014 at local time
22:00 h. The sensor readings recorded were TOC 6 mg/L; NH4
1.91 mg/L; TURB 180 UNF; UV254 13.9 abs/100 cm; COND 1045 uS/cm;
TEMP 23.2C; pH 7.8. The Q parameter contributions of the PCA model
revealed unusually high values for NH4 and in a less extent for pH and
water temperature (Fig. 5C). This rainy episode event started with a
sharp rise on NH4 suggesting a possible runoff from the wastewater
treatment plants located nearby. Event 2 on 22/08/2014 ﬁnished in
the beginning of event 3.
The peak of the event 3, occurred at 03:00 h on 01/09/2014 with sen-
sor recordings: TOC 2.6 mg/L; NH4 0.24 mg/L; TURB 153.3 UNF; UV254
11.2 abs/100 cm; COND 3413 uS/cm; TEMP 24.8C; pH 8.4. This event
shows unusual Q parameter contribution (Fig. 5D) only for the COND
parameter. In contrast, the rest of parameters were within their regular
levels. This event was produced by the unusual increase of concentra-
tions of dissolved minerals in water. Conductivity value was recorded
above 3400 μS/cm and, as a result, the DWTP had to close its activity
of water intake. Valuable information about weather conditions during
event 3, was retrieved from the meta-analysis of the meteorological da-
tabase. During 30/08/2014 and 31/08/2014, multiple local heavy rain-
falls (up to 25 mm of accumulated rainfall in short time) were
recorded by the meteorological stations located in radius of 30 km
near the potash mining facilities nearby the river basin. It is plausible
therefore to suggest that these heavy rainfalls also affected the indus-
trial area and produced signiﬁcant mineral spills which were detected
at the DWTP intake as an unusual event.
