Forecasting the stabilization and operational performance of anammox-based nitrogen removal processes is 
essential for engineering operations, especially for treating municipal wastewater by anammox-based processes, 
which were usually unstable in practice39,70. By integrating and comparing the big data collected from different 
anammox-based studies, this study revealed the difference in data characteristics between experiments using 
sequencing batch reactor and continuous flow reactor, using floc and aggregate, using synthetic wastewater 
and municipal wastewater, as well as using different anammox-based process types. Results showed that the 
anammox-based continuous flow reactors operated by microbial aggregates had great potential to treat high 
levels of nitrogen pollutants. The big difference between the characteristics of synthetic wastewater and municipal 
wastewater applied in current experiments extremely weakened the practical meaning of anammox-based 
experiments based on synthetic wastewater30. Meanwhile, the PNA experiments and PNA combined with PDA 
experiments showed higher TIN removal efficiency than the PDA experiments. Stronger positive correlations 
(0.55–0.95) between influent and effluent nitrogen concentrations were found in the PNA experiments, implying 
the higher sensitivity of PNA processes to the influent characteristics.
Importantly, this study provided a new insight: the fast implementation and high accuracy of the AutoML 
algorithm for these important output variables (i.e., effluent NH4
+-N, effluent NO3
−-N, effluent NO2
−-N, 
effluent TIN, NH4
+-N removal efficiency, TIN removal efficiency, and NARR) makes it a useful tool to learn 
the information inside the anammox-based datasets, and predict the practical operation of anammox-based 
nitrogen removal processes. GBM and XGBoost models were identified as the most accurate machine learning 
models for predicting the effluent variables in the anammox-based processes (R2 = 0.814–0.993). The high 
accuracy of these models was not only achieved for data collected from literature but also confirmed by unseen 
data from our anammox-based experiment, which proved the excellent generalization ability in predicting the 
performance of anammox-based nitrogen removal processes.
Moreover, the potential improvement strategies for anammox-based processes could be proposed based 
on the interpretable results of optimal models generated by the AutoML algorithm. The appropriate ranges of 
key characteristics such as C/N, influent NH4
+-N, influent TIN, influent COD, HRT, and NLR were identified 
by the 1D and 2D PDP results. To reduce effluent NH4
+-N, it is recommended to keep C/N at 2.72–6.32 and 
influent TIN at 18.85–87.76 mg/L. NO3

There are also some limitations existing in this study. First, the prediction accuracy of optimal models 
generated by the AutoML algorithm suffered from a slight decrease in the prediction of effluent NO2
−-N, effluent 
NO3
−-N, and NARR. This decreasing generalization ability may be due to the different operating conditions and 
microbial compositions in experiments between the training dataset collected from the literature and the unseen 
dataset collected from our study. Importantly, it was found that effluent NO2
−-N, effluent NO3
−-N, and NARR, 
as output variables, were more sensitive to the different operating conditions and microbial compositions in the 
anammox-based processes. The generalization ability of candidate models generated by the AutoML algorithm 
may be improved by including more essential input variables related to these output variables in the training 
datasets55.
Secondly, various organics with complex chemical structures exist in municipal wastewater. Among them, 
some substituents (e.g., −CH3, −OH) in organics impact the substrate diffusion and membrane stability by 
changing the hydrophobicity. Moreover, some substituents (e.g., −Cl, −F) in organics contribute to the biotoxicity 
and will inhibit the anammox activity72. However, COD was set as an index for representing all these organics in 
most anammox-based experiments fed with municipal wastewater. The missing concentrations of these sensitive 
organics in the datasets will limit the understanding of machine learning models for the effects of different 
organics on the anammox-based nitrogen removal processes.
On the other hand, although we included the dominant anammox genera in the dataset as a categorical variable, 
more variables related to microbes should be considered in future studies. Nowadays, the data size of microbes 
in these experiments is much smaller than the data size of influent and effluent variables. The appropriate ways 
to collect and utilize microbial data of anammox-based experiments should be further explored to overcome 
the insufficient data volume73. Employing machine learning algorithms to scrutinize these microbial datasets 
will not only facilitate a more profound comprehension of the microorganisms involved in anammox-based 
processes, but also offer valuable insights that enhance the nitrogen removal efficiency of practical anammox-
based systems for treating municipal wastewater52. Future directions for developing multifactor optimization 
strategies for anammox treatment should increasingly explore the valuable information hidden in microbial 
information. Ultimately, the fusion of high-dimensional microbial community fingerprints with process 
variables will enable the development of biology-informed digital twins, shifting the paradigm of anammox 
optimization from empirical hydraulic control toward precision microbiome management.
