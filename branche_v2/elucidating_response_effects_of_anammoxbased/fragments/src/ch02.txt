removing nitrogen and phosphorus. However, eliminating nitrogen through the nitrification and denitrification 
process by the conventional activated sludge has been identified as an energy-intensive process3. Therefore, 
anaerobic ammonium oxidation (anammox), a new technology with low energy consumption, low sludge 
productivity, and a high nitrogen removal rate, has drawn increasing attention in recent years2,4–6.
Unlike traditional two-step nitrification and denitrification processes, the anammox process uses nitrite and 
ammonia as substrates and directly oxidizes them to N2
7. Anammox is one of the most promising processes for 
saving the operating costs of wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs), reducing organic carbon requirements 
by 100%, aeration energy consumption by approximately 60%, and sludge production by approximately 90%8. 
However, the stable supply of nitrite to anammox remains an urgent problem. To provide nitrite steadily, several 
types of combined processes including simultaneous nitrification, anammox and denitrification9,10, partial 
nitrification coupling with anammox (PNA)11, partial denitrification coupling with anammox (PDA)12,13, and 
more complex processes combining PNA and PDA processes14,15 have been investigated extremely. Moreover, 
these anammox processes for mainstream application face many technical bottlenecks such as low nitrogen 
concentration of municipal wastewater, high organic matter concentration16, and low water temperature17 in 
winter. To solve these issues, numerous attempts have been made to mainstream anammox processes from 
multiple operational factors such as influent characteristics, inoculation mode, temperature, reactor type, and 
hydraulic retention time. A large amount of data has been generated during these experiments. However, the

Machine learning algorithms have drawn increasing attention due to their ability to identify the internal 
relationships in big datasets and create corresponding prediction models18,19. Explainable analysis and 
interpretable methods have been developed to further explain the trained machine learning models20–22. For 
instance, Xu et al. (2022b)23 analyzed response effects of anammox processes to different antibiotics through 
a universal machine learning model (R2 inline-eq-IEq10.9). Similarly, Yang et al. (2023)24 focused on the heavy metal 
stress on anammox processes based on machine learning models. Moreover, Liu et al. (2023a)25 explored the 
N2O emissions from anammox-based processes. They revealed the relationship between N2O emissions and 
operational factors as well as microbial communities through machine learning models and corresponding 
interpretable analysis. These studies have demonstrated that machine learning models have performed well in 
big data analysis for anammox-based processes23–25.
However, the response effects of nitrogen treatment performance in the different types of anammox-based 
processes to multiple operational factors are still unclear. Moreover, previous studies for big data analysis of 
anammox-based processes established machine learning models manually23–25, which suffers from several 
drawbacks. On one hand, the procedure of training and tuning these machine learning models had a significant 
time cost22. On the other hand, the accuracy of these trained models heavily depended on the experience of 
model designers, which had poor reproducibility and robustness. To overcome these drawbacks, automated 
machine learning (AutoML) algorithms have been proposed and drawn increasing attention22,26,27.
Therefore, in this study, the AutoML algorithm was applied to predict nitrogen treatment performance and 
interpret the response effects of anammox-based processes to multiple operational factors. Data from relevant 
anammox-based studies were first analyzed to select the potential input and output variables, which formed the 
integrated dataset for running the H2O AutoML algorithm. Then, the candidate models generated by the H2O 
AutoML algorithm for 7 output variables were compared. Moreover, lab-scale anammox-based experiments were 
carried out to test the prediction performance of candidate models. Based on the optimal models, interpretable 
analysis was conducted to clarify the response effects of output variables to the important input variables. These 
results highlight previously overlooked yet crucial relationships impacting the anammox-based treatment 
performance, providing a significant step towards the improvement of mainstream anammox-based processes.
