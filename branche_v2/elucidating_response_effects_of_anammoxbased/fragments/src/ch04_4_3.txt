For predicting effluent TIN and TIN removal efficiency, the influent TIN and operation time were the most 
important input variables. Besides, the input variables including influent NO3
−-N, HRT, influent NH4
+-N, 
and NLR played more important roles in predicting effluent TIN (Fig. 2(d)), whereas the COD-related input 
variables (i.e., influent COD and C/N), HRT and NLR were more important for predicting TIN removal 
efficiency (Fig. 2(f)).
In the 1D PDP of operation time, the decreasing response of effluent TIN (Fig. S9(a)) and the increasing 
response of TIN removal efficiency (Fig. S11(a)) indicated the maturity of anammox-based nitrogen removal 
processes. Moreover, the short HRT (< 6.84  h) and long HRT (> 24.45  h) presented better TIN removal 
performance (Fig. S9(b) and Fig. S11(e)). For influent NH4
+-N, influent NO3
−-N, and influent TIN, identical 
response trends of the effluent TIN were achieved (Fig. S9(c), (d) and (e)). The response of the effluent TIN 
greatly increased at 181.18  mg/L, 50.07  mg/L, and 95.87  mg/L for influent NH4
+-N, influent NO3
−-N, and 
influent TIN, respectively. However, the increasing NLR did not lead to the growing response of effluent TIN 
(Fig. S9(f)) because NLR was affected by the mutual interactions of influent TIN and HRT. In the 1D PDP of 
influent TIN (Fig. S11(b)), influent COD (Fig. S11(c)), C/N (Fig. S11(d)), and NLR (Fig. S11(f)), similar trends 
of the response of TIN removal efficiency were observed. The highest responses of TIN removal efficiency were 
achieved at 87.76 mg/L, 179.33 mg/L, 2.95, and 0.49 kg/m3/d for influent TIN, influent COD, C/N, and NLR, 
respectively.
For the response of effluent TIN, the 2D PDP of operation time versus HRT (Fig. S10(a)) and operation time 
versus NLR (Fig. S10(e)) had a decreasing trend with the decrease of operation time. Instead, increasing response 
of effluent TIN with the increase of influent NH4
+-N, influent NO3
−-N, and influent TIN was identified in the 
2D PDP of operation time versus influent NH4
+-N, influent NO3
−-N, and influent TIN (Fig. S10(b), (c) and 
(d)). Moreover, special patterns in the 2D PDP of HRT versus influent NH4
+-N, influent NO3
−-N, and influent

15.64–17.20 h, and > 24.45 h (Fig. S10(f), (g) and (h)). The 2D PDP of HRT versus NLR further confirmed that 
high HRT (> 24.45 h) and low HRT (< 2.18 h) led to the lowest effluent TIN at high NLR levels (Fig. S10(i)).
