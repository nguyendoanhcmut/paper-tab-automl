NARR, as an important calculation variable for anammox-based nitrogen removal rate, was highly affected by 
the input variables including HRT, NLR, influent TIN, influent NO2
−-N, influent COD, and influent NH4
+-N 
(Fig. 2(g)). The response of NARR had a decreasing trend in the 1D PDP of HRT (Fig. S13(a)) because the 
HRT was the denominator in the equation of NARR (Text S1). In contrast, for nitrogen loading variables (i.e., 
NLR, influent TIN, influent NO2
−-N, influent NH4
+-N), the response of NARR increased (Fig. S13(b), (c), (d), 
and (f)), suggesting that the contribution of anammox processes was enhanced with higher nitrogen loadings. 
Meanwhile, except for influent COD > 506.33 mg/L, no obvious variation was observed in the 1D PDP of the 
influent COD (Fig. S13 (e)). The 2D PDP of influent COD versus NLR (Fig. S14 (a)), influent TIN (Fig. S14 (b)), 
influent NO2
−-N (Fig. S14 (c)), and influent NH4
+-N (Fig. S14 (d)) further confirmed that a great increase of 
NARR response occurred at the influent COD > 506.33 mg/L. Moreover, an extreme increase in NARR response 
was found at NLR > 1.28 kg/m3/d, influent TIN > 176.89 mg/L, influent NO2
−-N > 18.78 mg/L and influent NH4
+-
N > 152.61 mg/L. The combination of influent TIN > 176.94 mg/L and influent NO2
−-N > 18.78 mg/L led to the 
highest response of NARR (Fig. S14(e)). For HRT versus influent nitrogen and carbon variables (Fig. S14(f), 
(g), (h), (i) and (j)), an increasing trend of NARR response was identified with the decreasing HRT (< 7.36 h). 
Furthermore, higher NARR was found at the high NLR (> 2.18 kg/m3/d) and high influent NH4
+-N (> 148.53 kg/
m3/d) (Fig. S14(f) and (j)). Overall, the high NARR was observed at the high nitrogen loading and low HRT, 
implying that the anammox-based nitrogen removal processes were capable of adapting to high processing 
pressure in modern wastewater treatment plants69.
