3.2. Impact of salinity on AnMBR fouling profiles 
TMP profiles were monitored to characterize the ceramic membrane 
fouling under different salinity stress (Fig. 3). During Phase I and Phase 
II, a relatively similar membrane fouling profile was observed with 
operational time about 6.9–13.5 d. The dTMP/dt in slow and sudden 
TMP increase were 0.67 and 11.8 kPa/d, respectively. Further increase 
the salinity to 10 g/L in Phase III resulted in the fastest membrane 
fouling rate, showing dTMP/dt in slow and sudden TMP increase of 2.0 
and 24.4 kPa/d. Interestingly, as the salinity reached 20 g/L in Phase IV, 
the membrane fouling cycle was prolonged to 9.3–10.7 d with a much 
lower membrane fouling rate (dTMP/dt) of 0.36 kPa/d in the slow TMP 
increase stage. The highest TMP increase occurred in Phase III with 10 
g/L NaCl can be ascribed to the significant SMP secretion from microbes 
to confront the high salinity (Aquino and Stuckey, 2004). The TMP 
profile also indicated that Phase III was during a transition stage, which 
might be due to the transformation of the microbial community struc­
ture from salinity sensitive to salt-tolerant microbes under salinity se­
lective pressure (Luo et al., 2016) and the change of the membrane tank 
mixture characteristics especially the SMP content and SMP molecular 
weight distribution (See details in Section 3.3 and 3.4).
