1. Introduction 
The textile industry has been growing extensively and is becoming an 
important contributor to the national economic development of many 
countries (Carvalho et al., 2020; Li et al., 2018a). Additionally, the 
textile industry tends to consume a large quantity of water and over 90% 
of the water consumption becomes wastewater containing a high con­
centration of salinity with 5–6% NaCl, color, solids, chemical oxygen 
demand (COD) and heavy metals (Lin et al., 2015; Sari Erkan et al., 
2020). The discharge of effluent from the textile industry into natural 
water bodies posed a great potential hazard to aquatic organisms and 
public health because of its high toxicity and mutagenicity (Ozmen 
et al., 2008). Textile wastewater treatment can be treated by aerobic and 
anaerobic 
biological 
processes

physio-chemical processes such as coagulation and flocculation (Solanki 
et al., 2013). Biological treatment especially anaerobic process stands 
out due to its low cost and high efficiency (Bai et al., 2020; Georgiou and 
Aivasidis, 2006; Yu et al., 2015).

Yurtsever et al. (2015) studied the AnMBR performance for synthetic 
textile wastewater and indicated that complete color removal and about 
94% COD removal can be achieved. Berkessa et al. (2020) evaluated the 
feasibility of using an anaerobic dynamic membrane bioreactor for 
anthraquinone dye textile wastewater treatment and indicated that 
98.5% of soluble COD and over 97.5% of color can be removed. How­
ever, textile wastewater not only contains dyes but also several auxiliary 
chemicals and inhibitory compounds which may affect the treatment 
performance (Yurtsever et al., 2020). Yurtsever et al. (2020) investi­
gated the treatment performance of AnMBR for real textile wastewater 
treatment and only 84% COD removal efficiency can be obtained. 
The high salinity in textile wastewater would also inhibit the treat­
ment performance which can be ascribed to the negative effect on the 
metabolic activity of microorganisms thus reducing the pollutants 
degradation efficiency (Chen et al., 2019). Chen et al. (2019) evaluated 
the impact of salinity (NaCl 0–40 g/L) on AnMBR and suggested that 
COD removal efficiency was inhibited from 96.4% to 77.7%. The high 
salinity not only affect the treatment performance but also leads to more 
severe membrane fouling, which might be due to the increase of soluble 
microbial products (SMP) content under high salt stress (Yurtsever et al., 
2016). However, previous studies which evaluated the impact of salinity 
on AnMBR treatment performance mainly focused on the continuous 
stirred tank reactor (CSTR) configuration (Chen et al., 2019; Mu˜noz 
Sierra et al., 2018, 2019; Yurtsever et al., 2020). The upflow anaerobic 
sludge blanket (UASB) configured AnMBR appears to mitigate mem­
brane fouling compared with AnMBR in CSTR configuration due to the 
less fouling layer deposition (Liao et al., 2006; Wang et al., 2018a). 
Furthermore, UASB as a typical high-rate anaerobic reactor is commonly 
used for textile wastewater treatment and the usage of UASB configured 
AnMBR is conducive to the refurbishment of existing treatment plants 
(Liao et al., 2006). However, limited study was conducted in terms of the 
impact of salinity on UASB configured AnMBR for textile wastewater 
treatment. 
The biological process in the AnMBR is complicated and treatment 
performance is influenced by many factors (Huang et al., 2018). How­
ever, the relationship between the variables and pollutant removal has 
not been deeply clarified (Giri et al., 2011; Huang et al., 2018). Machine 
learning (ML) is a classification and regression technique based on past 
experience or data, which is widely used in wastewater treatment 
(Yaqub and Lee, 2022; Ye et al., 2020). Several models have been 
established to predict the AnMBR (Benyahia et al., 2013; Huang et al., 
2018). Huang et al. (2018) used a back propagation neural network 
(BPNN) model to predict the AnMBR treatment performance (COD and 
antibiotics removal) for pharmaceutical wastewater treatment. How­
ever, limited studies considered to include the impact of salinity in the 
AnMBR treatment performance prediction models. Adaboost, decision 
trees and random forests are typical machine learning algorithms. 
Adaboost takes multiple weak classifiers and combines them into a 
strong classifier (Freund and Schapire, 1997), which has a strong clas­
sification performance and is good at predicting pollutant concentration 
(Huang et al., 2023). Additionally, compared with decision trees and 
random forests, adaboost tends to have higher accuracy with less 
occurrence of overfitting, resulting in more realistic predictions (Chan 
and Paelinckx, 2008; Miao and Heaton, 2010). 
Therefore, this study aims to evaluate the impact of salinity on UASB 
configured AnMBR using ceramic membrane for synthetic textile 
wastewater treatment. The impact of stepwise increase of salinity (0–20 
g/L NaCl) on decolorization, organic degradation, anaerobic process 
and membrane fouling of an UASB configured ceramic AnMBR for 
textile wastewater treatment was evaluated. Then, the microbial com­
munity structure was analyzed to investigate the variations of microbial 
population under different salt stress. Finally, the effluent COD predic­
tion model based on Adaboost algorithm as a machine learning model 
was established to build the relationship between effluent COD and 
environmental factors in AnMBR.
