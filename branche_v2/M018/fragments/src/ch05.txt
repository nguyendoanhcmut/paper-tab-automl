4. Conclusion 
This study explored the impact of the stepwise increase of salinity on 
UASB-configured ceramic AnMBR treatment performance of textile 
wastewater. The following conclusions can be summarized:  
● Stepwise increase of salinity inhibited the COD removal from 92% to 
73%, but had a marginal effect on the MO removal (90–96%) in 
AnMBR.  
● Low salinity (5 g/L) boosted the biogas production whilst high 
salinity (>10 g/L) inhibited the process.  
● The increase of salinity resulted in SMP concentration soar and 
membrane fouling rate increase, peaking at a salinity of 10 g/L 
(Phase III) and recovering back to a lower level.  
● The microbial diversity analyses indicated a clear transition from 
salinity-sensitive microbes to a salinity-tolerant community, leading 
to the increased SMP concentration and more severe membrane 
fouling in Phase III and were mitigated after a new equilibrium was 
reached when the microbial community acclimatized to the high 
salinity  
● The Adaboost model was successfully established as the prediction 
model of effluent COD under different salinities, but more data is 
expected to improve the prediction accuracy.
