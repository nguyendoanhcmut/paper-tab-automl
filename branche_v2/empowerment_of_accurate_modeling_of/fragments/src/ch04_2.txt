3.2. Improved ML performance with operation time as a feature
The treatment efficiency of the AnMBR is not only affected by T-R, T- 
in, T-env, pH-in, COD-in, and flux but also by the reactor operation 
conditions. For instance, the microbial community structure and activity 
in an AnMBR are key factors that affect its efficiency (Hu et al., 2017; Xie 
et al., 2014). However, measuring microbial community structure is a 
difficult task because it is time-consuming and costly, which restricts the 
availability of community structure data. Consequently, this limitation 
may also hinder data-driven AnMBR modeling from effectively uncov­
ering the underlying mechanisms.
Based on the hypothesis that additional information may correlate 
with operation time, we incorporated reactor OD into the modeling 
process, assuming that OD may be a potential proxy for the microbial 
dynamics. The inclusion of OD resulted in a substantial improvement in 
model performance compared to the baseline feature set (T-R, T-in, T- 
env, pH-in, COD-in, and flux) (Fig. 4 and Fig. S1), with the mean of R2 
value increasing from 0.44 to 0.55 and mean of RMSE decreasing from 
2.47 to 2.20 (Fig. 4). Spearman correlation analysis indicates a weak 
association between OD and the target variable, suggesting a low like­
lihood of data leakage (see supplementary material). Thus, such 
enhancement suggests that the additional information carried by OD 
may have been effectively learned by the ML model. It is important to 
note that rather than being a direct causal parameter, OD acts as a 
surrogate variable capturing unmeasured temporal dynamics inherent 
to the AnMBRs process. This includes the gradual acclimatization of the 
microbial community (e.g., biofilm development) and the cumulative

The inclusion of ORP, HRT, MLSS, and MLVSS data did not markedly 
contribute to the model performance. When OD, ORP, HRT, MLSS, and 
MLVSS were used simultaneously to train the model, the overall effect 
was similar to that of the inclusion of OD alone. The Bland-Altman 
analysis showed that incorporating OD into the model reduced the dif­
ferences between the prediction and ground truth, rendering the dif­
ference more evenly distributed around zero compared to models 
without operation time (Fig. 5 and Fig. S2). This further confirms that 
including the operation time improves the modeling performance, 
whereas adding other input parameters yields only a limited improve­
ment. The limited contribution of typically parameters (e.g., ORP, HRT, 
MLSS, and MLVSS) likely reflects the specific constraints of the data to 
this study, such as measurement frequency and signal-to-noise ratio, 
rather than a lack of physical and biological relevance. Additionally, 
feature collinearity may have masked their importance, as the model 
could derive sufficient information from other correlated predictors. 
These results highlight the importance of feature selection for modeling.
