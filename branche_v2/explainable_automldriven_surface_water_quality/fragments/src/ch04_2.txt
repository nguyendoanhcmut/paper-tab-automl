3.2.1. Comparative performance of models

The performance of Auto-sklearn was comprehensively evaluated 
against seven machine learning models. Based on five independent runs 
using a stratified random split, a Tukey’s HSD statistical test (p < 0.05) 
grouped the models into distinct performance tiers. As shown in Table 1, 
Auto-sklearn, RF, and CatB constituted the top tier, with no statistically 
significant performance difference among them. Within this top group, 
however, Auto-sklearn consistently achieved the highest mean scores 
across all evaluation metrics, with a precision of 0.9636 ± 0.0027, recall 
of 0.9635 ± 0.0027, and a weighted F1-score of 0.9633 ± 0.0027. A 
detailed, per-class analysis presented in Table S7 further underscores 
this leading performance, showing that Auto-sklearn also attained the 
highest Macro F1-score. The remaining models were grouped into sub­
sequent performance tiers, with KNN and LR performing significantly 
poorer than all other methods.
The classification performance is further detailed in the confusion 
matrices (Fig. 4). Among the evaluated models, Auto-sklearn (Fig. 4a) 
emerged as the top-performing solution, demonstrating exceptional and 
well-balanced accuracy across all water quality classes. It achieved near- 
perfect scores for Class I (99.1 %) and Class II (98.4 %) and maintained 
high accuracy on the ambiguous intermediate classes: 96.1 % for Class 
III, 91.0 % for Class IV, and 84.4 % for Class V. This capability starkly 
contrasts with traditional models like LR (Fig. 4d), whose accuracy 
collapsed to just 53.3 % for Class III, 30.8 % for Class IV, and even 0 % 
for Class V, highlighting the severe limitations of linear models. The 
confusion matrices for other models are presented in Fig. S4.
The Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves and corre­
sponding Area Under the Curve (AUC) values in Fig. 5 further validate 
the models’ discriminative ability. Auto-sklearn (Fig. 5a) achieved the 
highest macro-average AUC (MA-AUC) of 0.9963, followed closely by 
other top-tier models like RF (MA-AUC = 0.9961) and CatB (MA-AUC =
0.9960). In contrast, LR (Fig. 5d) was substantially weaker with a MA- 
AUC of 0.9027, showing particular difficulty with Class III (AUC =
0.8257) and Class IV (AUC = 0.8762). Additional ROC analyses for the 
remaining models are available in Fig. S5.
This comprehensive comparison underscores the ability of the Auto- 
sklearn framework to automatically construct a model that achieves 
superior water quality classification performance.
3.2.2. Generalization assessment with temporal and spatial validation

To more rigorously assess model robustness and address potential 
data leakage from simple random splits, two additional validation 
schemes were implemented: a temporal hold-out (training on data 
before Jan 1, 2024, and testing on data after) and a spatial hold-out 
(leave-one-basin-out cross-validation). The results are summarized in 
Table 2.
The temporal validation results demonstrate the model’s robust 
classification performance, which remains stable across different time 
periods. The performance of models remained remarkably stable, with 
no statistically significant drop in the Weighted F1-score when pre­
dicting on future, unseen data. This indicates that the models effectively 
learned underlying patterns that are consistent over time.
The spatial validation provided critical insights into the geographic 
generalizability of models. While the average performance across the 
basin-level cross-validation remained high, the standard deviation 
increased significantly—for Auto-sklearn, from 0.0027 in the random 
split to 0.0154. This high variance is strong quantitative evidence of 
underlying regional heterogeneity, indicating that the model’s perfor­
mance, while strong overall, varies across basins with distinct charac­
teristics (Table S8). Crucially, despite this performance variability, the 
relative ranking of the models remained consistent. This stability in 
relative performance confirms that the Auto-sklearn framework is a 
robust and reliable choice for nationwide water quality classification.
