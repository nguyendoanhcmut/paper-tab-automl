For intermediate classes (III and IV), CODMn exhibited the strongest 
influence, with notably negative SHAP values for high indicator con­
centrations (Figs. 6c, d). The impact of TP and DO are showed varying 
patterns between these classes, suggesting their role in fine-tuning 
classification decisions. Class V and WV predictions (Figs. 6e, f) were 
primarily driven by CODMn, DO, and TP, with their extreme values being 
particularly indicative of degraded water quality. Across all classes, TP, 
CODMn, and DO consistently demonstrate high importance, while in­
dicators such as WT, NTU, and EC showed relatively minor contributions 
to the classification decisions. These SHAP importance rankings align 
logically with the single-factor classification standard (Table S1). The 
SHAP analysis effectively quantifies which indicators most frequently 
serve as the “limiting factor” in the official classification rule. For 
instance, the high importance of CODMn for predicting poorer water 
quality classes (e.g., Class V, WV) is expected. This finding aligns 
perfectly with the national standard (Table S1), in which high CODMn 
values are a key condition that defines these poor water quality 
classifications.
A comprehensive importance ranking of the water quality indicators 
was established (Fig. 7a). This was achieved by first calculating the 
mean absolute SHAP values for each indicator within each of the five 
independent runs. These values were then normalized within each run to 
sum to one. The final importance scores shown are the average of these 
five normalized values. A one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post-hoc test 
was performed on these final scores to assess statistical significance (p <
0.05). As denoted by the letters on the chart, the analysis identified 
CODMn and TP as the most influential indicators; their importances were 
not statistically different from each other but were significantly greater 
than all other indicators. DO ranked third, representing the next distinct 
level of statistical importance. A detailed visualization of the cumulative 
importance for each indicator, broken down by class, is provided in the 
supplementary materials (Fig. S6).
To screen for the key water quality indicators, we progressively 
incorporated them into the Auto-sklearn model according to the 
importance ranking. As shown in Fig. 7b, model performance (Weighted 
F1) improved rapidly, reaching 0.9205 ± 0.0097 with the top three 
indicators (CODMn, TP, and DO). Crucially, the addition of the fourth 
indicator (NH3-N) did not yield a statistically significant improvement in
