4.2. Rationality and practicality of key water quality indicators screening

4.2.1. A cost-effective nationwide indicator set

Water quality classification assessment traditionally relies on a 
comprehensive set of indicators, with national standards such as 
GB3838-2002 requiring monitoring of 24 fundamental indicators 
(MEEPRC, 2002). While this extensive monitoring approach ensures 
accuracy, it poses significant challenges in terms of cost and time effi­
ciency (Sun et al., 2021). Our SHAP-based analysis identified CODMn, 
TP, and DO as the most influential indicators for water quality classifi­
cation in China (Fig. 7a), offering a potential solution to this practical 
challenge.
The statistical effectiveness of this three-indicator set is robust. Our 
SHAP-based sequential analysis showed that these indicators alone 
could achieve a high Weighted F1 of 0.9205 ± 0.0097, capturing the 
majority of information necessary for accurate classification. To validate 
this further, we compared the Auto-sklearn trained on these three in­
dicators against a programmatic baseline applying the standard single- 
factor rule to the same limited set. As detailed in Table 3, the Auto- 
sklearn model achieved a statistically significantly higher Weighted 
F1-score (0.9205 ± 0.0097) compared to the rule-based baseline 
(0.8976 ± 0.0038). More importantly, the Auto-sklearn model demon­
strated superior performance across all classes, most critically for Class 
WV, where it achieved an F1-score of 0.8285—a substantial 18.9 % 
relative improvement over the baseline’s 0.6968. This superiority stems 
from the Auto-sklearn’s ability to capture complex, non-linear in­
teractions that the rigid rule inherently misses, confirming that the 
combination of our key indicators with an intelligent model provides a 
more accurate and practically valuable assessment, especially for man­
aging the most polluted water bodies.
The identification of these key indicators provides a practical solu­
tion to the prohibitive costs associated with comprehensive water 
quality monitoring. By focusing on just three indicators (CODMn, TP, and 
DO), monitoring programs can achieve reliable assessment while 
reducing both capital investment for equipment and operational ex­
penses for chemical analysis. This simplified approach is particularly 
valuable for developing regions with resource constraints, making sys­
tematic water quality monitoring more economically feasible and 
widely implementable.
4.2.2. Regional heterogeneity

While our reduced indicator set (CODMn, TP, and DO) performs well 
for general classification at the national scale, our regional analysis 
critically demonstrates that optimal indicator combinations vary in 
response to distinct, regional-scale anthropogenic pressures. As shown 
in Fig. 8 and Table S9, the Song-Liao and Yellow River Basins required a 
different key indicator set where NH3-N replaced DO. This shift is a 
direct reflection of the region-specific pollution drivers.
The exceptional severity of nitrogen pollution in these basins is 
quantitatively supported. The Song-Liao River Basin, for instance, has a 
Net Anthropogenic Nitrogen Input estimated at a staggering 9453 kg N 
km–2 yr–1, a value noted to be higher than that of southern basins (Cao 
et al., 2025). Similarly, the Yellow River Basin is the most severe region 
for agricultural non-point source pollution in China, with its provinces 
contributing a disproportionately high 42.67 % of the entire nation’s 
agricultural TN emissions (Tan et al., 2025). This extreme loading means 
that raw pollutants, rich in NH3-N, are the dominant feature of the 
pollution profile.
This immense pollution pressure is critically amplified by the re­
gion’s distinct hydrological conditions, which starkly contrast with 
other major basins. The northern basins are characterized by extremely 
low water availability. The annual average runoff of the Liaohe (36.7 
km3) and Yellow (52.1 km3) rivers is an order of magnitude lower than 
that of the southern Yangtze and Pearl rivers, which both exceed 300 
km3 (Gao et al., 2020). The Yellow River exemplifies this stress, irri­
gating 13 % of the nation’s cropland with only 3 % of its total river 
runoff (Ren et al., 2016). This “high load, low flow” dynamic means that 
the immense nitrogen inputs are concentrated in a minimal volume of 
water, leading to exceptionally high pollutant concentrations that are 
not seen in high-flow southern basins.
This comparative analysis confirms that the nitrogen pollution in the 
Song-Liao and Yellow River Basins is uniquely prominent. While 
southern basins like the Yangtze may have large total nitrogen loads, 
their vast dilution capacity mitigates the concentration and, thus, the 
immediate severity (Wang et al., 2020). In contrast, the combination of 
extreme nitrogen input intensity and severely limited dilution capacity 
in the Song-Liao and Yellow River Basins creates an environment where 
primary pollutants like NH3-N are a more direct and sensitive signal of 
degradation than secondary ecosystem responses like DO. This evidence 
validates the findings of our model and underscores the necessity of 
basin-specific indicator sets for effective water quality management.

system required by Chinese national standards (Table S1). Many 
important indicators specified in the standards, such as heavy metals, 
volatile phenols, and petroleum, were not included in our analysis.
