Building upon the identification of CODMn, TP, and DO as the key 
water quality indicators at the national scale, the SHAP analysis was 
extended to evaluate regional variations. To ensure the robustness of 
these regional findings, the analysis for each basin was performed using 
five independent model runs with different random seeds. The final 
importance scores, representing the mean of the five normalized SHAP 
values, are visualized in Fig. 8. The detailed results, including the mean 
and SD for each indicator in every basin, are available in the supple­
mentary material (Table S9).
The results revealed a high degree of consistency across most of 
China. Specifically, seven of the nine major river basins—the 
Continental, Hai, Huai, Pearl, Southeast, Southwest, and Yangtze River 
Basins—retained CODMn, TP, and DO as their three most critical in­
dicators, mirroring the national-level analysis. This widespread agree­
ment across diverse geographical regions further validates the 
fundamental significance of these three indicators in water quality 
assessment.
However, notable regional variations emerged in the two northern 
basins. In both the Song-Liao and the Yellow River Basins, NH3-N 
replaced DO as one of the top three key indicators (Table S9). These 
findings highlight that while CODMn, TP, and DO form an effective na­
tional minimum indicator set, regional hydro-environmental charac­
teristics may necessitate tailored monitoring approaches.
