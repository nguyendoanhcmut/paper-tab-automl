A B S T R A C T
Membrane bioreactor (MBR) systems for on-site greywater treatment and reuse have been widely 
investigated, where robust nitrification monitoring is critical to prevent operational disruptions 
and maintain effluent standards. This research proposed an interpretable machine learning-based 
framework to predict nitrification efficacy (classified as "sufficient" or "insufficient") in MBR 
systems, involving model-based and post hoc interpretability. Three interpretable machine 
learning algorithms—logistic regression (LR), random forest (RF), and extreme gradient boosting 
(XGB)—were systematically evaluated, accounting for model bias-variance trade-offs during bi­
nary classification. Six input features were selected based on direct measurability, compatibility 
with commercial sensors, and operational relevance. Model optimization criteria focused on 
maximizing precision to prevent erroneous controls for insufficient nitrification, achieved by low 
false positive rate (FPR: misclassified insufficient nitrification) and high true positive rate (TPR: 
correctly identified sufficient nitrification). Results showed that both LR and XGB achieved 
identical precision scores > 0.85 under standard conditions. Cross-scenario validation demon­
strated RF’s superior generalizability (precision=0.87) when predicting nitrification in biocarrier- 
amended MBRs using training data from non-biocarrier systems, reflecting the potential trans­
ferability of the model. The post hoc interpretability and stability analysis further identified 
prediction sensitivity to data limitations and distributional biases, providing critical insights for 
improving and even properly deploying models in practice.

consistent production of high-quality effluent suitable for reuse (Gao et al., 2025). MBR systems synergistically combine biological 
processes—including heterotrophic oxidation and autotrophic nitrification—with membrane filtration for effective solids separation

Nitrification—converting ammonium (NH4
+-N) into nitrite (NO2
- -N) and then nitrate (NO3
- -N)—is critical in MBRs to meet water 
reuse standards. Centralized plants typically use the proportional integral differential (PID) control strategy to maintain fixed dissolved 
oxygen (DO) setpoints, yet this strategy adapts poorly to influent fluctuations and incurs high energy costs (Gu et al., 2023). On-site 
greywater systems face amplified load variability: daily/seasonal usage patterns cause sustained low loads punctuated by sharp peaks, 
while highly variable water quality challenges empirical control parameters (DelaPaz-Ruíz et al., 2024; Hassan et al., 2024). Although 
PID adjusts aeration valves, its static DO targets lack real-time responsiveness to dynamic NH₄⁺-N loads (e.g., dynamic adjustment 
based on the NH₄⁺-N load) (Li et al., 2022; Shi et al., 2024). Furthermore, PID relies on submerged DO probes, which are prone to 
fouling and drift in small-scale MBRs due to continuous aeration and sludge accumulation. This compromises measurement reliability, 
potentially masking sensor failure as load variation.
Reliable nitrification monitoring remains challenging for decentralized MBRs, where inconsistent efficiency risks process stability 
despite compliant effluent (observed in the current trial). Notably, real-time monitoring of nitrification processes within 
MBRs—particularly under decentralized operational scenarios—remains unaddressed in existing studies. Commercial NH4
+-N/NO2
- -N/ 
NO3
- -N sensors are often impractical for installing in the reactor: high sludge concentrations cause rapid fouling and maintenance 
burdens, increasing operational costs (Huang et al., 2024). Organic matter, suspended solids, and ionic interference further distort 
readings, while sensor response lags fail to capture rapid nitrogen dynamics—hindering real-time control.
Machine learning (ML) offers promising data-driven solutions for monitoring MBR nitrification, using effluent water quality pa­
rameters to assess performance without intrusive sensors, avoiding sensor installation in the reactors with challenges mentioned above 
(Van de Walle et al., 2023). These methods enable rapid, low-cost, and real-time monitoring—facilitating prompt process interventions 
and enhancing safety (Bahramian et al., 2023). Compared to mechanistic models, ML requires simpler inputs and incurs minimal 
maintenance, ideal for on-site systems (Duarte et al., 2024). Given greywater treatment’s critical need for regulatory and public trust, 
prioritizing interpretable ML methods provides transparent prediction rationales for operator action and model refinement (Allen 
et al., 2024). Additionally, utilizing interpretable ML methods allows us to further understand the limitations of current training

Previous studies on data-driven prediction in MBR-based wastewater treatment have primarily focused on effluent quality, targeted 
pollutant removal efficiency, and membrane fouling (Muniz de Queiroz et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2022; Zhong et al., 2022). However, 
relying solely on effluent monitoring provides limited process controllability and fails to enable timely interventions during abnormal 
operational events. This approach is also labor-intensive, time-consuming, and inadequate for tracing underlying process flaws before 
effluent quality violations occur (Moretti et al., 2024). In contrast, real-time process monitoring enables proactive control, enhances 
system resilience, and supports early fault detection—critical for ensuring consistent water quality across diverse reuse applications.
This work aims to develop an interpretable ML model to monitor the nitrification process in MBRs via effluent parameters, reducing 
the difficulty of input feature acquisition in practice. Features were selected based on direct measurability, sensor compatibility, and 
operational relevance. To mitigate overfitting and balance bias-variance tradeoffs, three interpretable algorithms—logistic regression 
(LR), random forest (RF), and extreme gradient boosting (XGB)—were evaluated, simultaneously considering the bias and variance of 
each algorithm. Model training prioritized operational stability by maximizing precision, depending on the higher true positive rate 
(TPR) and lower false positive rate (FPR), trained on data from a suspended-sludge MBR. Furthermore, SHapley Additive exPlanations 
(SHAP) analysis, feature importance, and pairwise assessments elucidated factor-prediction linkages and informed model refinement. 
The models were further validated on a hybrid system, integrating suspended sludge and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) biocarriers, 
performing simultaneously nitrification-denitrification, demonstrating cross-scenario prediction applicability. Finally, an automated 
aeration control strategy is proposed to operationalize the monitoring framework.
